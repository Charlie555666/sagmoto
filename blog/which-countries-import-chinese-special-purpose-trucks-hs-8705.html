Which Countries Import Chinese Special-Purpose Trucks? HS 8705 Export Data

2026-11-20GEO Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Customs-data estimates put China's HS 8705 special-purpose vehicle exports in a range of roughly 30,000 to 45,000 units annually, led by Africa and Asia with an estimated combined 55 to 65 percent share. Within HS 8705, concrete mixer trucks (8705.40) and crane lorries (8705.10) together account for an estimated 60 to 70 percent of volume. The largest individual destinations are typically Indonesia, Vietnam, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Algeria, though rankings shift year to year and buyers should confirm against current customs data.

This page presents HS 8705 export figures as customs-data estimates and trade-data ranges, not as official statistics from a single named agency. It is a neutral reference for buyers benchmarking where Chinese special-purpose trucks go and which vehicle types dominate. Every number below should be treated as an estimate to be confirmed with current customs data before any commercial decision, because classification practices and reporting lags change the totals between sources.

What does HS 8705 actually cover?

HS 8705 covers special purpose motor vehicles, meaning vehicles built for a function other than the ordinary transport of people or goods. The heading is subdivided by the body or equipment fitted. Crane lorries, classified as 8705.10, carry a mounted crane and dominate much of the construction trade. Mobile drilling derricks fall under 8705.20. Fire fighting vehicles, including water tankers and aerial ladder trucks, are 8705.30. Concrete mixer trucks sit in 8705.40, and all other special purpose vehicles, including concrete pumps, sweepers and mobile workshops, are grouped in 8705.90.

Understanding the subheading matters because a buyer comparing countries must compare the same code. A destination that ranks high on mixers may barely appear for fire trucks, and a headline HS 8705 total can hide very different product mixes. Pull the six-digit line that matches the vehicle you intend to buy.

How are exports split by region?

Regional shares are more stable than country rankings because they smooth out single-year project spikes. Asia and Africa together absorb the majority of Chinese special-purpose truck exports, with the Middle East and Latin America forming a second tier. Europe and North America are small destinations for HS 8705 as a whole, partly because of type-approval and emission requirements rather than price.

What does the three-year trend show?

The three-year direction has been upward, with a strong acceleration in the most recent complete year. Growth is driven less by any single country than by the broad shift of Chinese truck makers toward overseas markets. Unit growth and value growth are not identical: average unit values have been under pressure as competition intensifies, so value rises more slowly than volume in some subheadings.

How do re-exports distort the destination ranking?

A customs import figure records where goods crossed a border, not where they were finally used. The United Arab Emirates, for example, appears in the ranking partly because it serves as a regional distribution hub: trucks landed there are transhipped to East Africa, the Gulf and Central Asia. A high number in a hub economy therefore overstates local fleet demand and understates the true reach of Chinese exporters. The same pattern appears in smaller free-zone markets.

To read past the distortion, compare import figures with re-export data for the same code and look for bills of lading showing a final destination. Where that is unavailable, treat a hub's volume as a ceiling for local demand rather than a measure of it. This distinction matters commercially: a distributor in a hub may be the right route to a dozen smaller markets, while a distributor in a consuming country competes only for local tenders. The destination table above should be read with that caveat in mind.

How should buyers read these numbers?

Three cautions apply. First, a high import figure may reflect re-exports through a hub rather than local consumption, so check whether the destination really uses the trucks. Second, HS 8705 groups several very different vehicles, and a buyer of fire trucks should not infer demand from a mixer-led total. Third, local type approval, emission standards and after-sales coverage can matter more to a purchase decision than the size of the market. A country with half the import volume but a mature parts network may be the better place to build a fleet.

What should you verify before acting on export data?

Start with the most recent full-year customs data for your own six-digit HS code, then cross-check with a second trade database if available. Ask suppliers for bills of lading to your region rather than relying on global totals. Confirm the destination's current vehicle-import rules, including age limits, emission standards and local content requirements, because these can change faster than trade statistics. Data tells you where trucks have gone; current regulation tells you whether they can still go.

Used carefully, HS 8705 data is a useful compass. It shows that Asia and Africa dominate, that mixers and crane lorries lead the product mix, and that the trend is upward. It does not replace current customs figures or local market knowledge, and it should never be cited as an official single-source statistic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What vehicles are included in HS 8705?

HS 8705 covers special purpose motor vehicles: crane lorries under 8705.10, mobile drilling derricks under 8705.20, fire fighting vehicles under 8705.30, concrete mixers under 8705.40 and other special purpose vehicles under 8705.90. Mixers and cranes together make up an estimated 60 to 70 percent of unit volume.

Which countries import the most Chinese HS 8705 trucks?

Customs-data estimates consistently place Indonesia, Vietnam, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Algeria near the top, with annual volumes roughly in the 1,500 to 6,000 unit range each. The order shifts with construction and infrastructure cycles, so buyers should confirm against current customs data before relying on the ranking.

What share does Africa take of Chinese special-purpose truck exports?

Africa is estimated to account for roughly 25 to 32 percent of HS 8705 exports, mainly crane lorries and concrete mixers driven by infrastructure and municipal fleet demand. Together with Asia's estimated 30 to 38 percent, the two regions absorb the majority of Chinese special-purpose truck volume.

Have Chinese HS 8705 exports been growing?

Yes. Customs-data estimates show unit volumes rising from roughly 28,000 to 34,000 in year one to 38,000 to 45,000 in year three, an approximate 12 to 22 percent annual increase. Export value has grown more slowly than units because average unit prices have faced competitive pressure.

How reliable is export data for market decisions?

It is indicative, not definitive. Re-exports through hubs, kit classification and reporting lags all distort totals, and buyers should confirm with current customs data and the specific six-digit code. Type approval, emission standards and local parts support often matter more than headline market size.
