How Do You Set Up a Local Service Workshop for a SAGMOTO Fleet?

2026-10-28Buyer Guide12 min read

Quick answer: Set up a SAGMOTO workshop with one 14 by 7 metre bay per 15 trucks, a pit or 5-tonne lift, torque wrenches to 2,000 Nm and a diagnostic scan tool, stock one clutch kit per 15 vehicles, and employ one technician per 10 trucks plus a foreman. A basic two-bay setup costs roughly 60,000 to 120,000 USD depending on region and tool quality.

A local workshop is the difference between a SAGMOTO fleet that stays on the road and one that waits weeks for an overseas dealer visit. When you operate ten, thirty or sixty trucks far from the factory, controlling routine service, brake work and warranty evidence in-house protects uptime and protects your warranty claim record. This guide sets out the physical, tooling, staffing and stocking decisions needed to stand up a competent workshop.

Plan bays by trucks, not by hope: One service bay measuring 14 m long by 7 m wide by 5 m clear height handles about 15 trucks of planned service per shift. Below that ratio, vehicles queue and downtime climbs. Two bays serve a 30-truck fleet; four bays suit 60 trucks with a night shift.

How Many Technicians Do You Need for 20 Trucks?

For a 20-truck fleet on single-shift operation, plan two qualified heavy-truck technicians plus one apprentice and a workshop foreman who also handles warranty administration. The rule of one technician per 10 trucks covers routine servicing, brake and clutch work, and minor electrical faults; the foreman keeps records, orders parts and photographs warranty evidence so nothing is lost.

If the fleet runs two shifts or covers harsh mining-access duty, add a third technician because service intervals shorten and breakdowns cluster. Understaffing is the most common cause of a workshop that looks busy but never catches up with the queue.

Capex splits into building, equipment and initial stock. A leased industrial unit needs floor strengthening for the pit or lift, while a purpose-built shed adds concrete and drainage. Should You Buy Genuine or Aftermarket Parts for Stock?

Hold genuine SAGMOTO parts for anything that affects warranty, safety or engine integrity: clutch kits, bearings, filters, seals, brake chambers and electronic sensors. Genuine parts carry the chassis-matched part number and keep warranty valid. Consumables such as bulbs, wiper blades, grease and generic fasteners can be sourced locally to save cost without risking a claim.

A mixed strategy protects cash flow: genuine for the critical 20 percent of line items that cause 80 percent of downtime, aftermarket for the trivial consumables. Always record the genuine part number on the job card so a future warranty audit is clean.

What Bay Dimensions and Pit or Lift Requirements Apply?

Each bay needs 14 m length to admit a 6x4 tractor and trailer combination or a dumped body, 7 m width for safe walk-around, and 5 m clear height for overhead crane or hoist. A pit 1.2 m deep and 0.9 m wide along the bay centre is cheaper than a lift and ideal for chassis and brake work, but a 5-tonne two-post or four-post lift is safer for transmission removal.

We recommend at least one lift plus one pit in a two-bay workshop: the lift for driveline jobs, the pit for inspections and brake servicing. Drainage and an oil-water separator in the floor are mandatory for legal waste handling in most markets.

What Tool List Does a SAGMOTO Workshop Need?

The core kit covers mechanical, hydraulic and diagnostic work. Torque wrenches must reach 2,000 Nm for hub and propeller-shaft nuts; a hydraulic press of at least 50 tonnes handles bearing extraction; an injector tester services the Weichai and Cummins units; and a diagnostic scan tool must cover the SAGMOTO CAN, J1939 and OBD protocols used across the X, E and i ranges.

How Do You Plan Initial Spare Parts Stocking by Fleet Size?

Stock scales with fleet size but not linearly, because shared failures let you hold fewer spares per truck as the fleet grows. For 10 trucks hold one clutch kit, two release bearings and a filter set for each model. For 30 trucks hold two kits and four bearings. For 60 trucks hold four kits, eight bearings and a standby turbo and alternator.

What Staff Roles and Headcount Should You Plan?

Beyond technicians, a functioning workshop needs a foreman or supervisor, a parts clerk and a washer or apprentice. The foreman owns service scheduling, warranty evidence and technician productivity; the parts clerk controls stock accuracy and urgent orders; the apprentice handles cleaning, tyre and basic service. For 60 trucks add a second foreman and a night-shift lead so the workshop can run continuously.

Service interval planning: Plan by operating hours, not just kilometres, for trucks on stockyard or construction duty where engine hours outrun distance. A 440 hp X6 on short cycles may need an oil and filter service every 400 hours rather than every 25,000 km, so track both metrics from day one.

How Do You Keep Records and Warranty Claim Evidence?

Every job card must capture the chassis number, fault code read from the scan tool, the genuine part number fitted, and dated photographs of the failed component and the fault screen. SAGMOTO warranty claims require this evidence trail, and a missing photo is the usual reason a claim is rejected. Store records in a simple digital system so a claim can be assembled in minutes.

Photograph the VIN plate, the old part number and the new part number side by side, and export the scan-tool fault report as a PDF. This discipline turns a disputed failure into a paid claim.

What Training and Safety Rules Apply?

Local technicians need familiarisation on SAGMOTO specifics: the Fast Gear and ZF shift patterns, the Weichai and Cummins service points, and the i9 high-voltage isolation procedure for the electric range. The factory offers a technician training course; budget for at least two staff to attend before the fleet arrives.

Safety rules cover lock-out-tag-out on air and electrical systems, high-voltage isolation for i9 work behind a barrier, and bunded storage for oils, coolant and batteries. Waste oil, coolant and batteries must go to licensed handlers, with a logbook proving disposal to satisfy environmental inspection.

Review the full SAGMOTO aftersales service and parts support programme before committing capex, and confirm training dates with the regional office. A workshop built to the bay, tool, stock and staffing plan above keeps a 10 to 60 truck SAGMOTO fleet serviced, warranted and earning.

How Do You Schedule the First 90 Days?

The first quarter sets the workshop habit. Weeks one to four focus on commissioning, tool calibration and technician training; weeks five to eight introduce the planned service log and the first scheduled oil and filter services; weeks nine to twelve review downtime data and adjust stock levels against actual failures. A 90-day review with the SAGMOTO regional office confirms that the bay, tool and staff plan matches real demand before any expansion is approved. This staged start avoids the common error of over-buying stock that sits idle while the team is still learning the model-specific fault patterns.
