SAGMOTO X5 vs FAW J7P: Heavy-Duty Mining Truck Comparison 2026

2026-09-11SAGMOTO BlogShaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd.

Introduction to the Comparison

The SAGMOTO X5 and FAW J7P are two of the most prominent Chinese heavy-duty trucks in the mining and construction haulage segment. Both trucks target the 6x4, 430 HP class for 49-tonne GVW operations in mining, quarrying, and heavy construction. This comparison provides fleet operators with a detailed analysis of powertrain, chassis, durability, maintenance, and pricing to inform procurement decisions. For the full SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 range, visit our product page.

While both trucks originate from major Chinese commercial vehicle manufacturers (SAGMOTO/Shaanxi Auto vs FAW/China First Automotive Works), their engineering approaches differ. The X5 uses Weichai WP10/WP12 engines with FAST transmissions, prioritizing component standardization and global parts availability. The J7P uses FAW's own CA6DM engine with CA-branded transmissions, prioritizing integrated drivetrain development and domestic parts cost advantages.

The X5's WP12.430E50 produces 430 HP and 2,100 Nm, providing a 10 HP and 300 Nm advantage over the J7P's CA6DM2-42E5 (420 HP, 1,800 Nm). This advantage is meaningful in mining operations where trucks must climb steep ramps with full loads. The X5's torque band (1,000-1,400 rpm) is also wider, providing more flexibility in gear selection on variable grades.

Gradeability and Pulling Power

With the FAST 12-speed transmission's first gear ratio of 12.10:1 and rear axle ratio of 4.11:1, the X5 achieves a starting gradeability of 30 percent with a 49-tonne combination. The J7P's CA transmission has a first gear ratio of 11.82:1 with the same rear axle ratio, achieving 27 percent gradeability. The 3 percentage point difference means the X5 can start on a 16.7 degree grade, while the J7P requires a 15.1 degree maximum grade for unassisted starting.

The X5's double-layer frame provides significantly greater torsional rigidity than the J7P's single-layer frame. In mining operations where trucks operate on uneven haul roads with high cross-slope angles, frame torsional rigidity directly affects frame fatigue life. Fleet data from mining operators in Zambia and Indonesia shows the X5 frame develops fewer fatigue cracks at 300,000-400,000 km compared to the J7P, attributed to the double-layer construction.

Fuel Economy Comparison

In controlled mining haul cycle testing (10 km loaded, 10 km empty, 20 percent grade sections, 40 km/h average speed), the SAGMOTO X5 recorded 52-58 litres per 100 km, while the FAW J7P recorded 55-62 litres per 100 km. The X5's 3-4 L/100km advantage is attributed to the WP12's higher thermal efficiency (43 percent vs 41 percent) and the FAST transmission's closer gear ratios enabling lower engine rpm in the operating range.

At an annual 80,000 km of mining haul operations, the 3.5 L/100km fuel advantage saves approximately 2,800 litres of diesel per truck annually. At a diesel price of USD 1.20 per litre, this represents annual savings of USD 3,360 per truck, or USD 16,800 for a 5-truck fleet.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO X5 and FAW J7P are both capable heavy-duty trucks for mining and construction operations. The X5's advantages in engine power and torque (430 HP/2,100 Nm vs 420 HP/1,800 Nm), fuel economy (3-4 L/100km better), frame durability (double-layer vs single-layer), and component life deliver a lower 5-year TCO despite higher initial purchase price. The J7P's advantages in lower purchase price and lower per-item maintenance cost appeal to budget-constrained operators or those with established FAW dealer relationships. For fleet operators prioritizing total cost of ownership, fuel efficiency, and long-term durability in demanding mining operations, the SAGMOTO X5 delivers the stronger value proposition.
