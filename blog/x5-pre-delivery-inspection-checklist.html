SAGMOTO X5 Pre-Delivery Inspection: A 40-Point Buyer Checklist

2026-11-19Buying & Import Guide9 min read

A pre-delivery inspection is the last moment at which a defect can be fixed without an argument about who pays. Once the truck is on a vessel and the bill of lading is issued, a missing tool, a leaking seal or a mismatched engine number becomes a claim rather than a punch-list item. A written forty-point checklist turns that moment from a handshake into a record that both the supplier and the buyer can sign. This article sets out the checks that matter on a SAGMOTO X5, an 18 tonne class medium-duty truck with a Cummins or Yuchai engine in the 180 to 240 hp band and a Fast eight-speed gearbox in 4x2 and 6x4 forms.

The X5 is often bought as a cab and chassis for a local bodybuilder, which changes what should be inspected. Some items belong to the factory and some belong to the body installer, and the buyer needs to know where one responsibility ends and the other begins. The checklist below assumes a complete truck with a cargo body fitted at the factory; if your order is a chassis only, the body-related points should be transferred to a second inspection after the local body is mounted. Either way, carry out the check in daylight, with the engine cold for the fluid readings and with a test route available.

Key point: Photograph the VIN plate, engine serial and odometer before anything else, and attach those images to the signed report. A PDI without a verified identity is worthless if a dispute later arises about which truck was actually supplied.

How to run the inspection

Work in a fixed order so that nothing is visited twice and no item is skipped. Start with documents and identity while the truck is stationary, move to the exterior walk-around, then open the bonnet for the engine bay, inspect the chassis and driveline from below, check the air and brake system, test the cab electrics, examine the wheels and tyres, and finish with a road test. Give each point one of three marks: pass, fail or not applicable, and write the defect in plain language rather than a code that only the factory understands. A check that is not recorded is a check that did not happen.

Bring a small kit: a torch, a gauge for tyre pressure, a tread depth gauge, a socket set for wheel nuts, a mirror for viewing the underside and a printout of the order specification. The single most useful document is the signed order sheet, because the PDI is a comparison between what was agreed and what arrived. Engine rating, gearbox model, axle configuration, tyre size and body type should all be checked against that sheet rather than against a catalogue.

The detailed checklist

A. Documents and identity. (1) Chassis VIN matches the commercial invoice. (2) Engine serial matches the certificate of origin. (3) Gearbox serial is recorded. (4) Certificate of origin is present and stamped. (5) Commercial invoice and packing list agree with the order. (6) Shipping marks and consignee details are correct.

B. Exterior and body. (7) Cab paint is free of transit scratches and dents. (8) Door gaps are even and doors shut without force. (9) Windscreen and mirrors are intact and correctly fitted. (10) Body or dropside welds are continuous and free of spatter. (11) Mudguards and bracketry are secure. (12) All lamp lenses are present, clean and uncracked.

C. Engine bay. (13) Engine oil is at the correct mark and the dipstick reads clean. (14) Coolant level is correct and the cap seals properly. (15) Drive belts are tensioned and free of glazing. (16) Hoses and clamps are seated with no weeping at the joints. (17) Air filter housing is sealed and the indicator is reset. (18) Battery terminals are tight and the hold-down is fitted. (19) Fuel water separator is drained and the hand pump works.

D. Driveline and chassis. (20) Gearbox oil level is correct for the specified grade. (21) Axle oil levels are correct in both axles on a 6x4. (22) Prop shaft flange bolts are present and torqued. (23) Universal joints have grease and no play. (24) Steering linkage has no looseness at the ball joints. (25) Leaf springs or air bags are seated and shackles are tight.

E. Brakes and air system. (26) Compressor charges without unusual noise. (27) Air dryer is fitted and drains correctly. (28) Brake chambers are not dented and rods are adjusted. (29) Slack adjusters are within free-travel specification. (30) ABS sensors are connected and the warning lamp cycles normally. (31) Air pressure builds to the working range and holds when parked.

F. Electrical and cab. (32) Engine starts cleanly from cold without excessive cranking. (33) Alternator charges at the correct voltage. (34) Gauges and warning lamps respond and then extinguish. (35) Heating and air conditioning work at both settings. (36) Wipers, washers, seatbelts and door locks operate normally.

G. Wheels and tyres. (37) Tyre brand, size and load rating match the order. (38) Tread depth is even across each axle and above the minimum. (39) Wheel nuts are torqued to the specified value in a star pattern.

H. Road test. (40) Drive at least ten kilometres with a ballasted or loaded body, checking steering tracking, gearbox engagement, brake balance and any abnormal noise from the hubs or driveline. Record the odometer reading at the end.

Acceptance thresholds that are worth measuring

Some checks are subjective but a few can be measured, and measured values make a report much harder to dispute. The table gives thresholds that are reasonable for a new X5; confirm them against the supplier's own tolerance sheet, since a figure that contradicts the factory standard will be rejected even if it is sensible. Record the actual value beside each threshold rather than simply ticking a box.

Key point: Measure the air system before the road test, not after. A truck that loses pressure while parked cannot be safely braked on a test route, and a failed pressure-hold check is a reason to hold shipment rather than a note to be fixed at the destination.

Factory acceptance versus destination inspection

Where the buyer can attend the factory, the PDI doubles as a factory acceptance test and any defect is resolved before the truck is loaded. Where attendance is not possible, commission a third-party inspector with a written scope based on this checklist and ask for the report and photographs before the balance of payment is released. The cost of an independent inspection is small relative to the value of the truck, and the report creates a clear record of condition at the point of loading. Specify that the inspector checks the forty points and not merely that the truck exists and starts.

If the X5 arrives with a SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake body from the exporter, add body-mount bolt torque and floor flatness to section B, since the body is part of the delivered product. If the chassis arrives bare for a local bodybuilder, take the section B photographs at the factory and repeat them after the local body is fitted, so any damage in transit can be attributed to the correct stage. Keep the signed checklist with the vehicle file together with the invoice, the bill of lading and the insurance certificate. When the truck is eventually sold, that file is evidence of how it was accepted and maintained from day one.

Conclusion

A forty-point PDI is not a formality; it is the cheapest insurance a fleet buyer can purchase. Verify identity first, walk the truck in a fixed order, measure the values that can be measured, and finish with a loaded road test before the unit leaves the factory or the yard. Record each result with a photograph where it matters and refuse to sign a report that contains failures hidden in the small print. A clean X5 delivered on schedule with a complete document file is the outcome this checklist is designed to produce, and it starts with the discipline to check all forty points rather than the twenty that are convenient.

Item forty is the build sheet itself: confirm wheelbase, body mountings and axle rating match what was ordered, since a clean report on a mis-specified chassis is still a rejected truck. Verify those dimensions against the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake range before travelling to the yard.
