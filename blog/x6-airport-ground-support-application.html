X6 Medium Duty Truck for Airport Ground Support Applications

2026-11-06Industry Application8 min read

Airports from regional hubs in North Africa to greenfield terminals in Central Asia need a dependable medium duty chassis that can work the apron, the taxiway and the landside service road on the same shift. The SAGMOTO X6 fills that role with its 210 to 245 hp Cummins ISD engine, a 12 to 60 tonne gross combination weight envelope, and a chassis geometry that accepts dropside, box and specialist bodies without major rework. Because the X6 is offered in both 4x2 and all-wheel-drive formats, ground handlers spec it for paved apron catering one day and unpaved perimeter water runs the next. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading has supplied X6 chassis to handling agents who refit them locally with catering lifts, tank modules and belt loaders, and the platform's common rail engine meets the staged emission rules most civil aviation authorities now require for airside vehicles.

Why the X6 Suits Airside Duty

The X6 carries a wheelbase range from 3.8 to 5.6 metres, letting body builders mount a 6 metre belt loader or a compact catering box without overhang problems. Its 245 hp top of range gives the torque to climb an aircraft nose ramp loaded, while the 210 hp version is enough for landside baggage shuttles that never board. The cab is a forward-control design with a low bonnet line that improves the driver's view of wingtips and ground crew, a safety factor on a crowded apron. Airside variants use a spark-arrestor exhaust and a chassis earth strap to discharge static before the truck approaches a fuelling point, and the 24 volt electrical system runs the additional beacons and warning lights required by ramp protocol. The X6 also clears the 3.2 metre height gate at most cargo terminals without a low-profile conversion.

Specify the all-wheel-drive X6 only where the perimeter or de-icing pad is unpaved. On a sealed apron the 4x2 saves roughly 400 kilograms of tare weight and returns better fuel burn, and that payload stays available for water, catering trays or baggage instead of being spent on driven front axles you never use.

Catering Truck Builds

The most common X6 airside conversion is the catering truck, where the chassis carries a temperature-controlled box and a scissor or column lift that rises to the aircraft door sill at 2.7 to 5.8 metres. The X6's power take-off drives a hydraulic pump for the lift, the same architecture used on its tipper sibling, so the hydraulic discipline transfers directly. The box is insulated to hold 4 to 8 degrees Celsius for chilled carts and is built with a 12 volt vapour compression unit drawing from the chassis alternator. A typical X6 catering build serves narrow-body aircraft with 60 to 110 meal carts and reaches the sill in under 90 seconds. Body builders favour the 4.5 metre wheelbase because it balances the lifted mass over the rear axle and keeps the stabilisers within the permitted outrigger footprint.

Water and Lavatory Service

Potable water and lavatory servicing are separate circuits on a proper X6 build, never mixed. The clean water tank is food-grade polyethylene of 4,000 to 6,000 litre capacity with a 12 volt diaphragm pump delivering 2.5 bar at the coupling, enough to fill an aircraft tank in under eight minutes. The waste or lavatory module is a sealed stainless steel tank of equal volume with a vacuum or piston pump and a fully enclosed hose bay to meet hygiene rules. The X6 4x4 is the preferred base here because service points sit off the paved loop on soft ground near the de-icing area. Both tanks are baffled to keep the load from shifting during the low-speed apron run, and the chassis earth strap is bonded to the tank to dissipate static at the fill point.

Never run potable and waste water through one pump set even if the body builder offers a shared manifold. Aviation hygiene audits fail a combined system instantly, and a single cross-contamination event pulls the vehicle from the ramp and the handler from the contract. Two independent circuits cost little and protect the operating licence.

Baggage and Cargo Handling

For baggage the X6 is built as a belt loader or a towable cart shuttle. The belt loader version mounts a 0.6 metre wide conveyor at a 20 degree incline reaching the cargo door, powered by the chassis power take-off through a right-angle gearbox at a belt speed of 0.3 metres per second. The cart shuttle is a flatbed with a 2.4 tonne payload and a low deck height of 0.7 metre so handlers load trolleys without ramps. Because the X6 gross combination weight reaches 60 tonnes with a drawbar trailer, a single chassis moves a train of baggage dollsies landside between the terminal and the remote stand. Fleets planning a mixed apron fleet can compare the X6 with the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake body options that share the same wheelbase and power take-off points for simpler local conversion.

Maintenance Lifts and Technical Stands

The X6 also serves as a mobile maintenance platform, where the chassis carries a man-rider cage or a fixed stairs unit to reach aircraft panels, beacon lenses and pitot covers. The lift is a column or scissor type driven by the same hydraulic pump as the catering build, with a 500 kilogram man-riding rating and a platform height up to 6.0 metres. Safety interlocks cut the pump if the stabilisers are not down or the cage gate is open, and the X6's 24 volt system runs the interlock controller. For night or low-visibility work the chassis mounts steerable work lights and a rotating beacon bar, both on the auxiliary circuit so the engine can idle while the body draws power. The forward-control cab gives the technician a clear approach path when positioning under a wing.

Specification and Conversion Notes

Body builders should request the X6 factory conversion pack that pre-drills the frame at the certified holes and supplies the correct length bolts with locking compound, because drilling the rail elsewhere weakens it at the point of highest lift moment and risks a failed certification. The power take-off hydraulic schematic and the static earth drawing let a certified shop complete the airside approval without returning to the factory, and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading ships the spark-arrestor and earth strap as a factory-fit option to shorten that path. A correctly prepped chassis converts in days rather than weeks and returns to revenue sooner, which matters when the handling agent is bidding against incumbents for a multi-year ramp contract.

When ordering an X6 for airside work, specify the spark-arrestor muffler, the static earth strap, the 24 volt twin-battery set and the chassis prep for the body builder's power take-off. The Cummins ISD in the 210 to 245 hp band pairs with a six-speed overdrive gearbox that keeps engine speed low on the long landside run between the fuel farm and the terminal, improving fuel burn per service cycle. Body mounting uses the standard 700 millimetre frame width with through-bolts at the designated rail holes, and the air brake system is dual-circuit with a spring parking brake that holds on a 1 in 8 grade. Handlers building a first airside fleet can review the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake catalogue for the base chassis drawings the conversion houses need before cutting a body.

Fuelling, Fuels and Emissions on the Ramp

Airside vehicles face tightening emission rules, and the X6's Cummins ISD common rail meets the staged limits most authorities now apply to apron traffic, with the option of a diesel oxidation catalyst and a closed crankcase breather to cut the smoke that ordinary engines trail across the stand. The spark-arrestor muffler is mandatory where the truck works near fuelling or de-icing, and the chassis earth strap must be bonded to the tank before any fluid transfer. Fuel for the X6 is drawn from the airside bowser using the same jet-A1 exclusion rule that keeps petrol off the ramp, and the 24 volt system is isolated during fuelling so a stray spark cannot ignite vapour. Handlers should log the X6 under the same permit as the pushback tractors so the civil aviation authority sees one coherent ground fleet rather than a mix of unregistered vehicles.

Deployment and Training

Airside X6 trucks enter service after a ramp familiarisation that covers wingtip clearance, the speed limit of 30 km/h on the manoeuvring area, and the radio call sequence before crossing an active taxiway. Drivers need the airside driving permit issued by the civil aviation authority, and the X6's compact turning circle of 13.5 metres suits the tight stands at older terminals. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading provides the chassis build data and the power take-off hydraulic schematic so local body shops can certify the conversion, and supplies the spark-arrestor and earth strap as factory fit options to shorten the approval path. With a documented service plan the X6 airside fleet supports a regional airport's full ground handling need from a single, familiar chassis type.
