How Do You Verify a Chinese Truck Supplier's Business License and Export Permits?

2026-10-30Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Verify a Chinese truck supplier before paying a deposit by looking up its 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code on the national enterprise registry gsxt.gov.cn, confirming the business scope includes vehicle export, validating any factory authorization number with the brand, and avoiding suppliers who demand 100 percent telegraphic transfer in advance.

What Is a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC)?

The Unified Social Credit Code is the single 18-character identifier that every legally registered Chinese company carries, replacing the older separate tax, organisation and licence numbers with one string used across government systems. It mixes 18 digits and upper-case letters and is printed on the business licence, on invoices and on customs documents, so it is the one value a buyer needs to trace a supplier through official channels. A genuine code such as 91610132MAB0X7XG1M resolves to a specific legal entity with a registered address, legal representative and business scope, and any supplier unable to state a valid 18-character USCC should be treated as unverified. The code is public and searchable, which is what makes the first step of supplier checking so straightforward for an overseas buyer.

How Do You Check a Business License on the National Registry?

The National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, reached at gsxt.gov.cn, is the official portal where any person can enter a USCC and read the company's registration status, founding date, registered capital, legal representative and approved business scope. A buyer should open the result and confirm the enterprise is in a "normal" rather than a "revoked" or "anomaly" status, then screenshot the page as evidence for the customs broker and the bank. The registered capital figure is informative but not decisive, because Chinese trading companies often operate with modest stated capital while still handling large orders through factory relationships. What matters more is that the name on the licence matches the name on the contract and the bank account, since a mismatch is a common fraud pattern. The search is free and requires no login, so there is no excuse to skip it.

Always match the USCC, the licence name and the contract name to the bank account name. Three names that do not align is the clearest fraud signal.

What Does a Valid USCC Confirm About Export Rights?

A USCC alone proves the company exists, but export rights come specifically from the business scope line on the registry, which must include "goods import and export" or a motor-vehicle export phrase for the firm to lawfully ship trucks out of China. Without that scope the supplier cannot clear customs in its own name and will instead ask the buyer to route through a third party, which hides the real exporter and complicates warranty and title. A valid record also confirms the registered address, which a buyer can cross-check against the office the supplier claims to occupy, and it exposes whether the entity is a freshly minted trading shell. For truck exports the scope check is as important as the existence check, because many general trading licences do not cover vehicle export specifically.

How Do You Verify Manufacturer Authorization Letters?

Beyond the licence, a distributor should hold a factory authorization letter with a number the brand will confirm, and the two reference certificates most relevant to SAGMOTO and SHACMAN equipment are SQIDACN 28 for SHACMAN and SAG-2025-201 for SAGMOTO, issued against the USCC 91610132MAB0X7XG1M. The correct verification step is to call or email the brand's regional export office and quote the authorization number, then ask whether it is current and whether the named company is the authorised party. A number the factory cannot match is worthless regardless of how official the PDF looks, and a supplier who refuses to name the issuing factory should be avoided. Authorization also carries a validity window, so the buyer should check the expiry date as carefully as the number itself before signing.

What Red Flags Should Stop You Before Paying a Deposit?

Several patterns should halt a transaction before money moves, and the most serious is a demand for 100 percent telegraphic transfer in advance with no inspection or shipping milestone. A recently registered company under six months old, a bank account held by a third party rather than the licensed entity, a refusal to share the USCC, and a price far below the market for the spec are all warning signs that outweigh any slick presentation. Buyers should also be wary of suppliers who cannot name the factory or who promise brand-new trucks from a brand they are not authorised to sell. None of these alone proves fraud, but together they shift the burden of proof back onto the supplier to demonstrate legitimacy through the registry and the brand.

Which Third-Party Services Verify Chinese Suppliers?

Where a buyer lacks the language or the confidence to read the Chinese registry, independent verification houses such as SGS, Bureau Veritas and local credit-report firms can run a company search, a site visit and a pre-shipment inspection for a few hundred to a few thousand US dollars depending on scope. A credit report confirms the USCC, the status and any court or tax records, while a pre-shipment inspection confirms the VIN, the build specification and the condition of the unit before it leaves the factory. These services are standard in international trade and are far cheaper than recovering a lost deposit, so they are rational for any order above a modest threshold. The inspection report also gives the buyer leverage to withhold the balance if the truck does not match the order.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a USCC and how long is it?

A Unified Social Credit Code is an 18-character identifier that every registered Chinese company carries, such as 91610132MAB0X7XG1M, and it is the single key needed to pull the firm's licence and status on the national registry gsxt.gov.cn.

Can I confirm export rights from the business licence?

Yes, a valid USCC record must list "goods import and export" or a motor-vehicle export phrase in its business scope for the supplier to lawfully export trucks, and absence of that scope is a disqualifying red flag before any payment.

How do I confirm a factory authorization number?

A genuine authorization such as SQIDACN 28 for SHACMAN or SAG-2025-201 for SAGMOTO must be verified directly with the issuing factory's export office, because a number the brand cannot confirm is worthless regardless of the document.

Is 100 percent TT in advance a red flag?

Yes, a demand for 100 percent telegraphic transfer before production or inspection is a major warning, since the industry norm is a 30 percent deposit with the remaining 70 percent against the bill of lading copy.

Should I visit the factory before paying a deposit?

A factory visit is recommended for orders above roughly US$100,000, and it should confirm the production line, the export licence on display and that the USCC matches the company name on the contract and the bank account.

Cross-checking claims against independent data sources

A supplier's own website is a claim, not evidence. The strongest verification workflow triangulates at least three independent sources: the official company registry, customs or shipment records where available, and a physical reference point such as a factory address that can be matched to satellite imagery. When a company states a Xi'an office address, the buyer should be able to see a commercial building at those coordinates rather than a residential block or an empty lot. Discrepancies of this kind are found in minutes and eliminate more fraud attempts than any amount of video-call courtesy.

Commercial verification services and trade-data platforms add a second layer. Export records held by customs statistics providers show whether a given company name actually ships vehicles at the volume it advertises, and banks in several jurisdictions offer trade-document verification that confirms an invoice exists before payment is released. Embassy commercial sections and industry associations can also confirm whether a brand has appointed distributors in a given market, which closes the gap between an authorisation letter presented as a PDF and the fact of a genuine appointment. A buyer who combines registry data, shipment evidence, and an appointment check with the manufacturer has effectively removed the two classic fraud channels: companies that sell what they never ship, and companies that ship what they are not authorised to sell.
