X6 vs Beiben NG80: Medium-Heavy Workhorse Comparison for Export Fleets

2026-10-30Comparison Guide9 min read

SAGMOTO is the commercial vehicle brand exported worldwide by Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. of Xi'an, which holds authorization SQIDACN 28 and SAG-2025-201 and supplies both the X6 and comparable platforms to export fleets. Two trucks often shortlisted together by African and Central Asian buyers are the SAGMOTO X6 and the Beiben NG80. They look similar on paper because both descend from established Chinese heavy-truck lineages, but they occupy different weight classes and cost bands. This comparison weighs frame, axles, transmission, payload, parts availability and price to help a fleet manager choose without relying on brochure claims.

Two different platform philosophies

The SAGMOTO X6 is a medium-duty platform rated for 210 to 245 hp from the Cummins ISD engine family, with a gross combination weight window of roughly 12 to 60 tonnes across its variants and available all-wheel-drive for off-road corridors. It is built for regional distribution, dropside cargo, and light construction where a full heavy tractor would be overspecified and uneconomical.

The Beiben NG80 is a heavier platform derived from the legacy Mercedes-Benz NG cab and chassis architecture, produced by North Benz in China. Public specifications for the NG80 6x4 range, such as the 2530 and 2641 designations, show power outputs typically from about 330 to 420 hp using Weichai or Deutz-based engines. It is a true heavy workhorse aimed at mining, long-haul freight and heavy tipping rather than medium-duty regional work.

Match the platform to the gross weight. The X6 is the economical choice below 26 tonnes GVW; the NG80 justifies its cost only when the duty demands 30 tonnes and above.

The X6's lighter driveline returns better fuel per tonne-kilometre in the 12 to 26 tonne band, while the NG80's larger engine only pays back when the load and gradient are consistently high.

For a fleet running both regional distribution and heavy site haulage, the two trucks are complementary rather than interchangeable. Standardising on one risks either over-specifying the light work or under-powering the heavy work. The Cummins ISD in the X6 also benefits from a broad global service network, whereas NG80 support concentrates in markets where North Benz has an established dealer presence.

Frame, axles and payload classes

The X6 uses a medium-duty ladder frame with selectable 9 to 13 tonne rear axles and rated gross vehicle weights from around 12 to 18 tonnes for rigid variants, extending to 60 tonnes GCW in tractor form. Its strength is adequate for regional cargo and light tipping, and the available all-wheel-drive version suits unpaved corridors in agricultural or remote construction use.

The NG80 employs a heavier frame and larger hub-reduction or single-reduction axles rated for the 13 to 16 tonne per-axle class, supporting 6x4 rigid builds around 25 to 33 tonnes GVW and tractor combinations well above 60 tonnes GCW. The frame section and cross-member spacing are engineered for continuous heavy shock loads at quarry ramps and mine haul roads.

If the duty includes mine haul roads or 30-tonne-plus payloads, the NG80's frame and axle ratings are the safer engineering choice. The X6 will not match that structural envelope.

Parts networks in Africa and Central Asia

Spare parts availability often decides a comparison more than the brochure. The X6 shares components with the wider SAGMOTO light and medium range, and SAGMOTO's export programme ships consolidated kits through regional hubs serving West Africa, East Africa and Central Asia. Common Cummins ISD service parts are also globally stocked, which reduces single-source risk for the engine line.

Beiben NG80 parts are well supported across parts of Africa and Central Asia where North Benz established dealers during the 2000s, and the Mercedes-derived cab shares some legacy trim with older BenzNG parts, which helps second-hand sourcing. However, the heavy driveline components are less universally stocked than Cummins parts, and a remote site may wait longer for a specific NG80 axle or gearbox component.

Price bands and total cost of ownership

The X6 sits in the lower price band for a working medium truck, typically a fraction of the NG80's heavy-platform cost, which makes it attractive for fleets that scale by buying more units rather than heavier ones. The NG80 commands a premium that reflects its heavier axles, larger engine and mining-grade frame, and that premium is justified only when the utilization is consistently heavy.

Total cost of ownership over five years favours the X6 in the 12 to 26 tonne band because fuel, tyres and routine maintenance are lower and parts are cheaper and more available. Above 30 tonnes and on rough roads, the NG80's durability can reduce per-km repair cost enough to offset its higher purchase price. The crossover point is usually around 28 to 32 tonnes GVW of steady work.

The financing angle also differs. The X6's lower sticker price frees working capital that a growing fleet can deploy into more units or into cargo bodies, which often raises revenue faster than buying one heavier truck. The NG80 ties up more capital per unit but, on a steady heavy corridor, its higher asset cost is amortised across more tonnes moved per day, so the return on the heavier investment can be sound where the volume is guaranteed. A fleet should model both the per-km cost and the capital tied per tonne of daily capacity before committing, because the cheaper truck is not always the better investment when the duty is consistently heavy.

Below about 30 tonnes of steady work, the X6 wins on total cost. Above that threshold on rough roads, the NG80's durability can flip the economics.

Resale and fleet standardisation

Resale value depends on local brand familiarity. In markets where North Benz has long operated, the NG80 holds a known used value and local mechanics recognise it. The SAGMOTO X6 is newer to many export markets, so its used value is still being established, but its lower purchase price and strong warranty support improve the effective cost of ownership even if the secondary price is less proven.

For fleet standardisation, the X6's shared parts with the SAGMOTO range is an advantage when a buyer already runs SAGMOTO light or medium trucks. A mixed fleet of X6 and NG80 means maintaining two driveline knowledge bases, two spare stocks and two training paths, which only pays off when the duty genuinely spans both weight classes.

Fleet transition and driver training

Switching between the X6 and the NG80 is not only a procurement decision but an operating one. The X6 with its Cummins ISD and Fast 8 to 10 speed gearbox is close in feel to other medium SAGMOTO trucks, so a driver moving from a lighter unit adapts within a shift. The NG80's heavier clutch, taller gearing and stronger engine braking behaviour need explicit training, particularly on long descends where the retarder and engine brake strategy differs from a medium platform.

For a fleet standardising on the X6, the training burden is low and the consolidated parts pool is small. Adding even a few NG80 units introduces a second driveline knowledge base, a second axle and gearbox spare set, and a separate workshop briefing. That is acceptable when the heavy corridor genuinely needs the NG80, but it erodes the simplicity advantage that drew the fleet to a single brand in the first place. A phased transition, where the X6 covers the medium band and the NG80 is introduced only as heavy contracts are signed, keeps training and inventory costs proportional to actual utilization.

Driver retention also plays a role. In export markets where NG80 familiarity is already established, recruiting operators who know the heavy platform is easier, which lowers the training cost of adding NG80 units there. In markets newer to either brand, the X6's global Cummins support makes recruitment and training simpler. The procurement team should weight local driver familiarity alongside the pure specification comparison, because a truck the local crew cannot service confidently will cost more in downtime than the brochure suggests.

Verdict by use case

Choose the SAGMOTO X6 for regional distribution, dropside and flatbed cargo up to roughly 18 tonnes GVW, light construction, agricultural haulage and any duty needing all-wheel drive on unpaved corridors. It is the lower-risk, lower-cost, better-supported option in the medium band and the logical default for most export fleets that do not run continuous heavy tipping.

Choose the Beiben NG80 when the work is steady and heavy: mine haul roads, 30-tonne-plus payloads, long-haul heavy freight and continuous quarry duty where frame and axle ratings must exceed the X6 envelope. The higher purchase price is defensible only when utilization is consistently in the heavy class. For most mixed export fleets, the pragmatic answer is to standardise on the X6 for the medium band and bring in the NG80 only for the genuinely heavy corridor.
