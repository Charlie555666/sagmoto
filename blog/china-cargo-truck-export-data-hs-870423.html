How Many Heavy Cargo Trucks Does China Export? HS 870423 Data by Destination

2026-10-30Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: China's heavy diesel goods-vehicle exports under HS 8704.23, which covers trucks with a gross vehicle weight above 20 tonnes, ran at an estimated 250,000 to 320,000 units per year across all HS 8704 codes in 2024-2026, with the over-20-tonne segment the largest single share. Russia, Mexico and Saudi Arabia led destination demand, while FOB unit values typically fell in the US$35,000 to 65,000 band.

What Does HS Code 8704.23 Actually Cover?

The Harmonized System code 8704 groups motor vehicles for the transport of goods, and the final digit splits the class by gross vehicle weight. HS 8704.23 is the band for diesel goods vehicles with a GVW exceeding 20 tonnes, which captures the bulk of China's heavy tippers, tractor trucks and rigid cargo chassis that are exported as complete vehicles. This is distinct from 8704.21 (GVW not over 5 tonnes) and 8704.22 (GVW over 5 but not over 20 tonnes), which together cover light and medium commercials. Because most exported Chinese dump trucks, tractors and heavy rigids sit above the 20-tonne line once loaded, 8704.23 is the code that best represents the country's heavy-export scale. Customs classifications vary slightly by destination that applies its own tariff lines, but the 20-tonne threshold is the consistent international anchor for the heavy band.

How Many Heavy Trucks Does China Export Each Year?

Customs data show approximately 250,000 to 320,000 units per year of heavy trucks across the full HS 8704 family in the 2024-2026 window, and the over-20-tonne 8704.23 segment accounts for the largest portion of that volume. Industry observers place the 8704.23-specific share at roughly 55 to 65 percent of total HS 8704 exports, implying on the order of 140,000 to 200,000 heavy GVW-over-20t units annually. The remainder splits between medium and light commercials under 8704.21 and 8704.22. These ranges are compiled from customs-release aggregates and exporter shipment reports rather than a single official line, and they track closely with the production figures reported by the major Chinese truck groups. Year-on-year the heavy band proved more resilient than the light band as overseas infrastructure programmes absorbed tippers and tractors.

What Is the Typical Export Value per Unit?

FOB export values for heavy GVW-over-20t trucks typically range from US$35,000 to 65,000 per unit, with the low end representing a basic 6x4 tipper and the high end a fully specified 560 hp tractor with retarder and premium cab. Regional price levels differ: African and CIS buyers often specify mid-range tractors around US$40,000 to 50,000, while Gulf and Latin American fleets opt for higher-spec units near US$55,000 to 65,000. These are FOB plant or port figures and exclude ocean freight, insurance and destination duties, so landed cost is materially higher. Volume discounts on fleet orders of 50 units or more can push effective unit value down by 5 to 10 percent against list. The value band matters to importers because it sets the working-capital and financing envelope for a fleet renewal programme.

Why Do Customs Split 8704.21, 8704.22 and 8704.23?

The GVW split exists because tariff treatment, emission expectations and certification rules differ sharply between a 4-tonne delivery van and a 40-tonne tractor, so customs authorities need a clean line between light, medium and heavy. For the exporter and importer the split determines which technical regulation applies at the border: a 8704.23 unit above 20 tonnes will face heavier axle-load and brake standards than a 8704.22 unit, and many markets grant simplified entry or different duties to sub-20-tonne vehicles. The 20-tonne threshold is also the point at which most tipper and tractor chassis cross from medium to heavy duty in engineering terms, so the code aligns with how the industry itself classes the vehicles. Analysts tracking China's export strength therefore watch 8704.23 as the truest proxy for heavy-commercial outbound volume.

Regional demand for Chinese heavy trucks in 2024-2026 split broadly into three blocs: the CIS and Russia corridor, Africa, and ASEAN, with the Middle East and Latin America forming smaller but high-value slices. The CIS and Russia bloc is now the single largest regional buyer of Chinese heavy trucks, ahead of Africa and ASEAN combined in some quarters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is HS 8704.23 and what trucks does it include?

HS 8704.23 covers Chinese heavy diesel goods vehicles with a gross vehicle weight above 20 tonnes, and it represents roughly 55 to 65 percent of the 250,000-320,000 unit annual HS 8704 export total, making it the largest single heavy-truck code by volume.

Which country imports the most Chinese heavy trucks?

Russia is the single largest destination, taking an estimated 60,000 to 90,000 heavy units per year from China, well ahead of Mexico at 25,000 to 40,000 and Saudi Arabia at 18,000 to 30,000 units annually.

What is the typical FOB export value per heavy truck?

FOB export value for heavy GVW-over-20t trucks typically ranges from US$35,000 to 65,000 per unit, with basic 6x4 tippers near the low end and 560 hp tractors with retarders near the high end, before ocean freight and duties.

How fast are Chinese heavy-truck exports growing?

Heavy-truck exports grew roughly 8 to 15 percent year-on-year in 2024-2026 across the CIS and Africa regions, while ASEAN expanded 5 to 10 percent annually and Latin America rose 6 to 10 percent on nearshoring demand.

Do used Chinese heavy trucks add to these export figures?

Used-vehicle export under separate licensing adds an estimated 20,000 to 40,000 heavy units per year on top of the 250,000-320,000 new-truck figure, and these are reported outside the main HS 8704.23 new-vehicle statistics.

What separates the cheapest and priciest units inside one HS code

The customs band for heavy goods vehicles is wide because the code groups everything from a basic 6x4 day-cab tractor to a premium sleeper tractor with air suspension, retarder, and a full driver comfort package. A spec sheet review of export contracts shows the main price drivers are cab type, engine power class, transmission brand, axle specification, and emission hardware. A tractor built to Euro 2 for a frontier market can leave the factory at 40 percent below the price of the same platform configured to Euro 5 with selective catalytic reduction, and that gap widens further once AdBlue infrastructure and higher-grade lubricants are counted in the destination market.

Buyers comparing offers on the same HS code should therefore normalise quotations to a common specification before judging value. A practical checklist compares power rating, wheelbase, axle load rating, tyre size, cab suspension, and the after-sales package rather than the headline price. Customs data on unit values tell the same story from the top down: shipments to premium-regulated destinations consistently clear at higher average unit values than shipments to frontier markets, not because exporters charge more for identical trucks, but because the specification mix itself is different. Reading the unit value column as a proxy for specification mix is one of the fastest ways to sanity-check whether a quoted price is normal for its corridor.
