Importing SAGMOTO E6 Trucks to Cameroon: Port, Duties and Buyer Checklist 2026

2026-10-20SAGMOTO Buying Guide13 min read

Cameroon is a hub, and the E6 is the workhorse

Cameroon is the economic gateway of the CEMAC region. Its Port of Douala is the busiest in Central Africa, and from Douala, freight fans out to Yaoundé, Bafoussam, Bamenda, Ngaoundéré and — crucially — to the landlocked neighbours Chad and the Central African Republic. For fleet buyers, that makes a versatile medium-duty truck the backbone of the business, and the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake family, built on the E6 platform, is exactly that backbone. This guide covers how to import the SAGMOTO E6 to Cameroon: the Douala route, the CEMAC duty structure, the E6's specifications across body types, and a practical buyer checklist for 2026.

The E6 is SAGMOTO's versatile medium-to-heavy-duty range, spanning 12 to 60 tonnes GCW. It is offered in cargo, dump and tractor variants on a modular chassis, which means a single parts inventory can cover a mixed fleet. For Cameroon — where one operator may run a cargo truck to the market, a dump truck on a construction site, and a light tractor on regional distribution — that commonality is a real cost advantage.

For Cameroon's mixed duty, the Cummins ISD 210 or 260 with the 8-speed Fast Gear box is the recommended default: low-end torque for loaded starts on the hilly routes to the west and north, and an 8-speed spread that keeps the engine in its efficient band on the long Douala–Ngaoundéré run. EURO V is the safe registration choice, though EURO III remains acceptable for many in-service fleets; confirm the current MINTP (Ministry of Transport) position before ordering.

Key point for Cameroon: Standardise your fleet on one E6 engine (ISD 210 or 260) and one transmission (8JS85T). The E6's value is parts commonality — a mixed-spec fleet throws that advantage away and doubles your critical-spare inventory.

Body choices: tractor, cargo and dump

The E6 is not a single truck but a chassis for three distinct jobs. Specifying the right body is the first buying decision.

Tractor: the SAGMOTO tractor trucks built on the E6 6×4 chassis suit regional distribution and container shuttles within Cameroon and to neighbouring CEMAC states. With a 60-tonne GCW ceiling and the ISD engine, it covers the Douala–Yaoundé corridor efficiently.

Cargo: the E6 4×2 cargo truck (flatbed, dropside or box) is the urban and regional workhorse, with a payload up to 15 tonnes on the 4×2 variant. This is the unit most Cameroon operators buy first, because it serves both market distribution and general freight.

Dump: the SAGMOTO dump truck models on the E6 6×4 chassis (10–16 m³ bodies) serve construction, laterite haulage and quarry access. For heavier 8×4 dumping, the larger X6s / X9s platforms are the step-up; the E6 is the right size for municipal and light construction duty.

The import route: Douala and the CEMAC corridor

Every E6 imported to Cameroon arrives at the Port of Douala. The flow is: SAGMOTO factory in Shaanxi → container or RoRo vessel from a Chinese port → transit via the Indian Ocean and around the Cape or through the Suez → discharge at Douala Autonomous Port. From Douala, units are cleared and either driven or trucked inland. For landlocked Chad and CAR, Douala is the mandatory seaboard, so many E6 units are transhipped onward under Cameroon transit arrangements.

Douala is efficient but busy, and congestion is real. Build clearance buffer into the schedule, and use a clearing agent (transitaire) registered with the Douala port community system. For a complete cargo or dump truck, container shipping is common; RoRo is competitive for multiple units. Either way, the documents must be consistent — invoice, packing list, bill of lading and Certificate of Conformity all referencing the same VIN and value.

The two charges that move the number are the CEMAC import duty and Cameroon's 19.25% VAT, both assessed on the CIF value plus accumulated levies. As with the other markets in this series, the VAT is the real cash variable and is payable at clearance. VAT-registered businesses may recover input VAT through the normal credit mechanism, which matters for established freight companies.

Homologation and registration

Cameroon expects imported vehicles to meet recognised standards and to carry a Certificate of Conformity. For the E6, SAGMOTO issues the CoC, an EURO III/V emissions declaration, and E-mark / lighting and brake documentation. Cameroon is left-hand drive, and the E6 is delivered LHD as standard — no conversion required. The MINTP road-worthiness inspection (visite technique) will check brakes, lighting, tyres and emissions, so ensure the unit ships with the correct lamp configuration and a speed limiter set to the local legal maximum.

Buyer checklist for a Cameroonian E6 import

  1. Choose the body first: cargo (4×2), dump (6×4) or light tractor (6×4).
  2. Standardise on ISD 210 or 260 and the 8JS85T 8-speed across the fleet.
  3. Confirm EURO III vs EURO V with MINTP, and request the CoC document pack.
  4. Obtain a written landed-cost estimate including CEMAC duty and 19.25% VAT.
  5. Engage a Douala-registered transitaire before the vessel sails.
  6. Confirm LHD, lamp configuration, speed limiter and bilingual VIN plate.
  7. Pre-position a shared critical-spare kit (filters, belts, brake kits) in Douala or Yaoundé.
Key point: The E6's whole-life cost advantage in Cameroon comes from parts commonality and the Cummins ISD service network. Protect that advantage by standardising the spec and by using a registered Douala clearing agent who knows the CEMAC paperwork cold.

Payment, delivery and warranty terms

SAGMOTO's standard export terms for Cameroon are a 30 percent deposit to schedule production and the balance against a clean bill of lading or an irrevocable letter of credit. Stock E6 units ship in 15 to 20 days; a body-specific or emission-specific configuration extends this to 30 to 45 days. The sea transit to Douala runs 28 to 36 days depending on the routing, so a realistic first-order horizon is seven to eleven weeks from deposit to Cameroonian registration. Build the CEMAC duty and the 19.25 percent VAT into the cash-flow plan from day one, because both are payable at Douala clearance.

The E6 carries SAGMOTO's 12-month / 100,000 km warranty, with the Cummins ISD engine covered under Cummins' own international framework — a genuine advantage in Cameroon given the regional Cummins service presence. Fenghan Trading issues the CoC and the export document pack and provides remote diagnostic support, and can coordinate driver training for fleet orders. For operators serving Chad and CAR, the warranty relationship should be paired with a Douala or Yaoundé service contact, because the overland leg to those markets is where a small fault becomes a long delay.

After-sales and parts strategy

The E6's Cummins ISD engine is serviced through Cummins' regional network, so routine filters, turbochargers and aftertreatment parts are obtainable without waiting on a container from China. SAGMOTO ships a critical-spare kit with fleet orders and provides remote technical advisory. For a Douala-based operator serving Chad and CAR, pre-positioning one shared kit inland (Yaoundé or Bafoussam) shortens the downtime that would otherwise strand a truck far from the port.

Conclusion

Importing the SAGMOTO E6 to Cameroon is a straightforward, well-trodden process: ship to Douala, clear under the CEMAC tariff, register LHD under MINTP, and standardise the fleet for parts savings. The E6's 12–60 tonne GCW span, Cummins ISD power and cargo/dump/tractor flexibility make it the right platform for a Cameroon operator who needs one chassis to do three jobs. For operators serving the wider CEMAC region, the E6 also functions as the practical bridge unit between the light urban delivery truck and the heavy X-series mining and construction platforms, letting a growing fleet scale body-by-body without fragmenting its parts inventory. Specified well, an E6 lands in the USD 28,000–58,000 range and delivers the reliability and parts coverage that define a profitable Central African fleet in 2026.
