E3 MAX Mobile Crane Chassis: Application Guide

2026-10-29Industry Application10 min read

A mobile crane is only as stable as the chassis underneath it, because the carrier must hold the tare weight, the boom reaction and the outrigger loads without twisting, and it must drive the hydraulic pumps that lift the load. The SAGMOTO E3 MAX heavy chassis is a capable crane carrier base because its Weichai WP12 produces 460 to 520 hp and up to 2,250 Nm, its frame is built for tipper and freight duty, and its power take-off can run a crane hydraulic pump all day. This guide walks through frame strength, outrigger load paths, PTO provision, stability, axle load distribution, the 10 to 25 tonne crane classes that pair well, and the jobsite cycles a crane carrier actually sees.

1. Frame Strength for Crane Duty

The E3 MAX rides on a heavy straight-rail frame with a deep section and close pitch, which is what a crane subframe needs to spread the turret load without localising stress at a single cross-member. For a 20 tonne class crane we specify the reinforced frame option with extra cross-members ahead of and behind the turret, and the subframe is bolted and welded to the rails so the slewing moment transfers into the chassis as a beam load rather than a point load. A frame that flexes under the turret will crack the body and wander the crane's level sensor, so stiffness here is the first spec decision.

We also specify the air or heavy multi-leaf suspension to hold the chassis square when the outriggers extend, because a chassis that lists on its springs shifts the crane's bubble and the operator chases it all day.

2. Outrigger Loads and the Load Path

Outriggers move the crane's reaction from the tyres to the ground through four pads, and the chassis must carry that reaction through the subframe to the frame without exceeding the rail allowable. A 20 tonne crane at full extension can put 30 to 45 kN on each pad depending on the boom angle and the lifted radius, and the E3 MAX frame is checked against that vertical and lateral combination before the build is released. The outrigger boxes are mounted to the reinforced cross-members, never to a single rail, and the pad area is sized to the site ground bearing so the crane does not punch into soft ground.

On a 25 tonne class unit the loads climb further, so we step up to the 8x4 E3 MAX carrier and widen the outrigger spread, because the longer wheelbase and the four-point pad pattern are what keep the combination inside the stability envelope on a real yard.

Check the pads against ground, not just the chart: A crane that is stable on a concrete pad may tip on a gravel shoulder because the bearing pressure exceeds the soil. Size the outrigger pad area to the worst site you will visit, not the best, or the stability calculation is paper only.

3. PTO for the Crane Hydraulic Pump

The crane is driven by a transmission-mounted or engine-mounted power take-off feeding a variable displacement pump at a flow the boom and winch demand. For a 20 tonne crane we specify a PTO rated around 90 to 130 kW take-off with a pump delivering roughly 180 to 260 litres per minute at system pressure near 250 to 310 bar, which gives a smooth simultaneous boom and winch action without a pressure sag. The pump and the PTO are sized to the crane maker's data sheet, not to a generic figure, because every crane brand has its own flow and pressure curve.

The hydraulic tank gets a dedicated cooler with a thermostatic fan, because a crane that cycles all day in summer heat will cook the oil and the seals if the cooling is left to chance. We fit the cooler as standard on every E3 MAX crane carrier.

4. Stability and Level Control

Stability is a function of outrigger spread, chassis level and the lifted radius, and the E3 MAX carrier supports the crane's own level sensor through a rigid frame and a level subframe. We set the outrigger cylinders to hold the chassis within a fraction of a degree, and we mark the maximum working radius on the cab card so the operator never lifts beyond the chart. The carrier's low centre of gravity, with the crane turret mounted close to the frame, helps the bubble stay calm when the boom swings across the side.

We also confirm the carrier's parking brake and the outrigger interlock logic, because a crane that can slew while the pads are up is a danger. The interlock is part of the crane control, but the chassis must present clean electrical signals for it to work.

5. Axle Load Distribution

A crane carrier must satisfy the axle limits both driving to site and parked on pads, and the two states are different. On the road the weight sits on the tyres within the legal bridge split; on site the outriggers take most of the reaction and the tyres carry little. We calculate the tare split with the crane and the counterweight fitted, then set the wheelbase so the steer and drive axles are legal in the heaviest market you work. A 20 tonne crane on an 8x4 E3 MAX typically sits around 7.5 tonnes on the steer and 19 tonnes across the rear bogie, which clears the common 11.5 and 19 tonne limits with margin.

The counterweight position also shifts the split. We model the crane fitted and stowed, because a counterweight hung too far back overloads the rear axle and a hungry front axle lightens the steer. The carrier spec fixes the legal envelope before the crane is mounted.

6. Crane Model Pairing, 10 to 25 Tonne

The E3 MAX pairs with a wide band of crane brands in the 10 to 25 tonne class. A 10 tonne unit suits a 6x4 460 hp carrier for light building-supply and equipment-set work; a 16 tonne unit suits the 480 hp 6x4 for general hire and plant lifting; a 20 tonne unit needs the 500 hp 8x4 for the turret load and the road weight; and a 25 tonne unit needs the 520 hp 8x4 with the widest outrigger spread for the heaviest chart. We match the carrier to the crane maker's mount drawing so the turret bolts line up first time.

Because the E3 MAX shares its heavy backbone with the tipper line, the same depot can run a crane carrier and a SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 on one common set of suspension and brake parts, which lowers the inventory cost of a mixed plant yard.

7. Jobsite Cycles and Duty

A crane carrier spends little time at highway speed and a lot of time idling on site with the PTO running, so the duty is closer to a tipper than a long-haul tractor. The Weichai WP12 at 460 to 520 hp is rarely near full load when lifting, but its 2,250 Nm means the boom never lags and the carrier climbs the site access without strain. Fuel use is dominated by idling and the hydraulic pump, and we fit idle-up control so the engine holds the pump speed without labouring at a low idle.

The jobsite also beats the driveline with potholes, mud and grade changes, so we specify the hub-reduction rear axle option where the site is rough, because it protects the diff and raises the ground clearance for the access track to the pad.

Match the carrier to the worst site, not the yard: A crane that lifts fine on the depot apron may not reach the far corner of a muddy site because the carrier lacks the clearance or the axle. Spec the ground clearance and the diff protection to the roughest access you will drive, then the crane earns its keep everywhere.

8. Maintenance for Crane Carriers

The crane carrier lives in dust and load, so the plan differs from a dry freight truck. Grease the turret bearing and the outrigger pivots daily, flush the hydraulic tank and change the element every 1,000 hours of PTO run, inspect the subframe bolts for slack every service, and wash the chassis to clear the grit that eats the suspension. The PTO and the pump seals need a weekly look, and the outrigger pads should be checked for cracks that let the crane settle on a weak corner.

Reinforce the frame at the turret, size the PTO to the crane data sheet, set the outrigger pads to the ground bearing, and keep the axle split legal in the heaviest market. A correctly specified E3 MAX crane carrier lifts safely on the worst pad and drives home legally at the end of the shift, which is all a plant hire or contracting fleet asks of it.
