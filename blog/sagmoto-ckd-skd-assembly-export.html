Does SAGMOTO Offer CKD or SKD Kits for Local Truck Assembly?

Sep 30, 2026Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Yes. SAGMOTO supplies both SKD and CKD kits for local assembly. SKD ships major assemblies semi-assembled and needs only bolt-up and fluids; CKD ships component-level packs requiring a full line. Typical duty saving is 10 to 25 percent versus a built-up unit, with MOQ from 20 units for SKD and 100 units for CKD.

Knocked-down supply is the standard route into any market where the government wants vehicles assembled locally and the tariff schedule rewards it. Across North and West Africa, parts of the Middle East and Central Asia, the duty differential between a complete truck and a set of components is large enough to change the commercial case, and several markets now penalise built-up imports directly.

The difficulty is that CKD and SKD are used loosely, and buyers often discover after contracting that they have bought something between the two. This article sets out what each level contains, what the plant must do, what the duty arithmetic produces, and where the minimum quantities and support commitments sit.

What Is the Difference Between SKD and CKD?

Semi knocked down, or SKD, means the truck arrives as major assemblies that are already built and tested: a complete engine and gearbox assembly, an assembled chassis frame with axles and suspension fitted, a trimmed cab, and a body or tipper assembly where applicable. The local operation bolts these together, connects the driveline, wiring and air systems, fills fluids, mounts the body, and performs the final setting and inspection. No machining, welding or painting of major structures is required.

Completely knocked down, or CKD, means the truck arrives at component level: frame rails, crossmembers, axle housings and shafts, engine parts or a sub-assembled engine, loose body panels, wiring looms, fasteners and trim. The local plant builds the frame, assembles axles, paints the cab and body, assembles the engine where the scope includes it, and produces a finished vehicle. This is a manufacturing operation, not an assembly operation, and it carries the capital cost, quality system and training burden that implies.

Key point: Choose SKD when the objective is duty saving and market entry with minimal capital. Choose CKD when the objective is local content, industrial policy qualification and long-term cost reduction. SKD needs a workshop; CKD needs a factory, and confusing the two is the most expensive error in a kit programme.

How Much Duty Can a Kit Save?

The duty case is the reason most buyers ask about kits, and it is genuinely strong in the right market. Most tariff schedules place complete vehicles at a higher rate than components, and the differential is often 10 to 25 percentage points. A built-up truck imported at 20 percent duty against a kit whose weighted average component rate is 8 percent saves 12 percent of the customs value, which on a 50-unit fleet order is a substantial number.

Two secondary savings are worth counting. Freight per unit improves because knocked-down kits pack densely: two to three SKD units fit in a 40 ft container where a built-up truck occupies a flat rack or a roll-on slot, which reduces ocean freight per unit materially. And local assembly frequently qualifies for industrial incentives, tax holidays or preferential treatment in public and parastatal tenders, which in some markets is worth more than the duty saving itself.

What Is Included in a SAGMOTO SKD Kit?

An SKD kit is supplied as a matched, per-vehicle set so that the local line is not sorting parts. A typical kit contains the frame with axles, suspension and brake gear fitted; the engine and gearbox assembly with its mounting hardware; the cab, trimmed and glazed; the body or tipper assembly plus the hydraulic system where the model requires it; the wiring loom, air and fuel plumbing; wheels and tyres; and a fastener and small-parts pack for one vehicle.

The kit ships with assembly documentation in the agreed language: a build sequence, torque values, fluid specifications and capacities, and the end-of-line inspection checklist that the plant must complete and record. Consumables that are cheaper to buy locally, such as batteries on some markets and standard tyre sizes, can be excluded by agreement to reduce freight and to increase local content.

Buyers planning an SKD programme for mixed work should note that the kit structure follows the model. A fleet assembling both a tractor and a tipper, for example, is running two kit specifications and should plan the line and the training for both. The model families that suit kit supply best are the high-volume, single-specification ranges such as the SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 range and the SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover range, because repetition is what makes an assembly line efficient.

What Does a CKD Programme Require?

A CKD programme is a manufacturing project and should be planned as one. The plant needs a frame line with jigs, an axle assembly area, a paint facility with pretreatment and topcoat capability, a cab trim line, an engine assembly area where the scope includes it, and an end-of-line test area for brakes, lights, alignment and road test. Utilities, compressed air, paint waste treatment and a parts warehouse sized to the build schedule all sit inside the project.

The people requirement is equally significant: 40 to 120 production staff depending on volume, plus inspectors and production engineering. Most first-time assemblers bring in supplier support for the first two or three build batches, and that support should be costed rather than assumed as goodwill.

What MOQ, Lead Time and Support Apply?

SKD programmes start at roughly 20 units per shipment. Below that, the container packing efficiency, the documentation work and the technical support cost per unit do not repay themselves, and built-up units are cheaper. CKD programmes start at about 100 units per year, because the tooling, jigs, training and quality process only amortise across volume.

Lead time to first vehicle differs sharply by level. SKD typically produces a first truck within 90 to 150 days of contract signature, including plant preparation, training and the first kit shipment. CKD takes nine to eighteen months because the plant itself has to be built and commissioned. Buyers with a defined fleet replacement date should work backwards from that date rather than forwards from the signature.

  • Technical documentation. Build manuals, torque specifications, fluid charts and inspection checklists in the agreed language, supplied with the first kit.
  • Training. Assembly training at the supplier's facility or at the plant, plus on-site support for the first build batches.
  • Tooling and jigs. Specified and quoted separately; required for CKD and for higher-volume SKD.
  • Parts supply for the line. A line-side parts programme so that a shortage does not stop the build.
  • Quality audit. An agreed audit process with defined acceptance criteria, which is also what protects the warranty position.

Should Your Market Entry Use Kits or Built-Up Units?

The decision reduces to three questions. Is the duty differential large enough to pay for the plant? Is the annual volume high enough to amortise the tooling and training? And is there a policy reason, meaning local content rules, tender preferences or import restrictions, that makes assembly necessary rather than merely cheaper?

Where the answer to all three is yes, CKD is the right long-term structure. Where the duty case is real but volume is modest, SKD is almost always right, because it captures most of the duty benefit at a fraction of the capital cost and can later be deepened into CKD as volume grows. Where the differential is small or volume is under about 50 units a year, buy built-up and put the capital into parts inventory and service capability instead, which will do more for fleet availability than a marginal duty saving.

Conclusion

SAGMOTO supplies knocked-down kits at both levels, and the right choice depends on volume and policy rather than on ambition. SKD captures 8 to 18 percent of vehicle value in duty saving with plant capital of USD 150,000 to USD 500,000 and a first truck inside five months. CKD captures 12 to 25 percent, qualifies for local content and industrial incentives, and requires USD 3 to 15 million and a year or more of project work. Both are viable; choosing the wrong one for the volume available is not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SAGMOTO offer CKD or SKD kits for local truck assembly?

Yes. SAGMOTO supplies semi knocked down kits, which ship major assemblies pre-built and require bolt-up, fluids and final setting, and completely knocked down kits, which ship at component level for full local assembly. SKD starts at 20 units per order and CKD typically starts at 100 units per year, subject to model and market.

What is the minimum order quantity for a SAGMOTO CKD or SKD kit programme?

SKD programmes start at about 20 units per shipment and CKD programmes at about 100 units per year, because CKD requires tooling, jigs, training and a documented quality process that only amortise across volume. Below those thresholds, built-up units delivered complete are usually cheaper and faster than any kit arrangement.

How much duty can I save by assembling trucks locally from kits?

Local assembly from kits typically reduces total import duty and tax by 10 to 25 percent of landed vehicle value compared with a fully built-up import, because most markets tariff knocked-down components below complete vehicles. The saving is largest where the duty differential between parts and vehicles exceeds 15 percentage points and where local assembly also qualifies for industrial incentives.

What tooling does an SKD assembly plant need?

An SKD line needs a level floor with chassis stands rated for the axle load, overhead lifting of 5 to 10 tonnes, torque wrenches calibrated to the specified values, fluid filling equipment, air system test gear and a basic end-of-line check area. Capital cost is typically USD 150,000 to USD 500,000, well below the USD 3 to 15 million required for a full CKD body and paint line.

Does assembling locally affect the SAGMOTO warranty?

Warranty is maintained on kit-assembled trucks provided the assembly partner follows the documented build procedure and passes the agreed quality audit, and cover remains 12 months or 100,000 km on the powertrain. Assembly faults are the responsibility of the assembly partner, while component defects remain covered, so the contract must state the split clearly before the first kit ships.
