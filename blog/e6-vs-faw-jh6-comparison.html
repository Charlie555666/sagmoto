SAGMOTO E6 vs FAW JH6: Heavy-Duty Tractor Comparison for Long-Haul Operations 2026

2026-09-11SAGMOTO BlogShaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd.

Introduction

The SAGMOTO E6 and FAW JH6 are two of China's most popular heavy-duty long-haul tractor trucks, competing in the 6x4, 420-460 HP segment. Both trucks target long-haul freight corridors in China and export markets including Russia, Central Asia, Africa, and Southeast Asia. This comparison provides fleet operators with a detailed analysis of powertrain, cab comfort, fuel economy, maintenance, and pricing to inform procurement decisions. For the full SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover range, visit our product page.

The SAGMOTO E6 and FAW JH6 share similar GVW ratings (49 tonnes) and target applications (long-haul freight), but represent different engineering philosophies. The E6 uses a Weichai WP12.460 engine with a FAST 12-speed transmission, prioritizing component standardization and global parts availability. The JH6 uses a FAW CA6DM2-46E5 engine with a FAW CA12TAX230M transmission, prioritizing integrated drivetrain design and domestic parts cost advantages.

Both engines produce 460 HP, but the E6's WP12.460 develops its peak power at 1,900 rpm versus the JH6's 2,100 rpm, meaning the E6 achieves peak power at lower engine speed. More importantly, the WP12.460 delivers 100 percent of its torque (2,200 Nm) at 1,000 rpm, while the CA6DM2-46E5 only delivers 83 percent of its torque (1,750 Nm) at 1,000 rpm. This low-rpm torque advantage benefits the E6 in heavy-load starts and grade climbing, where the engine can pull from lower rpm without downshifting.

Fuel Economy Comparison

In controlled long-haul testing (1,000 km highway at 80 km/h, 40-tonne trailer), the SAGMOTO E6 recorded 30-33 L/100km while the FAW JH6 recorded 31-34 L/100km. The E6's 1 L/100km advantage is attributed to the WP12's higher thermal efficiency (43 percent vs 41 percent) and the FAST transmission's optimized gear spacing enabling lower engine rpm at cruise speed.

The JH6 has a slight advantage in cab comfort, with a wider lower berth, more standing height, automatic climate control, and lower noise levels. These advantages matter for long-haul operations where drivers spend 10-14 hours per day in the cab and sleep 6-8 hours in the sleeper. Driver comfort directly affects driver retention, which is a significant cost factor for fleets facing driver shortages.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO E6 and FAW JH6 are both excellent long-haul tractor trucks with nearly equivalent 5-year TCO. The E6's advantages in fuel economy and global parts availability make it the better choice for export market operators who value uptime and fuel efficiency. The JH6's advantages in cab comfort and lower maintenance costs make it attractive for domestic Chinese operations and markets with established FAW dealer networks. For fleet operators making procurement decisions, the choice should be based on specific operational priorities: choose the E6 for fuel efficiency and global parts support; choose the JH6 for driver comfort and lower domestic maintenance costs.
