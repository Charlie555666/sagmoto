How Do You Verify a China Truck Exporter's Business License and Customs Registration?

2026-11-27Buyer Guide10 min read

Quick answer: Check three documents in order: the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code on the business licence, the scope wording covering goods import and export, and the 10-digit customs registration code. Then confirm the invoice issuer, contracting entity and bank beneficiary are one named company. AEO status helps clearance but does not prove manufacturing capability.

A verification exercise is not a test of whether a seller is honest; it is a test of whether the party named on your contract can lawfully ship the trucks and answer a claim afterwards. Chinese company documents are public in structure and follow a predictable format, so a buyer can do most of the work from a desk. The sequence below turns a pile of scans into a short set of answers, with each step ruling out a specific failure. Run it before a deposit, not after a dispute starts.

How do you read an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code?

The Unified Social Credit Code, usually shortened to USCC, is an 18-character identifier assigned to one registered Chinese legal entity. It appears on the business licence, the VAT invoice and most formal company documents. Read it as a whole rather than trying to interpret every segment: the practical test is that the same 18 characters appear on every paper you receive, in the same order. A code that changes between the licence, the proforma invoice and the contract means you are dealing with more than one legal entity, even if the trading name looks similar. Ask for a clear colour scan of the licence and type the code into a note; comparing typed strings is more reliable than comparing photographs of small print.

The code also links to the registered company name, which must match the name on the quotation. A seller that explains a different name as a holding company, a sales branch or a partner factory may have a perfectly legitimate structure, but the relationship should be stated in writing and reflected in the contract.

What must the business licence scope include?

The business licence states an approved scope of activity. For a truck exporter, the wording should cover goods import and export, or motor vehicle sales together with import and export of goods. A licence that lists only domestic retail, consulting or general trading may not support an export invoice issued in that company's own name. Scope wording matters more than registered capital, which can be set high without any factory or stock behind it. Ask for the licence bearing the 18-character code and check that the named company is the one issuing your quotation.

A declared manufacturer should show production or assembly of motor vehicles in its scope, often with a registered plant address. A trading company will show sales and import and export only, which is entirely normal for an intermediary. A seller calling itself a factory while holding a sales-only licence is a trading company, and you should price the relationship accordingly.

Key point: The licence scope tells you what the entity is authorised to do, not what it claims to do. Match the wording to the role the seller plays in your contract.

Which customs registration code proves export standing?

A company that declares shipments to customs holds a 10-digit customs registration code, sometimes described as a customs filing or registration number. It appears on the customs declaration and confirms that the entity is known to the customs authority and can act as a declarant or a consignor. It does not prove that the company builds trucks, that it owns a factory, or that it will honour a warranty. Treat the 10-digit code as one layer of a stack, not as a verdict.

Ask for a sample customs declaration with the code visible, and check that the exporter name on it matches the licence and the invoice. A seller that can quote a code but cannot produce a sample declaration has given you a number without evidence.

Should you treat AEO status as proof of a good supplier?

No. AEO status is a customs compliance and security accreditation that can bring practical benefits such as fewer inspections and faster clearance. It tells you the company keeps good records and meets customs obligations, which is useful. It says nothing about build quality, spare parts depth, warranty administration or whether the entity manufactures the trucks it sells. A trading company can hold AEO status and a factory may not have applied for it. Ask for the certificate and check the expiry date, then place it alongside the licence and invoice checks rather than above them. In most export markets the benefit is logistics efficiency, not a guarantee of product performance.

How do you verify the invoice issuer and bank beneficiary?

This is the step that protects the money. The entity that signs the commercial contract, the entity named as issuer on the proforma and commercial invoices, and the beneficiary on the bank account should be the same named company with the same 18-character code. A mismatch is the most common route to a lost deposit, because a payer has little recourse once funds reach an unrelated account. Ask for a bank certificate or a signed letter on company letterhead naming the account, and confirm the account name by an independent channel rather than by replying to the email that requested it.

Payment behaviour is a useful signal. A request to pay an individual's account, a third-party payer with no connection to the contract, or a beneficiary in a different country from the contracting entity should stop the transaction until it is explained in writing. A beneficiary that appears for the first time on the final invoice is a red flag, whatever the discount offered.

Which red flags should stop a payment?

Red flags rarely appear alone. A seller that refuses a licence scan, changes the company name between the proforma and the contract, and offers only a hotel address for a factory visit is describing a structure that cannot be verified. How do you cross-check a declared factory against a trading company?

A manufacturer's licence carries a production address and, in many cases, a scope that includes vehicle manufacturing. A trading company usually lists a commercial office. If a seller claims factory status, ask for the registered plant address and offer a live video walk-through at an agreed time. A real-time view of the chassis area, with questions answered while the camera is moving, is far more useful than a recorded clip. Ask who issues the chassis certificate and the Vehicle Identification Number: the registered manufacturer issues both, and a trading company cannot name the issuer precisely. If the seller forwards scans from an unnamed source, you are dealing with an intermediary.

What is the practical six-step verification sequence?

Work in this order and note the answer to each question on a single sheet. First, request the licence and record the 18-character code; this rules out a seller who cannot identify a legal entity. Second, read the scope wording; this rules out an entity not authorised to export goods. Third, obtain the 10-digit customs registration code and a sample declaration; this rules out a company that has never shipped. Fourth, check AEO status and its expiry; this establishes customs standing but deliberately does not count as proof of manufacturing. Fifth, compare the contracting entity, invoice issuer and bank beneficiary; this rules out a misdirected payment. Sixth, cross-check the declared factory through the certificate issuer and a live site view; this tells you whether the party selling the trucks builds them or resells them. Buyers who also want brand appointment evidence can review the authorized heavy truck distributor verification guidance, but the six steps should be completed first.

Keep the scanned documents, the typed codes and the notes together in the order file. If a dispute arises months later, that record shows what you checked and what the seller represented at the time. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code used for?

The 18-character Unified Social Credit Code is the identifier that links a Chinese company's business licence, VAT invoice and bank account, and it must match character for character on all three. Type the code from each document and compare the strings rather than trusting a photograph of small print.

Which business licence scope wording covers truck exports?

The scope must include goods import and export, or motor vehicle sales together with import and export, otherwise the company cannot lawfully invoice an export shipment in its own name. A manufacturer will also show production or assembly wording and a registered plant address.

What does a 10-digit customs registration code confirm?

The 10-digit customs registration code confirms the entity is registered with customs and can appear as exporter on a declaration, which rules out a company that has never shipped. It does not prove factory ownership, shipment volume, parts depth or warranty performance.

Does AEO certification prove a supplier is a manufacturer?

AEO certification shows a company meets customs compliance and security standards and may see inspection rates below 5 percent, but it does not prove manufacturing capability or product quality. A trading company can hold AEO status while a factory may not have applied.

Why must the invoice issuer match the bank beneficiary?

In a clean transaction the contracting entity, invoice issuer and bank beneficiary carry the same name and the same 18-character code, and any difference should be explained in writing before payment. A personal account, a third-party payer or a beneficiary introduced at the last invoice is a serious warning sign.
