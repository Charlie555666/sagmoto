A reliable supplier eliminates these risks at the source. Here is how to identify one.

Type 2: Large Trading Companies (Verify Carefully)

Shandong- and Hebei-based trading companies advertise aggressively on B2B platforms. Some are legitimate volume exporters with their own inspection teams; others are intermediaries who buy from dealers and resell. They can be a reasonable choice for used trucks (where factory warranty has expired anyway) — but for new vehicles, apply the 5-point framework strictly: if they cannot show factory authorization, you are paying new-truck prices without new-truck protection.

Type 3: B2B Platform Listings (Highest Risk)

Listings on generic B2B platforms often advertise "Shacman dump truck USD 20,000-26,000" — prices below the factory gate price of a new vehicle. These are almost always used, refurbished, or stock units photographed to look new. If a quote is 30-50% below every other offer, the product is different, not the price.

Common trap: A trader quotes a "new X3000 6x4" at USD 32,000. The truck arrives and is a 2023 stock unit with Euro II emission that cannot be registered in your Euro V market. The discount was USD 4,000; the loss is the entire truck.
