SAGMOTO X5 Cooling System Maintenance: Radiator, Water Pump and Thermostat Guide

2026-10-20Maintenance & After-sales12 min read

The cooling system is the X5's first line of defence in hot climates

The SAGMOTO X5 is a medium-duty workhorse built around the Cummins ISD and Yuchai YC6J engine families, with power ratings from 200 to 380 HP and an 8-speed Fast Gear 8JS85T transmission on a reinforced 800 × 250 mm frame. It is exported to some of the harshest operating environments on earth — open-pit mines, Gulf construction sites, equatorial haul roads and desert corridors where ambient temperature routinely exceeds 45 °C with heavy dust loading. In those conditions the cooling system is not a comfort feature; it is the component that decides whether the truck completes its shift or is towed off the grade with a warped cylinder head. This guide covers the radiator, the water pump and the thermostat of the X5, the service intervals that keep them alive, the inspection steps a workshop can follow, the failure symptoms to recognise early, and the genuine-parts discipline that protects the engine.

The X5's cooling demand is driven by duty as much as by climate. A 6×4 X5 hauling aggregate on a mining ramp holds high load and low road speed, which means the fan and the radiator are working at maximum while the truck barely moves air through them. Compared with a highway run at the same engine power, a mining or construction duty cycle loads the cooling package far harder per hour. The maintenance schedule below is therefore written for high-duty and hot-climate operation; fleets in temperate, low-duty service can extend the longer intervals but should never skip the pre-summer inspection.

Step-by-step radiator inspection

The radiator is the most exposed and most neglected part of the X5 cooling loop. Follow this procedure at every 500-hour service.

1. External fin inspection and cleaning

Shut down, isolate the battery, and visually inspect the fin pack from both sides. A clogged radiator shows a uniform grey or brown mat of dust on the air side, while a clean one shows bright metal between fins. Use low-pressure compressed air or a soft-water wash from the engine side outward — never a high-pressure lance straight into the fins, which folds them and permanently reduces airflow. In Gulf or Saharan dust, clean monthly regardless of the schedule.

2. Core and tank leak check

With the engine cold, inspect the aluminium core and the plastic or soldered tanks for staining, crusty deposits or dampness. Coolant leaves a white or coloured trail as it evaporates; that trail marks the leak path. Pressure-test the system to the X5 specification (typically around 1.2 to 1.4 bar) and watch the gauge for drop over five minutes.

3. Mount and vibration check

A radiator that vibrates against the frame cracks at the tanks. Check the rubber isolators and the mounting bolts; the X5's reinforced frame resists twist, but a loose mount will still fatigue the core on a haul road.

Step-by-step water pump inspection

The water pump is a sealed unit driven by the fan belt. It fails quietly until it fails loudly.

1. Weep hole inspection

The pump has a small weep hole at the bearing seals. A few drops after a long run are normal; a continuous coolant drip or a rust streak from the hole means the seal has failed and the pump must be replaced. Do not seal the hole — it is a tell-tale, not a drain.

2. Bearing play and noise

With the belt off, rock the fan hub. Any perceptible axial or radial play indicates worn bearings. A grinding or whining noise at idle that changes with revs is the same warning. Replace the pump; a seized pump takes the belt and the fan with it.

3. Belt and tensioner

Check the drive belt for glazing, cracking and fraying, and confirm tensioner travel is within range. A slipping belt under-drives the pump and the fan exactly when the X5 needs them most.

Step-by-step thermostat inspection

The thermostat governs warm-up and peak temperature. A stuck-open unit over-cools the engine and raises emissions and wear; a stuck-closed unit overheats within minutes.

1. Function test

Remove the thermostat and suspend it in a heated water bath with a thermometer. It should begin to open at its stamped temperature (typically 82 to 88 °C for the X5's engine families) and be fully open by roughly 10 °C above that, with the spring side moving freely. If it sticks at any point, replace it.

2. In-vehicle symptom check

On a cold start, the upper hose should stay cool until the thermostat opens, then warm rapidly. If the hose is hot immediately, the thermostat is stuck open; if the hose never gets hot and the temperature climbs, it is stuck closed.

Climate-specific advice: dust and heat

Dust is the radiator's enemy. In mining and desert operation, fine dust packs the fin pack like felt and blocks airflow without any visible leak. The countermeasure is discipline, not parts: a daily walk-around that includes a look at the radiator face, and a monthly pressure-clean, prevents the slow loss of cooling capacity that ends in an overheated shutdown. Fit a pre-cleaner to the engine air intake as well, because a dust-starved engine runs hot and dirty.

Heat compounds the dust problem. Above 45 °C ambient, the temperature difference between coolant and air shrinks, so the radiator rejects less heat per pass. The X5 should run the uprated cooling package in these markets, the coolant concentration should be verified before every summer, and the fan clutch should be treated as a wear item replaced on schedule rather than at failure. Park the truck in shade during crew breaks where possible, and never remove or disable the thermostat to "fix" an overheating complaint — an engine that never reaches operating temperature wears faster and emits more.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO X5 cooling system — radiator, water pump and thermostat — is robust, but only against a maintenance routine that respects the climate it works in. Set the intervals on engine hours, clean the radiator more often than the coolant is changed, test the thermostat and the fan clutch before blaming the coolant, and fit only genuine SAGMOTO components. Fleets that follow this routine in mining and hot-climate duty typically avoid the two most expensive cooling failures — a warped head from a slow-overheat and a tow from a clogged fin pack — and keep their X5 units earning instead of overheating.
