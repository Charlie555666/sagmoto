SAGMOTO X6 vs DAF XF: Heavy-Duty Tractor Comparison 2026

September 8, 2026Comparison Guide11 min read

The DAF XF has been the benchmark premium European tractor in the 480-530 hp heavy-duty segment for more than a decade, prized for fuel efficiency, driver comfort, and residual value. The SAGMOTO X6 is the latest Chinese challenger, with Weichai powertrain options, a price point that delivers immediate acquisition savings, and configurations that match the African, Middle Eastern, and Central Asian operations where heavy-duty tractor demand is concentrated. This 2026 comparison examines the X6 against the DAF XF across powertrain, driveline, payload, fuel efficiency, maintenance, parts availability, residual value, and total cost of ownership.

The analysis applies to 6×4 tractor configurations in the 480 hp class. Where the comparison spans configuration variants (530 hp X6 vs 530 hp XF), the higher-power ratings are noted. The goal is to give fleet procurement managers a structured framework for selecting between the two platforms based on commercial priorities.

Powertrain Engineering

The SAGMOTO X6 6×4 tractor is powered by the Weichai WP13.480E32 inline-six diesel engine displacing 12.54 litres. The engine delivers 480 hp at 1,900 rpm and 2,350 Nm of peak torque from 1,200 to 1,500 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 2,000 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger. The engine is Euro II compliant for export markets, with Euro V optional for selected regions.

The DAF XF 480 6×4 tractor is powered by the PACCAR MX-13 480 inline-six diesel engine displacing 12.9 litres. The engine delivers 480 hp at 1,600 rpm and 2,600 Nm of peak torque from 1,000 to 1,400 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 2,500 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger with twin intercoolers. The engine is Euro VI compliant as standard for European operations, with Euro II versions for export markets.

The two engines produce identical peak power but differ in torque profile: the PACCAR MX-13 delivers 250 Nm more torque (2,600 Nm vs 2,350 Nm) at lower engine speed. This gives the DAF XF a substantial low-end pulling advantage, particularly noticeable on mountain passes and during launch from standstill with 60-tonne gross train weight. The Weichai WP13.480E32 has a higher torque curve width (300 rpm flat region) compared to the PACCAR MX-13 (400 rpm flat region), but at higher absolute engine speed.

The X6 transmission is the FAST 16JSD200E-B with 16 forward gears and overdrive top gear. The clutch is a single-plate dry type with hydraulic actuation at 430 mm diameter. The DAF XF uses the ZF TraXon 12-speed automated manual transmission with intelligent shift logic, or the manual ZF 16S 2230 TD as standard in export markets. The TraXon automated unit delivers smoother shift quality and reduced driver fatigue, although the manual unit remains the preferred choice in many emerging markets.

Engineering Insight: The PACCAR MX-13 delivers 250 Nm more peak torque than the Weichai WP13.480E32, giving the DAF XF 10-15 percent better low-end pulling power on mountain passes and during launch. For heavy-haul operations exceeding 80 tonnes gross train weight, this torque advantage is significant.

Driveline and Axle Configuration

The SAGMOTO X6 6×4 tractor uses SAGMOTO-designed hub-reduction rear axles rated at 16 tonnes each, with a 5.263 final drive ratio. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 7 tonnes with parabolic leaf springs. The frame is a straight-ladder design constructed from 8 mm + 8 mm double-layer high-strength steel with section height 300 mm.

The DAF XF 480 6×4 tractor uses DAF hypoid rear axles rated at 13 tonnes each (with optional 16-tonne axles for severe duty), with final drive ratios from 3.31 to 4.11 depending on specification. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 7.5 tonnes with parabolic leaf springs. The frame is a ladder-frame design constructed from high-strength steel with section height 285 mm.

The X6 axle rating at 16 tonnes per tandem (32 tonnes combined) is higher than the standard DAF hypoid axles at 13 tonnes per tandem (26 tonnes combined). For severe-duty African mining, Middle Eastern quarry, and Central Asian infrastructure applications, this axle advantage is meaningful. The DAF axles, however, deliver smoother operation and longer service life under sustained highway conditions.

Cab and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO X6 is equipped with the H78L flat-floor cab as standard, with full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, and a refrigerator compartment in premium trim. Cab interior dimensions are 2,200 mm wide, 2,100 mm tall from floor to headliner. The driver's seat is air-suspended with integrated headrest and lumbar support. The passenger seat is mechanical-suspension with fold-down design.

The DAF XF is equipped with the XF cab (medium length) or the XF Luxury Cab (long length with flat floor) depending on specification. The XF cab interior dimensions are 2,280 mm wide, 1,950 mm tall from floor to headliner. The XF Luxury Cab interior dimensions are 2,280 mm wide, 2,200 mm tall with flat floor. Both cabs feature full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, and refrigerator compartment. Driver seat is air-suspended with Grammer or ISRI premium design.

The DAF XF Luxury Cab delivers best-in-class NVH (noise, vibration, harshness) levels at cruise speed, with in-cab noise measured at 67 dBA versus 72 dBA for the X6. The XF Luxury Cab offers 100 mm more interior height than the X6 H78L, providing better stand-up comfort for drivers taking rest breaks. Both cabs meet European crash safety standards (ECE R29). The X6 cab offers competitive ergonomics, while the DAF cab delivers premium fit and finish.

Fuel Efficiency and Operating Economics

Fuel efficiency is a defining metric for premium tractor comparison. The SAGMOTO X6 with Weichai WP13.480E32 returns 30 to 36 litres per 100 kilometres at 80 km/h cruise speed with 60-tonne gross train weight in African and Middle Eastern long-haul operation. The DAF XF 480 with PACCAR MX-13 returns 27 to 33 litres per 100 kilometres under comparable conditions.

The DAF advantage is approximately 7-9 percent in fuel efficiency, driven by the more advanced engine calibration, the optimised driveline, the lower in-cab noise enabling smoother driving patterns, and the wider torque curve enabling earlier upshifts. For a fleet operating 100,000 kilometres per truck annually, this translates to USD 2,500-4,000 in annual fuel savings per truck.

Scheduled maintenance costs differ substantially. The X6 annual maintenance budget at 100,000 km is approximately USD 4,500-5,500, while the DAF XF annual maintenance budget at 100,000 km is approximately USD 9,000-11,000. Over a 5-year operating cycle, the X6 delivers USD 22,500-32,500 in maintenance savings. Tyre wear is comparable between the two trucks when fitted with equivalent tyres.

Resale Value and Total Cost of Ownership

The DAF XF retains approximately 40-44 percent of its acquisition value after 5 years or 800,000 km in well-maintained fleet operation. The SAGMOTO X6 retains approximately 28-34 percent of its acquisition value under comparable conditions. The DAF advantage is 10-12 percentage points, reflecting the strong brand prestige and the well-developed European residual value market for DAF trucks.

Despite the lower resale value, the X6 delivers a substantially lower five-year TCO. The acquisition cost saving (USD 55,000-70,000 per truck) outweighs the resale disadvantage (USD 10,000-15,000) plus the higher fuel cost (USD 10,000-20,000 over 5 years). The net five-year TCO advantage for the X6 is USD 35,000-55,000 per truck versus the DAF XF 480.

For a 50-truck fleet, this represents USD 1.75 to 2.75 million in five-year TCO advantage for the X6 over the DAF XF. This is meaningful capital that can be redeployed into additional fleet capacity, expanded service infrastructure, or accelerated fleet replacement.

TCO Insight: Despite the DAF XF delivering 7-9 percent better fuel efficiency and 10-12 percentage points higher resale value, the SAGMOTO X6 saves USD 35,000-55,000 per truck in five-year TCO because of the USD 55,000-70,000 acquisition cost advantage and USD 22,500-32,500 maintenance savings. For 50-truck fleets, this is USD 1.75-2.75 million in savings.

Parts Availability and Service Network

The DAF XF is supported by PACCAR's authorised service network with workshops in most major African and Middle Eastern markets. PACCAR operates authorised facilities in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, and Muscat, complemented by a wider network of authorised independent workshops. Parts availability is generally good with 95 percent parts fill rate within 48 hours for major wear parts.

The SAGMOTO X6 is supported by the SAGMOTO and Shacman authorised service network, which has expanded significantly across Africa and the Middle East since 2018. Authorised workshops are in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Dar es Salaam, Addis Ababa, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, and Muscat, complemented by the Shacman facility footprint. Parts fill rate is approximately 90 percent for major wear parts within 72 hours.

The DAF service network delivers slightly faster parts turnaround and more consistent service quality across regions. However, the Shacman network is more extensive in lower-tier African markets (Tanzania, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe) where the X6 is often better supported than the DAF XF.

Verdict by Application

For premium long-haul fleet operations in the Middle East and North Africa with established DAF service networks, the DAF XF remains the preferred choice for buyers prioritising brand prestige, residual value, and superior NVH. The 7-9 percent fuel efficiency advantage and 10-12 percentage point resale advantage are material for high-utilisation fleets.

For emerging market fleet operations in Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, and Latin America, the SAGMOTO X6 is the more commercially attractive choice. The USD 55,000-70,000 acquisition cost advantage, the USD 22,500-32,500 maintenance savings, and the strong service network in lower-tier markets deliver substantial TCO advantage. The X6 is particularly well-suited to severe-duty African mining, Middle Eastern quarry, and Central Asian infrastructure applications where the higher axle rating is valuable.

For fleet operators evaluating both platforms, the recommended approach is to acquire a 5-truck pilot of each platform and run them through a 12-month comparative evaluation. This provides real-world data on fuel efficiency, maintenance, downtime, and driver acceptance that complements the TCO modelling. Conclusion

The SAGMOTO X6 and the DAF XF 480 occupy similar positions in the heavy-duty 480 hp tractor segment but deliver very different commercial outcomes. The DAF XF offers premium European engineering, superior NVH, better fuel efficiency, and higher resale value. The SAGMOTO X6 offers substantially lower acquisition cost, lower maintenance cost, broader service availability in emerging markets, and higher axle rating for severe-duty applications. For African, Middle Eastern, and Central Asian fleet buyers prioritising TCO and total addressable value, the X6 represents a compelling alternative that has reshaped the segment economics since 2022.
