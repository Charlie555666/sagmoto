i5 Electric Light Truck Buying Guide for Mozambique 2026

2026-11-06Buying Guide8 min read

For Mozambican distributors moving parcels, beverages and spare parts inside Maputo and Matola, the battery-electric SAGMOTO i5 removes the diesel line item entirely while keeping a 120-160 kW permanent-magnet drivetrain that handles urban grades without strain. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd., based in Xi'an, supplies the i5 as a turnkey electric light truck and supports African fleets with charging-design advice rather than simply selling metal. This guide covers the practical import pathway into Mozambique, the charging reality around Maputo, how to match battery size to duty cycle, and the total-cost case that makes an electric light truck pay back against a diesel equivalent on short, predictable routes.

Why the i5 electric light truck fits Mozambique

Mozambique's freight inside its southern corridor is short-haul by nature. A delivery loop from a Maputo cross-dock to the suburbs of Matola, Boane and the Maputo Port logistics zone rarely exceeds 120 km in a working day, and that is exactly the envelope where a 100-140 kWh battery shines. The i5 carries a permanent-magnet electric motor rated between 120 kW and 160 kW peak, delivering instant torque from zero rpm that suits stop-start city driving better than a comparable combustion engine. Because Mozambique generates most of its grid power from Cahora Bassa hydro through Electricidade de Moçambique (EDM), a depot that charges on off-peak night tariff runs the truck on genuinely low-carbon, low-cost electricity. The absence of a diesel particulate filter, exhaust after-treatment and a multi-speed gearbox also removes the most common breakdowns seen on combustion light trucks in hot, dusty African conditions.

Key takeaway: the i5 is a city-and-suburb vehicle, not a long-haul EV. Keep daily operation under 180 km and you finish each shift with 10-20 percent reserve, avoiding the deep discharges that shorten battery life.

Maputo charging infrastructure planning

Charging is the part of an electric truck project that fails most often when treated as an afterthought. A single i5 with a 140 kWh pack needs roughly 140 kWh of delivered energy nightly if it runs close to empty, which at a 22 kW three-phase AC wallbox takes about seven hours, or at a 60 kW DC charger takes just over two hours. Most Maputo depots already have three-phase supply because they run compressors and cold rooms, so the realistic constraint is not grid connection but the breaker panel and the charger mounting. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading recommends installing two charging points per vehicle in a multi-unit fleet so that one fault does not ground the whole shift. For operators with roof space, a 15-30 kW solar array with a simple inverter offsets daytime top-ups and hedges against EDM tariff increases, though solar alone will not fast-charge a depleted pack overnight. Cable management and a weatherproof enclosure rated for the Maputo coastal humidity are worth specifying up front, because a corroded connector is the most common cause of a charger dropping out mid-cycle and leaving a truck short of range the next morning.

Battery, range and duty-cycle matching

The i5 is offered with a 100 kWh or 140 kWh lithium pack. The smaller pack suits fleets running under 130 km per day with light loads, while the 140 kWh pack gives headroom for air-conditioning load in Maputo's hot season and the occasional 170 km intercity run to Xinavane or Magude. Real-world range sits near 200 km on the 140 kWh unit with a 2-3 tonne payload and moderate air-con use, falling to about 150 km in sustained 35°C heat with maximum cooling. Battery thermal management keeps cell temperature in the safe band during fast charging, which protects warranty. Operators should log the first month of routes with a simple odometer and state-of-charge notebook before committing to pack size, because over-specifying the battery wastes capital while under-specifying it forces midday charging that disrupts the delivery schedule. Regenerative braking on the i5 recovers roughly 15-20 percent of the energy on the frequent stop-go loops around the port logistics zone, and that recovery is what lets a modest 140 kWh pack cover a full urban shift without a lunchtime plug-in.

Recommended i5 configurations for Mozambican fleets

A box-body i5 for parcel distribution and a flatbed i5 for beverage cages are the two most common Mozambique builds. Both share the 160 kW motor and the 140 kWh pack as the sensible default, with a 4.5-tonne GVW that sits inside light-vehicle licensing and avoids the heavier commercial regime. The cab is left-hand drive to match Mozambique road law, and the low centre of gravity from the floor-mounted battery actually improves stability on the uneven ring-road surfaces around the port. A reinforced suspension variant is available for the beverage trade where cage loads concentrate weight over the rear axle, and the standard regen calibration can be softened for new drivers who are unused to one-pedal driving. Fleets wanting a heavier electric option can review the SAGMOTO new energy electric trucks range, which includes the larger i9 for medium-duty work, while operators who also run combustion cargo vans should compare against the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake catalogue before deciding the split of their order.

Payment, shipping and containerised delivery

Because an electric truck carries a high-value battery, containerised shipping to Maputo is strongly preferred over Roll-on/Roll-off: the sealed box protects the pack from salt spray on the 30-day voyage and lets the importer declare the vehicle and charger together under one bill of lading. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading settles on a 30 percent advance with balance against documents, or a confirmed letter of credit at sight. The charger and any spare modules travel in the same container, which also reduces the chance of a mismatched connector standard arriving separately and stalling commissioning. Plan for a two to three week equipment inspection on arrival, because Mozambique customs may request a physical check of the battery documentation to confirm it meets transport-safety rules for lithium energy storage.

After-sales, battery warranty and technician training

The i5 powertrain is simpler than a diesel, but it is unfamiliar to many Mozambican workshops, so technician training is non-negotiable. The standard battery warranty runs 5 years or 200,000 km, whichever comes first, with the motor and controller covered for 3 years. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading runs a remote diagnostic session over WhatsApp at +86 15319431311 where a fault code screenshot is turned around into a fix the same day, and supplies a starter kit of contactors, fuses and coolant pump seals. Because there is no engine oil, the recurring service list shrinks to tyres, brakes, coolant and cabin filters, which a depot can handle with one trained mechanic and a quarterly video check-in with the exporter's service desk.

Total cost of ownership versus diesel

On a 35,000 km per year duty cycle, a diesel light truck in Mozambique burns roughly 3,500-4,200 litres of diesel annually at a cost that moves with the import-parity fuel price, while the i5 consumes about 0.55-0.7 kWh per km, or roughly 21,000-24,500 kWh per year. At an EDM off-peak rate that energy bill is typically 55-65 percent lower than the diesel equivalent, and the removed service items add another 30-40 percent saving on maintenance. Against the roughly USD 12,300 import burden and a purchase price a few thousand above a comparable diesel unit, the i5 normally reaches cost parity in 26 to 40 months of daily urban work, after which the operating saving is pure margin. For fleets with predictable short loops and a depot with three-phase power, the electric case is strong; for operators running irregular 300 km-plus intercity legs, a diesel or the larger i9 remains the better tool.
