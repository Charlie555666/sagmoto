How to Verify an Authorized Shacman (SAGMOTO) Truck Exporter: 7 Steps + 6 Red Flags

By Shaanxi Fenghan Trading — Authorized SAGMOTO Exporter Updated: September 22, 2026 10 min read

Chinese heavy trucks — Shacman (SAGMOTO) above all — have never offered better value. But the export boom has also attracted a wave of middlemen, shell companies and outright fraudsters who sell "new Shacman trucks" they do not own, with warranties that do not exist. For an overseas fleet buyer sending a five-figure deposit to a company 8,000 km away, verification is not optional. It is the deal.

This guide gives you the exact verification process we recommend to every buyer — the same one we pass ourselves as Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd., an authorized SAGMOTO exporter in Xi'an. Seven steps to confirm a supplier is legitimate, and six red flags that should end the conversation immediately.

Part 1: The 7 Verification Steps

Step 1 — Ask for the Export Authorization Certificate

Shaanxi Automobile Group issues export authorization certificates to its approved dealers. This is the single most important document in the verification process. A genuine authorized exporter will send you a scan within minutes — it names the company, the authorization scope and the validity period. If a supplier hesitates, delays, or sends a blurred image, treat it as a failed verification.

Step 2 — Match the Company Registration

Every Chinese company has a Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码). Ask for the business license (营业执照) and check that the company name on the license matches exactly: the name on the authorization certificate, the name on the proforma invoice, and the name on the bank account. Any mismatch — a different company collecting payment, for example — is a serious warning sign. Fenghan Trading's registered entity, for reference, is Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. (陕西风瀚贸易有限公司), registered in Xi'an, Shaanxi.

Step 3 — Verify the Factory Ordering Relationship

Authorized exporters place orders directly in the factory ordering system. Ask the supplier: "After I pay the deposit, what is the factory order number, and when does production start?" A legitimate exporter can give you a production schedule within days of the order — chassis production typically starts 7-15 days after deposit, with delivery 15-45 days depending on configuration. A trader who cannot show a factory order reference is buying from someone else's allocation.

Step 4 — Demand a Fully Itemized Specification

Before any deposit, the proforma invoice must list: exact engine model (e.g. Cummins ISME420, Yuchai YC6MK400, Weichai WP10.380E22), gearbox model (e.g. Fast 12JSD200T), axle ratio, emission standard (Euro II/III/V), tire specification, cab type, and every optional item with its individual price. "Standard configuration" is not a specification. Configuration errors are the most expensive mistake in truck importing — an authorized exporter eliminates them because your specification goes directly into the factory system.

Step 5 — Check Delivery References in Your Region

Ask for two or three recent delivery cases to your country or region: the destination port, approximate date, and the model delivered. Then test the supplier's knowledge: a supplier who has really delivered to Lagos knows about SONCAP; one who has delivered to Mombasa knows KEBS requirements; one who has shipped to Callao knows the RoRo schedule. Generic answers reveal generic experience. Fenghan Trading has documented deliveries across 50+ countries — Nigeria, Ghana, Tanzania, Kenya, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, the Philippines, Peru, the Dominican Republic and more — and can reference the specific documentation requirements of each.

Step 6 — Test the After-Sales Channel Before You Pay

Send a technical question through the supplier's WhatsApp or email: something specific, like "What is the oil capacity of the 12JSD200T gearbox?" or "Can you supply the clutch kit for the ISME420 engine?" Measure two things: response time and answer quality. A reliable supplier answers technical questions within hours, accurately — because they will be your parts and support channel for the next ten years. Step 7 — Visit, or Verify by Video

For orders of five units or more, visit China. A legitimate exporter will arrange your visit to both their office and the factory — Fenghan Trading regularly hosts buyers at its Xi'an office in the Chanba Free Trade Zone and arranges production-line inspections at the Shaanxi Automobile base. If a visit is not practical, request a live video call from the supplier's office and warehouse, and a video walk-around of your actual vehicles with their chassis numbers before shipment.

Part 2: The 6 Red Flags

Red FlagWhat It Usually Means
1. Price 30-50% below marketThe truck is used, refurbished, a stock unit, or does not exist. New Shacman trucks have a factory gate price — nobody sells below it.
2. No chassis number before depositThe seller does not have the vehicle. Legitimate suppliers share chassis numbers and production schedules freely.
3. Payment to a personal accountClassic fraud pattern. Payment must go to the registered company account matching the business license.
4. Stock photos onlyThe "supplier" is a website with copied photos. Demand chassis-specific photos and videos with a dated handwritten note.
5. 100% advance payment demandStandard terms in the industry are 30% deposit + 70% against B/L copy, or L/C at sight. Anyone demanding full prepayment is either inexperienced or dishonest.
6. No physical address in ChinaShell company. Verify the office address exists — authorized SAGMOTO exporters are concentrated in Xi'an, next to the factory.
If you see two or more red flags, stop. No discount justifies the risk of a USD 40,000 wire transfer to an unverified company. There are enough verified, authorized suppliers in the market — choose one of them.

What Factory Authorization Actually Buys You

Buyers sometimes ask whether authorization is worth the (usually small) price difference versus a trader. The answer is in what authorization delivers:

  • 100% new vehicles — current production, zero storage degradation, full service life ahead.
  • Configuration accuracy — your spec goes into the factory ordering system; the truck is built to it, not approximated to it.
  • Factory warranty — typically 12 months / 60,000-100,000 km on the powertrain, honored through the OEM channel.
  • Genuine parts supply — authorized exporters stock and ship OEM parts; traders cannot guarantee part authenticity.
  • Recourse — an authorized dealer answers to the factory. A trader answers to nobody.
Bottom line: Verification takes 15 minutes of asking for documents. Skipping it can cost the entire truck. Verify first, negotiate second.
