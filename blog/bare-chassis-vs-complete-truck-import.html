Should You Import a Bare Chassis or a Complete Truck?

The first question every overseas distributor asks when planning a SAGMOTO order is whether to land a cab-chassis and fit the body locally, or to take a complete built-up truck straight from the factory. There is no single right answer, because the better choice depends on your import tariff schedule, the strength of your local bodybuilding industry, your tolerance for split warranties and how quickly you need the unit working. A cab-chassis is the bare driveline, cab and frame with no superstructure, which lets a local workshop mount exactly the body your market needs. A complete truck arrives ready to work, with the body factory-fitted and inspected as one unit. How Does Import Duty Differ Between Chassis and Complete Vehicles?

In most markets the customs authority taxes a complete vehicle at a higher rate than a bare or cab-chassis, because a finished truck is treated as a consumer good while a chassis is treated as an industrial component. This single fact drives many buyer decisions. The table below shows the typical spread a distributor should model before choosing, using representative bands rather than any one country's schedule.

The duty gap can erase the apparent savings of a cheap local body if your market taxes chassis almost as hard as complete units. Buyers should obtain a written tariff ruling from their customs broker before committing, because the planning mistake of assuming a low chassis rate has sunk many otherwise sound imports. What Shipping Method Fits Each Option?

Shipping method follows directly from the form you choose. A cab-chassis, with its exposed frame and no fragile body, travels well inside a standard container or on a flat rack, which protects it from weather and port handling. A complete truck is often shipped Roll-on Roll-off, driven on and off the vessel, which is the cheapest complete-vehicle method but exposes the unit to salt spray. The table compares the common methods and their approximate cost ranges so a buyer can build a landed-cost model.

RoRo looks attractive on price, but a complete truck shipped this way arrives with a salt film that must be washed and undercoated immediately, and any transit damage is harder to prove than in a sealed container. Cab-chassis buyers generally prefer the container route precisely because the bare frame survives the trip without cosmetic claims. The SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake range is frequently ordered as complete units for markets with weak bodybuilding capacity, which avoids the container-versus-RoRo debate entirely.

Model the duty gap and the shipping method together, not separately, because a low chassis duty can be cancelled by a premium flat-rack rate, and a cheap RoRo rate can be cancelled by a high complete-vehicle tariff.

When Does Local Bodybuilder Capability Decide the Choice?

The deciding factor is rarely the truck itself and almost always the quality of your local body shop. If your market has certified bodybuilders familiar with SAGMOTO frame dimensions, PTO standards and local weight codes, a cab-chassis is usually the smarter buy: you pay less duty, you get a body tuned to local needs, and you support local industry. If no such shop exists, or if the body is safety-critical like a tanker or aerial platform, a complete factory-built truck removes the risk of a poor local mount. Distributors should visit the proposed bodybuilder, review past SAGMOTO or similar conversions, and confirm they can obtain the chassis drawing pack and the correct PTO specification from the supplier before signing. A weak bodybuilder turns a cheap chassis into an expensive, delayed and possibly unsafe vehicle.

Why Does Warranty and Lead Time Favor One Option?

A complete truck gives a single warranty and a single point of accountability: if the body or chassis fails, you claim against one supplier. A cab-chassis splits responsibility between the truck maker and the bodybuilder, which can slow claims but also lets you choose a specialist body warranty. Lead time tells the opposite story. A cab-chassis ships faster because there is no body to build, while a complete unit adds body mounting, painting and factory inspection to the schedule. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading quotes typical lead times of 30 to 60 days for a cab-chassis and 45 to 90 days for a complete built truck, and the complete-unit window grows further if the body is complex or custom-painted. Buyers balancing urgency against simplicity should weigh these two factors against each other rather than treating either as decisive on its own.

Choose the complete truck when accountability matters more than speed, and choose the cab-chassis when a fast, duty-light landing and a local body solution matter more than a single warranty claim path.

Conclusion

Importing a SAGMOTO from China as a cab-chassis or as a complete truck is a genuine buyer decision with no universal answer, and the right call depends on your local duty schedule, your bodybuilder's capability, your warranty preference and your deadline. Distributors who plan the whole chain from factory to final registration, rather than optimising only the purchase line, consistently land vehicles that earn from day one and avoid the costly surprises that a narrow view creates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a cab-chassis cheaper to import than a complete truck?

Yes, a cab-chassis is usually cheaper to import because many markets tax complete vehicles 10 to 25 percent while chassis fall in a 5 to 15 percent band, and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading quotes the SAGMOTO X5 or E3 MAX cab-chassis ex works with ocean freight of about 1,500 to 3,000 USD by container. The saving only holds if your local bodybuilder can mount the body for less than the factory body premium plus the extra duty, so buyers should price both routes. Where the chassis duty gap is small, the complete truck often wins on total cost once bodybuilding and inspection are included.

Can I mount any body on a SAGMOTO cab-chassis?

No, you cannot mount just any body, because the SAGMOTO X7 and i5 cab-chassis require a bodybuilder certified to the local weight, electrical and PTO standards, and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading supplies the chassis drawing pack so the converter stays within axle and frame ratings. A body that overloads the front axle or misuses the PTO will fail inspection and void support, so the bodybuilder must confirm compatibility before cutting steel. Choosing a certified converter protects both the vehicle and your warranty position.

Do complete trucks include warranty on the body?

Yes, a complete SAGMOTO truck from Shaanxi Fenghan Trading carries a chassis warranty of typically 12 to 24 months and a factory-fitted body covered under the same claim path, which gives one point of accountability. By contrast a cab-chassis with a local body splits responsibility, with the bodybuilder holding a separate body warranty of usually 6 to 12 months. Buyers who want a single claim contact should choose the complete truck, while those comfortable managing two suppliers can save on duty with the chassis route.

Are RoRo shipments safe for complete trucks?

Yes, Roll-on Roll-off is safe for complete trucks and costs about 1,200 to 2,500 USD versus 2,000 to 4,500 USD for a flat-rack container, but cab-chassis often ship inside a standard container to protect the exposed frame from salt spray. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading advises the method by destination port and will arrange wash and undercoat notes for RoRo arrivals. The main RoRo risk is transit cosmetic damage from weather, which is why many buyers still prefer the sealed container for valuable or painted units.
