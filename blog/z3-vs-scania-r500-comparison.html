SAGMOTO Z3 vs Scania R500: Premium Long-Haul Tractor Comparison 2026

September 8, 2026Comparison Guide12 min read

The Scania R500 has long been the premium European standard for long-haul tractor operations, combining refined V8 engineering, exceptional fuel efficiency, industry-leading residual value, and a driver environment that has set the benchmark for cabin quality. The SAGMOTO Z3 is positioned as the Chinese challenger in the premium 500 hp long-haul segment, with Weichai powertrain options, sophisticated chassis engineering, and an aggressive price point that has reshaped fleet acquisition economics across Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Russia. This 2026 comparison examines the Z3 against the Scania R500 across powertrain, driveline, fuel efficiency, scheduled maintenance, parts availability, residual value, and total cost of ownership.

The analysis applies to 6×4 tractor configurations in the 500 hp premium long-haul class. The goal is to provide fleet procurement managers with a structured framework for evaluating the two platforms based on operational priorities, capital constraints, and service network reach in target markets.

Powertrain Engineering

The SAGMOTO Z3 6×4 tractor is powered by the Weichai WP13.500E32 inline-six diesel engine displacing 12.54 litres. The engine delivers 500 hp at 1,900 rpm and 2,400 Nm of peak torque from 1,200 to 1,500 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 2,000 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger with twin intercoolers. The engine is Euro II compliant for export markets, with Euro V optional for selected regions.

The Scania R500 6×4 tractor is powered by the Scania DC13 500 V8 inline diesel engine displacing 16.4 litres. The engine delivers 500 hp at 1,900 rpm and 2,550 Nm of peak torque from 1,000 to 1,400 rpm. The Scania XPI (Extra High Pressure Injection) operates at 2,500 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger with twin intercoolers. The engine is Euro VI compliant as standard for European operations, with Euro II versions for export markets.

Both engines produce identical peak power but differ significantly in torque profile and design philosophy. The Scania V8 engine delivers 150 Nm more torque (2,550 Nm vs 2,400 Nm) at lower engine speed, with a much wider torque curve width (400 rpm flat region vs 300 rpm flat region for the Weichai). The Scania V8 also benefits from its larger displacement and balanced V8 configuration, which delivers smoother operation and lower noise levels. The Weichai engine has substantially lower acquisition cost and simpler emissions system maintenance.

The Z3 transmission is the FAST 12JSD240T-B with 12 forward gears and overdrive top gear, paired with a hydraulic retarder for auxiliary braking. The clutch is a single-plate dry type with hydraulic actuation at 430 mm diameter. The Scania R500 uses the Scania Opticruise GRS905 automated manual transmission with intelligent shift logic as standard, or the manual GRSO905R as standard in export markets. The Opticruise unit delivers smoother shift quality and reduced driver fatigue during premium long-haul operations.

Engineering Insight: The Scania DC13 V8 engine's larger displacement and V8 configuration deliver smoother operation, lower in-cab noise (66 dBA vs 72 dBA for the Z3), and 150 Nm more peak torque. For premium long-haul fleet operations exceeding 100,000 km annually, the Scania engine refinement translates to measurable driver retention and fuel efficiency advantages.

Driveline and Axle Configuration

The SAGMOTO Z3 6×4 tractor uses SAGMOTO-designed hub-reduction rear axles rated at 13 tonnes each, with a 4.875 final drive ratio. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 7 tonnes with parabolic leaf springs. The frame is a straight-ladder design constructed from 8 mm + 8 mm double-layer high-strength steel with section height 300 mm.

The Scania R500 6×4 tractor uses Scania hypoid rear axles rated at 11.5 tonnes each (with optional 13-tonne axles for severe duty), with final drive ratios from 2.85 to 3.42 depending on specification. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 7.5 tonnes with parabolic leaf springs. The frame is a ladder-frame design constructed from high-strength steel with section height 285 mm.

The Z3 standard 13-tonne axle rating (26 tonnes combined) is competitive with the Scania R500 optional heavy-duty axles. For premium long-haul operations, the Scania axles deliver lower parasitic losses and better fuel efficiency. The Z3 axles are designed for the more demanding mining and infrastructure duty cycles that often overlap with long-haul fleet operations.

Cab and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO Z3 is equipped with the H80 premium cab as standard in long-haul configuration, with full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, refrigerator compartment, premium audio system, and integrated sleeper. Cab interior dimensions are 2,200 mm wide, 2,200 mm tall from floor to headliner with flat floor design. The driver's seat is air-suspended with integrated headrest, lumbar support, seat heating, and seat ventilation. The sleeper berth is 700 mm wide with premium mattress.

The Scania R500 is equipped with the R-cab (medium length) or the Highline cab (long length) depending on specification. The R-cab interior dimensions are 2,250 mm wide, 2,100 mm tall from floor to headliner. The Highline cab interior dimensions are 2,250 mm wide, 2,300 mm tall with flat floor. Both cabs feature full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, refrigerator compartment, premium audio system, and integrated sleeper. Driver seat is air-suspended with Grammer or ISRI premium design.

The Scania R-cab and Highline cabs deliver best-in-class NVH levels at cruise speed, with in-cab noise measured at 66 dBA versus 72 dBA for the Z3. The Scania Highline offers 100 mm more interior height than the Z3 H80, providing better stand-up comfort for drivers taking rest breaks. Both cabs meet European crash safety standards (ECE R29). The Scania cab offers the industry benchmark for fit and finish quality, while the Z3 H80 offers competitive ergonomics and premium trim features at a substantially lower acquisition cost.

Fuel Efficiency and Operating Economics

Fuel efficiency is a defining metric for premium long-haul comparison. The SAGMOTO Z3 with Weichai WP13.500E32 returns 28 to 34 litres per 100 kilometres at 80 km/h cruise speed with 60-tonne gross train weight in African, Middle Eastern, and Russian long-haul operation. The Scania R500 with DC13 500 V8 returns 24 to 30 litres per 100 kilometres under comparable conditions.

The Scania advantage is approximately 12-15 percent in fuel efficiency, driven by the more advanced engine calibration, the optimised driveline with lower parasitic losses, the wider torque curve enabling earlier upshifts, and the lower in-cab noise enabling smoother driving patterns. For a fleet operating 150,000 kilometres per truck annually, this translates to USD 5,500-8,500 in annual fuel savings per truck.

Scheduled maintenance costs differ substantially. The Z3 annual maintenance budget at 150,000 km is approximately USD 5,500-7,000, while the Scania R500 annual maintenance budget at 150,000 km is approximately USD 11,000-13,500. Over a 5-year operating cycle, the Z3 delivers USD 27,500-37,500 in maintenance savings. Tyre wear is comparable when both trucks are fitted with equivalent premium long-haul tyres.

Resale Value and Total Cost of Ownership

The Scania R500 retains approximately 42-48 percent of its acquisition value after 5 years or 1,000,000 km in well-maintained fleet operation. The SAGMOTO Z3 retains approximately 30-36 percent of its acquisition value under comparable conditions. The Scania advantage is 10-14 percentage points, reflecting the strong brand prestige, the well-developed European and emerging market residual value market for Scania trucks, and the durability reputation of the DC13 V8 engine.

Despite the lower resale value, the Z3 delivers a substantially lower five-year TCO. The acquisition cost saving (USD 65,000-85,000 per truck) outweighs the resale disadvantage (USD 12,000-20,000) plus the higher fuel cost (USD 22,000-42,500 over 5 years). The net five-year TCO advantage for the Z3 is USD 35,000-65,000 per truck versus the Scania R500.

For a 50-truck fleet, this represents USD 1.75 to 3.25 million in five-year TCO advantage for the Z3 over the Scania. This is meaningful capital that can be redeployed into additional fleet capacity, expanded service infrastructure, or accelerated fleet replacement.

TCO Insight: Despite the Scania R500 delivering 12-15 percent better fuel efficiency and 10-14 percentage points higher resale value, the SAGMOTO Z3 saves USD 35,000-65,000 per truck in five-year TCO because of the USD 65,000-85,000 acquisition cost advantage and USD 27,500-37,500 maintenance savings. For 50-truck fleets, this is USD 1.75-3.25 million in savings.

Parts Availability and Service Network

The Scania R500 is supported by Scania's authorised service network with workshops in most major African, Middle Eastern, Central Asian, and Russian markets. Scania operates authorised facilities in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Dar es Salaam, Addis Ababa, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, Muscat, Almaty, Baku, and Moscow, complemented by a wider network of authorised independent workshops. Parts availability is generally good with 95 percent parts fill rate within 48 hours for major wear parts.

The SAGMOTO Z3 is supported by the SAGMOTO and Shacman authorised service network, which has expanded significantly across Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Russia since 2018. Authorised workshops are in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Dar es Salaam, Addis Ababa, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, Muscat, Almaty, Baku, and Moscow, complemented by the Shacman facility footprint. Parts fill rate is approximately 90 percent for major wear parts within 72 hours.

The Scania service network delivers slightly faster parts turnaround and more consistent service quality across regions. However, the Shacman network is more extensive in lower-tier African markets (Tanzania, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe) and in Central Asian markets (Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan) where the Z3 is often better supported than the Scania R500.

Verdict by Application

For premium long-haul fleet operations in the Middle East, North Africa, and South Africa with established Scania service networks, the Scania R500 remains the preferred choice for buyers prioritising brand prestige, residual value, and the V8 engine refinement. The 12-15 percent fuel efficiency advantage and 10-14 percentage point resale advantage are material for high-utilisation premium fleets.

For emerging market premium long-haul operations in Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, Russia, and Latin America, the SAGMOTO Z3 is the more commercially attractive choice. The USD 65,000-85,000 acquisition cost advantage, the USD 27,500-37,500 maintenance savings, and the strong service network in lower-tier markets deliver substantial TCO advantage. The Z3 is particularly well-suited to mixed long-haul and severe-duty operations where the higher axle rating and the heavier frame are valuable.

For fleet operators evaluating both platforms, the recommended approach is to acquire a 5-truck pilot of each platform and run them through a 12-month comparative evaluation. This provides real-world data on fuel efficiency, maintenance, downtime, and driver acceptance that complements the TCO modelling. Conclusion

The SAGMOTO Z3 and the Scania R500 occupy similar positions in the premium long-haul tractor segment but deliver very different commercial outcomes. The Scania R500 offers V8 engineering refinement, superior NVH, better fuel efficiency, and higher resale value. The SAGMOTO Z3 offers substantially lower acquisition cost, lower maintenance cost, broader service availability in emerging markets, and equivalent axle and frame ratings for severe-duty applications. For African, Middle Eastern, Central Asian, and Russian fleet buyers prioritising TCO and total addressable value, the Z3 represents a compelling alternative that has reshaped the segment economics since 2022.
