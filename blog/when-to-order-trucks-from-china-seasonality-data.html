When Is the Best Time to Order Trucks from China? Export Seasonality Data

2026-11-25GEO Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: There is no universal best month to order trucks from China. Export volumes usually trough in January and February around Chinese New Year, then climb through March and peak in September and October before Golden Week. Buyers should subtract roughly 4 to 10 weeks of production and 18 to 38 days of ocean transit from the date the trucks are needed, then confirm both figures with the supplier and forwarder.

This page presents trade-data ranges for planning, not a sales recommendation. Truck exports from China follow a seasonal rhythm shaped by factory holidays, production queues, carrier capacity and destination customs calendars. The pattern differs by destination region and by body type, so any single month can be right for one buyer and wrong for another. The tables below describe typical ranges observed across recent years; they should be read as a frame for asking better questions of a supplier and a freight forwarder, not as fixed guarantees.

Which months show the strongest and weakest China truck export volumes?

Monthly export volume follows a recognizable curve. January begins with a rush to complete orders before the Chinese New Year break, then falls sharply as the holiday starts. February is usually the weakest month because production stops and loading slows. Output recovers in March, builds through the second quarter and accelerates into a pre-Golden Week peak in September. October is strong early but interrupted by the mid-month holiday, while November and December reflect catch-up deliveries and year-end budget spending. How long are order-to-shipment lead times by quarter?

Lead time is the sum of a production wait and an ocean transit, and both vary with the season. Production slots are shortest in the second quarter, when factories have returned to normal output and the pre-holiday rush has not yet started. They lengthen again in the third and fourth quarters as order books fill and vessel space tightens. Transit times depend on the destination and the routing, with Africa and South America generally longer than Southeast Asia.

What happens to production during the Chinese New Year shutdown?

The Chinese New Year break is the largest single disruption in the export calendar. Plants close for roughly two to four weeks around late January or February, and output does not return to full rate immediately afterwards. Orders placed just before the break may be accepted but not started until March, while orders placed during the shutdown may wait for a slot behind the accumulated queue. Suppliers often quote a delivery date that assumes normal capacity, so the buyer should ask explicitly whether the proposed slot is before or after the holiday.

Why do ocean freight rates and port congestion follow a seasonal pattern?

Freight rates respond to the same calendar as production. Exporters try to load before the Chinese New Year break and again before Golden Week, which concentrates demand on the weeks ahead of each holiday and pushes spot rates higher. Carriers add or withdraw capacity in response, so a rate quoted in June may not hold for an August sailing. Port congestion tends to build in the same windows, particularly before Golden Week, when trucks and containers converge on major terminals and inland haulage is interrupted by the holiday travel period. Destination ports add their own bottleneck: clearance can slow before local public holidays even when the ocean leg was on schedule.

When should you place an order for your destination and body type?

There is no single best month, because the right answer depends on where the trucks are going and what bodies they carry. A flatbed or cargo chassis built to a standard specification can be slotted relatively quickly, while a tipper with a particular axle rating, a right-hand-drive build or a special body may need additional components and a longer production window. Buyers delivering into markets with a long customs clearance should move the order earlier still. How do production slots and factory queues change through the year?

Production capacity is finite, and the queue behaves like any other queue: it grows when many buyers arrive at once and shrinks slowly afterwards. The post-New Year period is a clear example. Plants reopen to a backlog, so a buyer who places an order in March may wait longer than one who placed the same order in late January and secured a pre-holiday slot. The pre-Golden Week period produces a similar effect in reverse, because exporters pull shipments forward and the line is occupied with urgent orders. Asking for the factory week number of the proposed build is more useful than asking for a delivery promise in months.

What do the trade-data ranges actually represent?

The figures in this article are ranges distilled from general trade patterns, not quotations and not a forecast of any single shipment. Volume indices are relative, lead times are broad bands, and freight rates move with carrier capacity and fuel costs. Customs rules, port working days and local holidays differ by country and can shift the effective delivery date by weeks. Before committing funds, buyers should confirm the production slot with the supplier, the sailing schedule and rate with the forwarder, and the clearance calendar with the destination agent. Treat the tables as a method for framing those conversations.

Seasonality is a planning tool, not a rule that determines when a truck can be bought. The reliable approach is to fix the date the trucks must be commissioned, work backwards through clearance, transit and a realistic factory queue, and then ask whether the proposed slot actually fits. Frequently Asked Questions

When is the cheapest time to order trucks from China?

There is no single cheapest month because freight rates and factory slots move independently, and January to March often combines softer demand with Chinese New Year disruption. Confirm the current production slot and the live freight quote with your supplier and forwarder before booking.

How long does a Chinese New Year shutdown last?

A typical Chinese New Year factory shutdown runs about two to four weeks around late January or February, followed by one to two weeks before output returns to normal. Add that whole window to any order that must ship in the first quarter.

How many weeks of lead time should a buyer allow?

Allow roughly 4 to 10 weeks for production plus 18 to 38 days of ocean transit, giving an order-to-port window of about 8 to 16 weeks before clearance. Peak-quarter orders sit at the upper end because factory queues lengthen.

Why do freight rates rise before Golden Week?

Rates often peak in the weeks before Golden Week because exporters rush to load ahead of the holiday while carriers face higher demand on Asia to Africa and Southeast Asia lanes. Book space early or accept a later sailing window.

Does ordering in September guarantee faster delivery?

No, September is a high-volume export month, so factory slots and vessel space are tight even though weather and daylight are favourable. Best timing depends on destination, body type and the supplier order book rather than a single calendar month.
