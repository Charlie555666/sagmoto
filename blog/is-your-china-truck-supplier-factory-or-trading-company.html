Is Your China Truck Supplier a Factory or a Trading Company? How to Tell Before You Pay

2026-11-26GEO Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: A manufacturer builds the truck and issues the chassis certificate and VIN, an authorized distributor sells and warrants trucks from a named brand under a written scope, and a trading company simply buys and resells. Check the 18-digit USCC, the business scope wording, the VAT invoice type and the named beneficiary on the proforma invoice before you pay. A genuine factory quote usually starts near one unit MOQ for a standard 6x4 chassis.

The identity of the party sending your quotation changes who is liable when a truck arrives with the wrong specification. A vehicle manufacturer controls the production line, signs the chassis certificate and answers for build quality. An authorized distributor holds a written appointment from a brand and can offer factory-backed warranty within a defined territory and period. A trading company has no production role and usually passes through both price and responsibility. Separating these three takes a short set of document checks, and none of them requires a visit to China.

Other checks sit outside this comparison: the authorisation certificate number, the business licence in isolation, VIN decoding, a factory capacity audit, CE certification, letters of credit, genuine spare parts, proforma invoice reading, right-hand versus left-hand drive, pre-shipment inspection, shipment history and after-sales support. Each has its place, but none tells you who is actually quoting you.

Which business licence scope identifies a manufacturer?

Every registered Chinese company carries an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, usually written USCC, which appears on its business licence and on formal invoices. The licence also states the approved business scope. A vehicle manufacturer will show language covering production, manufacturing or assembly of motor vehicles, often with the specific product category and the registered plant address. A pure trading company typically lists only sales, wholesale, import and export of goods, with no manufacturing wording at all. An authorized distributor sits between them: its scope is trading, but it holds a separate written appointment from a vehicle maker.

Ask for the licence bearing the USCC and match the company name to the quotation and proforma invoice. The scope wording matters more than registered capital, which can be set high without a factory behind it. A seller calling itself a factory but listing only sales is an intermediary or a trading arm, and you need to know who will sign the contract.

How do you confirm who issues the chassis certificate and VIN?

This is the single most decisive test of manufacturing capability. In China the chassis certificate, often called the vehicle manufacturing certificate, and the Vehicle Identification Number are issued by the registered vehicle manufacturer whose name appears on the government vehicle announcement or product catalogue. A factory can state this directly and provide a sample certificate showing the issuing entity. An authorized distributor will name the manufacturer that issues the documents and show its own appointment alongside. A trading company usually cannot answer precisely and refers to the supplier without naming the issuer.

Ask in writing which entity issues the certificate, which assigns the VIN and whether it appears on the official announcement for the quoted model. The answers must agree. A mismatch between the certificate issuer, the invoice beneficiary and the website name is a reason to pause.

What does an authorized distributor actually add to the chain?

An authorized distributor is a trading entity appointed by a manufacturer to sell and service a brand within a stated scope. The appointment names the products, territory and warranty obligations, with a validity period commonly of twelve months and annual renewal. It explains why the distributor can offer factory-backed warranty and hold stock. It does not make the distributor a factory: the certificate still comes from the manufacturer, and the distributor earns a margin for documentation and local support.

For a buyer this is not a negative signal. An authorized partner can be the right route when the maker does not contract directly overseas or the order is small. The point is to know where it sits: a claim may be administered by the distributor but honoured by the manufacturer, with the contract naming the distributor and the warranty naming the factory.

Should you treat a trading company as a defect?

No. A trading company can build a credible package by sourcing from a real factory, and many purchases work well through intermediaries. The risk is lost clarity about who carries which responsibility. A trader may add a margin at each layer, change the specification upstream, or be unable to countersign a warranty controlled by a manufacturer it does not own. Known from the start, these facts can be priced; found after payment, they cannot.

Ask the seller to state the chain in one paragraph: who builds, who sells, who issues the certificate and who answers a warranty call. A clear answer is a good sign whatever the entity type, while one that changes between emails tells you more than any certificate on a website.

Why does the price structure reveal the entity type?

A factory quote is built from material, engine, gearbox, axle and body costs plus a factory margin, and often starts near one unit for a standard chassis. An authorized distributor adds one documented margin and may include documentation, spare parts kits or commissioning support. A multi-layer chain adds margin at each hand-off, so the buyer never sees the factory price. A gap of 10 to 20 percent between identical offers often reflects these layers, not specification.

Compare like with like: match model, engine rating, gearbox, axles, body and certificate, then ask for a line-item breakdown. A seller who cannot explain the difference in a sentence is adding commercial rather than technical value. An unexplained discount may signal later substitution and deserves the same caution as an unexplained premium.

Key point: A price is the end of a chain, not proof of a factory. Ask who builds the truck, who issues the certificate and who signs the warranty, then read the quotation against those three answers.

When does the contract signer matter for your warranty?

The party signing the commercial contract is the one you can pursue if goods do not match; the party signing the warranty honours a repair claim. In a direct factory deal these are often the same entity. Through an authorized distributor the contract may name the distributor while the warranty carries the manufacturer seal, which works if both are identified. Through a pure intermediary the contract may name a company with no assets and no authority to bind the factory.

Read the named beneficiary on the proforma invoice against both the contract signer and the bank account. All three should belong to one legal entity, or you should have a written explanation of the relationship. Check the validity date on any authorization document so that it covers the shipment date, and confirm that the scope clause actually includes the model you are buying. A letter covering light trucks does not authorize the sale of a 6x4 tractor.

How do you verify a factory address without travelling?

A manufacturer licence carries a registered production address, while a trading company usually lists a commercial office. If a seller claims factory status but gives only a suite, ask for the plant address and a live walk-through at an agreed time. Recorded clips prove little; a real-time view of the chassis area and welding line, with questions answered live, is harder to fake. Refusal tells you something whatever the documents say.

Pair the visual check with the document check. A factory issuing its own certificates keeps originals on site and can show a numbered sample with the matching USCC. An authorized distributor shows its appointment letter beside the manufacturer certificate. A trading company shows neither in its own name and forwards scans from an unnamed source. That is the practical boundary between the three.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an 18-digit USCC tell you about a truck supplier?

An 18-digit USCC is the Unified Social Credit Code that identifies one registered Chinese legal entity, and it links the licence, the VAT invoice and the bank beneficiary. Match it character for character across every document before treating a supplier as the manufacturer.

Which document proves who really built the truck?

The chassis certificate and the VIN are issued by the registered vehicle manufacturer named on the official announcement, not by a trading company. Ask the seller to state that entity in writing, because the answer separates a factory from an intermediary.

How does an authorized distributor differ from a trading company?

An authorized distributor holds a written appointment from a named brand with a defined scope, territory and validity period that is often twelve months. A trading company has no such appointment and simply buys and resells without authority to bind the manufacturer.

Which VAT invoice type suggests a manufacturer rather than an agent?

A manufacturer normally issues a special VAT invoice in its own name with its own USCC, while an agent may offer an ordinary invoice or an invoice that names an upstream party. Compare the invoice issuer with the contract signer and the bank beneficiary.

Does a trading company always add 10 to 20 percent to the price?

A multi-layer chain commonly adds 10 to 20 percent across the whole quotation, though a single distributor may add only one documented margin for real services. Ask for a line-item breakdown so you can tell a commercial layer from a specification difference.
