Which Countries Import the Most Chinese Truck Chassis? HS 870600 Export Data

2026-11-19GEO Buyer Decision10 min read

Quick answer: Customs-data estimates place Chinese HS 870600 chassis exports at roughly 120,000 to 135,000 units worth USD 2.6 to 2.9 billion in a recent full year, up about 12 percent year on year. Vietnam, Nigeria, the Philippines and Indonesia are typically the largest destinations, together taking close to 45 percent of volume. All figures are trade-data ranges, not official statistics; buyers should confirm current volumes with live customs data before committing.

HS 870600 covers motor vehicle chassis fitted with engines, for the transport of goods or passengers and for special purposes. It is the code under which cab-and-engine assemblies, bare chassis with a powertrain, and bases for buses, tippers, fire appliances and other special bodies are recorded in international trade. Because the code spans tractors, rigid trucks and special vehicles, it is a broad indicator of Chinese commercial-vehicle demand rather than a single product category. The tables below present customs-data estimates and trade-data ranges to show where that demand is concentrated and how it has moved.

Key point: HS 870600 is a chassis-and-engine code, not a finished-truck code. It includes bases for goods vehicles, buses and special-purpose bodies, so destination figures reflect chassis demand across several vehicle types rather than one model or one body style.

What does HS 870600 actually cover?

The heading describes chassis equipped with an engine, designed to receive a body or cab. In practice that includes tractor bases, rigid truck chassis, bus chassis and special-purpose chassis such as those used for cranes, tankers and fire appliances. It excludes bodies, trailers and most completely built vehicles, which sit under other headings. For a buyer, the practical meaning is that a shipment recorded under 870600 may be a tipper base, a tractor chassis or a bus frame, depending on the destination market's vehicle programme.

This breadth is both useful and limiting. It is useful because it captures the underlying chassis demand in a market before bodies are added locally, which is exactly what many African and Southeast Asian importers do. It is limiting because the code does not separate a 4x2 light chassis from a heavy 8x4 tipper base, so an average unit value must be interpreted with care. A shift in the mix toward heavier chassis can raise total value faster than volume, and a market that moves from CKD kits to locally built bodies can change the recorded number without any change in real demand.

Which countries import the most Chinese truck chassis?

Trade-data estimates consistently put Southeast Asian and African markets at the top of the HS 870600 destination list. Vietnam, the Philippines and Indonesia buy large numbers of chassis for local body building and for both rigid and tractor applications. Nigeria and Egypt lead African demand, driven by construction, aggregates and freight, while Russia and several Middle East markets take higher-value heavy chassis. Key point: The top ten destinations absorb roughly three quarters of recorded HS 870600 volume, and the top four alone account for close to 45 percent. No single market dominates the trade, which is why a change in one country's import rules rarely moves the global total by more than a few percent.

The value ranking differs from the volume ranking. Russia and several Middle East markets appear higher by value because they import heavier, higher-horsepower chassis with larger engines and more specification content. Vietnam and the Philippines appear higher by volume because they import a broader mix that includes lighter rigid chassis and CKD sets. A buyer reading these tables should separate the two: a market can be large by unit count but modest by value, or the reverse, and the average unit value is a rough proxy for the chassis mix rather than for price level alone.

How are exports split by region?

Regional shares show the same pattern in aggregate. Southeast Asia is the largest receiving region because of Vietnam, the Philippines and Indonesia, where local body building is well established and chassis imports are a normal supply channel. Africa follows, led by Nigeria and Egypt and supported by a long tail of smaller markets. The Middle East and Latin America are smaller but fast-growing in value, and the CIS and Central Asia are dominated by a few large, high-specification buyers.

What does the three-year trend show?

The three-year picture is one of volume growth with a relatively stable average unit value. Customs-data estimates put exports near 95,000 to 100,000 units three years ago, around 110,000 to 118,000 units in the following year, and roughly 120,000 to 135,000 units in the most recent full year. Value has risen broadly in step, from about USD 2.0 to 2.1 billion to USD 2.6 to 2.9 billion, implying an average chassis value in the USD 21,000 to 22,000 range with modest movement.

The steady average unit value is worth noting. If Chinese manufacturers were moving strongly upmarket within this code, the average would climb noticeably; if they were competing purely on price, it would fall. The near-flat figure suggests a stable mix, with growth coming from more units rather than from a dramatic change in what those units are. Regional events can still distort a single year: a large order for high-specification tractor chassis in one market can lift value without lifting volume, and a temporary import restriction can suppress one destination while others take its share.

Key point: Across the three-year window, volume grew by roughly 25 to 30 percent while average unit value stayed near USD 21,000 to 21,500. That points to more chassis being shipped rather than a wholesale shift to heavier or lighter specifications.

How should buyers read these figures?

Every number in this article is a customs-data estimate or a trade-data range assembled from publicly reported patterns, not an official single source. Customs classifications differ between countries, re-exports can be recorded in more than one destination, and a shipment may be declared under a neighbouring heading depending on how complete the chassis is. For that reason the tables describe an order of magnitude and a direction of travel, not a reconciled total. Buyers should confirm current volumes, values and shares with the latest customs data for the markets they care about before making a sourcing or registration decision.

The practical use of the data is to identify where supply and competition are concentrated. A buyer importing into a top-five destination should expect strong local competition and a mature body-building ecosystem, which affects price and parts availability. A buyer entering a smaller market may find fewer competitors but a thinner support network. In both cases the ranking is the starting point for asking better questions about specification, approvals and after-sales, not a decision in itself. Those who want to look one level deeper at how chassis reach specific end markets can consult the China dump truck export data by destination cluster article, which breaks out a related vehicle category.

Suppliers offering finished bodies on imported bases can be compared through the SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake range, which illustrates the type of body that a chassis under this heading commonly receives. Use the data to frame the question, then verify the specific truck, the approval regime and the current landed cost in the destination market. Trade figures explain where the market has been; they do not decide which chassis a particular fleet should buy.
