SAGMOTO X5 vs X6: Which Heavy Dump Truck Fits Your Site?

2026-10-21Comparison Guide12 min read

The Short Answer

If you run a quarry or mine that moves 25-40 t payloads per trip over rough haul roads, the SAGMOTO X5 is the heavier, more durable choice with its bigger frame and 380-460 HP Weichai range. If your work is construction tipper and mixer duty at 18-30 t payload on mixed paved and site roads, the SAGMOTO X6 is lighter, cheaper, and more economical per tonne-km. Both share FAST transmissions and Weichai/Yuchai service ecosystems, but they are not interchangeable — the X5's frame, axle, and engine envelope sit a class above the X6.

Payload and GVW

The X5's 18-25 m³ body and 16 T tandem axle let it carry roughly 30-40 t of overburden or aggregate in an 8x4 rigid, where the X6's 10-20 m³ tipper tops out around 25-30 t. On a steep quarry ramp that difference is the payload that pays the truck's finance each month.

Frame is the real differentiator: The X5's 800 × 250 mm channel frame carries the bending load of a fully loaded 25 m³ body without the fatigue risk the X6's lighter 280 × 80 × 8 mm frame sees at the top of its range. For continuous high-cycle quarry loading, the X5 frame is the safer long-term bet.

Frame, Axles and Turning Circle

The X5 uses MAN-technology 16 T tandem axles and a deep channel frame tuned for sustained heavy rigid duty. The X6 uses hub-reduction or single-reduction 13 T axles with a lighter frame optimised for tipper and mixer versatility. Turning circle favours the X6 on tight construction sites: its shorter wheelbase options turn inside the X5's longer rigid platform. If your site has narrow haul roads and tight tipping faces, the X6 manoeuvres better; if you tip into a wide haul road from a bench, the X5's size is irrelevant.

Fuel Burn per Tonne-Kilometre

The X5 burns more absolute fuel (higher HP, heavier tare) but moves more payload, so its cost per tonne-km is competitive on long quarry hauls. The X6 wins on short, light cycles where its smaller engine is never over-worked.

Parts Commonality

Both use FAST gearboxes and Weichai engines, so filters, clutches, and consumables overlap heavily. The X5's MAN axles are a separate parts line from the X6's Hande axles, so a mixed fleet needs both axle spares. If you already run the SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 in X6 form, adding X5 units lets you share engine and transmission stock but not axle stock.

What each is genuinely bad at: The X5 is over-spec and heavy for light urban construction tipper work — its tare weight and purchase price are wasted where loads are small. The X6 is under-framed for continuous 35 t-plus quarry overburden and will show fatigue faster than the X5 at the top of its range. Match the truck to the load, not the logo.

Service Intervals and Warranty

Both platforms carry a 24-month or 200,000 km vehicle warranty, with the Weichai engine covered to 36 months or 300,000 km. On quarry and mining cycles, shorten the oil interval to 20,000 km and the hydraulic-tipper service to every 250 tipping hours regardless of distance, because dust and load shock dominate wear. The X5's MAN axles and the X6's Hande axles both use standard S-cam brakes with WABCO ABS, so brake shoes and chambers are cheap and interchangeable within each brand. A mixed X5/X6 fleet should budget one axle-specific spares line for each, plus a shared engine and FAST-gearbox bin.

Financing and Resale

Because the X5 and X6 sit close on sticker price, financing terms and resale decide the real cost. The X5's higher GVW ceiling and heavier frame hold value better in mining resale, where buyers pay a premium for proven quarry durability. The X6's lower tare and versatility hold value better in construction resale, where buyers want a truck that can flip between tipper and mixer work. Either way, keep the COC, original invoice, and service records, because West African used-truck buyers discount incomplete paperwork by 10-15%.

Braking, Safety and Cab

Both trucks use full-air S-cam brakes with ABS and optional EBS. The X5's larger tare and 16 T axles carry bigger brake chambers suited to sustained descending on mine access roads, while the X6's lighter build stops quicker in short site cycles. Cab comfort matters on relay tipper duties: the X5's X5000 sleeper and the X6's 2,300 mm wide-body sleeper both offer air-suspended seats and bunks, but the X5 platform is the quieter of the two because its cab is isolated on four shock absorbers. For two-driver quarry relay work, the X5's cab is the better rest environment; for single-shift construction tipper work, the X6 cab is entirely adequate.

Which to Buy First

If your capital is limited and your contracts are mixed, start with the X6 to prove the work and keep tare and running cost low, then add X5 units as quarry or mining overburden contracts lock in. If your first contract is already a high-volume quarry or overburden lane, go straight to the X5 because its per-trip payload advantage compounds from day one and its frame will outlast the X6 at the top of the duty cycle. Either way, standardise on Weichai power and FAST transmissions so your mechanic training and parts bin scale with the fleet rather than fragment across brands.

Spec Checklist: X5 Quarry Build

For a quarry or mine overburden lane, specify the X5 as follows:

  • 8x4 rigid, Weichai WP12.460, FAST 12JSD200 manual or AMT
  • 25 m³ reinforced steel body with Hardox floor
  • 16 T MAN tandem axle, 5.26 ratio for ramp torque
  • Inter-wheel and inter-axle differential locks
  • Hydraulic tipper cylinder rated to the body weight
  • Heavy-duty cooling and raised air intake for dust
  • Full-air brakes with EBS and engine retarder

Spec Checklist: X6 Site Build

For construction tipper and mixer duty, specify the X6 as follows:

  • 6x4 tipper, Weichai WP10.375, FAST 10JS with crawler
  • 18-20 m³ tipper or 8-9 m³ mixer drum
  • 2 × 13 T hub-reduction rear, 5.875 ratio
  • 8+6 mm double-layer frame for mixer torsion
  • Exhaust brake plus optional hydraulic retarder
  • Standard 2,300 mm sleeper cab, tropical radiator

Following these checklists keeps each truck inside its strength envelope: the X5 built heavy for high-cycle quarry output, the X6 built versatile for mixed construction and mixer work. Neither should be spec'd into the other's role.

Resale Timing

Trade X5 quarry trucks at five to six years, while frame and axle life still command a premium, and X6 site trucks at four to five years, while mixer and tipper versatility is freshest. Selling inside these windows captures the strongest West African used-truck demand and funds the replacement before a major component overhaul falls due, keeping fleet capital rotating rather than stranded in tired iron.

Recommendation

Specify the X5 where payload and frame life drive profit: quarries, mine-support, and high-cycle overburden. Specify the X6 where versatility and low running cost matter: construction tippers, mixer fleets, and road works. Both are documented against the SHACMAN X3000 heavy duty truck full specs if you want to benchmark against the sister platform before committing. For most mixed fleets, running both — X5 for the heavy lane, X6 for the light lane — is the most cost-efficient answer.
