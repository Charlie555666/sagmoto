SAGMOTO X6 Propeller Shaft and Driveline Maintenance: U-Joints, Center Bearing and Balance

2026-10-21Maintenance & After-sales14 min read

The X6 driveline is long, loaded and exposed

The SAGMOTO X6 is the 16 to 25-tonne backbone of the range, offered as a 6x4 tipper on the Weichai WP10 (336 to 375 HP) and as a 6x4 tractor rated to 60t GCW on the same engine with a FAST 12-speed and optional hydraulic retarder. Moving that torque from the gearbox to the tandem axles is a driveline that is longer and more exposed than a rigid truck's, which makes it the first thing to shake, clunk and, in the worst case, drop on a haul road. This guide covers the X6 6x4 layout from gearbox to axle, U-joint greasing and inspection, the center support bearing and rubber cushion, the slip joint, phasing and balance marks, vibration diagnosis at road speed, flange-bolt torque, the difference between a tired U-joint and a dropped shaft, inter-axle diff-lock discipline, and the parts to keep on the shelf.

Unlike a car, the X6 driveline is a series of straight shafts joined by universal joints that must flex as the suspension articulates. Every joint carries the full engine torque and every one is a wear point. The discipline that keeps a driveline alive is simple: grease on schedule, torque on schedule, and balance respected. A driveline that is greased but unbalanced will still throw a shaft; a balanced but dry joint will seize and snap.

X6 6x4 driveline layout

From front to back the X6 6x4 runs: gearbox output flange, a short front propeller shaft, the center support bearing on the chassis cross-member, an intermediate shaft, the inter-axle (forward) axle input, and the tandem rear axles. The 6x4 tractor uses 2 x 16-tonne single-reduction rear axles; the tipper uses 2 x 13-tonne hub-reduction sets. The inter-axle differential splits torque between the two rear axles and carries its own lock for low-grip sites.

Key point: On the X6 6x4 the center bearing is a maintenance item, not a fit-and-forget part. Its rubber cushion hardens and cracks with heat and splash, and a failed cushion lets the shaft whip — which is what breaks the joints downstream.

U-joint inspection and grease points

Every X6 U-joint has a grease nipple on the cross. The joint is happy only when fresh grease purges the old paste from the bearing caps; once the cap rusts, the trunnion wears and the joint develops the classic clunk. Grease with a lithium EP grade rated for the operating temperature, and pump until clean grease shows at the seal — not past it, because over-greasing blows the cap seal.

Inspection is a hands-and-eyes job. With the shaft static, grab each tube and try to rotate it against the joint; any radial movement or a metallic click at the cap means the bearing is loose. A rust weep at the cap seal is the early warning that water is in and grease is out. Mark the shaft with chalk at each service so a turned-down joint that hides wear is caught next time.

Center support bearing and rubber cushion

The center bearing hangs the intermediate shaft from the chassis and lets it flex as the rear suspension moves. Its rubber cushion absorbs the whip; when the rubber cracks or the bearing hums, the shaft runs out of true and the whole driveline vibrates. Check the cushion for splits and oil soak (a leaking axle or gearbox seal will soften it), and spin the bearing by hand for roughness. Replace the assembly as a unit — pressing a new bearing into a tired housing is false economy.

Slip joint, phasing and balance marks

The slip joint compensates for axle wind-up under torque, sliding in and out as the suspension loads. It needs grease too, but it must never be assembled out of phase. The X6 shafts are painted with balance and phasing marks at the factory; when you separate a shaft, paint your own alignment marks so the yokes go back at 0 degrees, not 90. A shaft rebuilt 90 degrees out of phase shakes from 30 km/h upward and will not balance no matter how many weights you fit.

Key point: If a shaft was removed for any reason, always reassembly to the original phasing marks. Out-of-phase U-joints impose a once-per-revolution speed ripple that reads exactly like an unbalanced tire but destroys joints instead of tread.

Vibration diagnosis at road speed

Driveline vibration is speed-related, which makes it diagnosable. A U-joint fault usually appears as a shudder through the floor at a specific speed band (often 40 to 70 km/h) that eases above and below. A center-bearing fault hums and grows with speed. A tire or wheel imbalance shakes the steering, not the seat, and a failed slip joint clunks on throttle tip-in. Use the table to narrow the cause before lifting a wrench.

Flange-bolt torque and why it matters

The shafts bolt to the gearbox, center bearing and axle flanges with high-tensile bolts and self-locking nuts. These must be torqued to the X6 specification, typically 90 to 110 Nm for the M12 flange bolts depending on grade, and re-torqued after the first 1,000 km of a new or rebuilt shaft because the joint seats. A loose flange bolt walks out, elongates its hole and the shaft separates — the single most common "dropped shaft" cause that is not a joint failure. Use a fresh self-locking nut every time; the nylon patch loses clamp after one use.

Symptoms of a failing U-joint before it drops a shaft

A U-joint fails in stages, and every stage is visible if the driver reports honestly. The first sign is a light clunk when selecting drive or reverse. The second is a squeak from the cap that grease no longer silences. The third is radial play you can feel by hand. The fourth — and the last safe one — is a visible rust weep at the cap and a hot shaft. Past that, the joint binds, the tube bends under torque and the shaft drops. The SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover range uses the same FAST 12JS and tandem layout, so this failure ladder applies to the X6 tractor as well as the tipper.

Key point: A U-joint that squeaks after greasing has already lost its cap grease film. It will not recover. Replace it that week — the cost of one joint is a rounding error next to a shaft through the fuel tank or a tow off a grade.

Mining and gravel duty greasing frequency

Off-road X6 tippers in mining and gravel service run in dust and water that flush grease from the caps faster than highway miles. Treat the U-joints as a daily or every-100-hour item, and fit shielded caps where the site allows. Wash the underside with low-pressure water only — a high-pressure lance drives water past the cap seal and starts the rust that ends the joint. After any ford or pressure wash, re-grease before the next shift.

Inter-axle differential lock usage discipline

The X6 6x4 inter-axle diff lock is a traction aid, not a cruise control. Engage it only for low-grip surfaces — mud, loose gravel, a slippery ramp — and disengage the moment the truck is back on firm, straight ground. Driving locked on a paved road forces the two rear axles to rotate at the same speed through a corner, which loads the diff, the shafts and the tires until something lets go. Train drivers: lock for the bog, unlock for the road, and never lock at speed.
