SAGMOTO i9 Electric Chassis as a Municipal Street Sweeper for Zero-Emission City Cleaning

2026-10-27Industry Application8 min read

From Chassis to Sweeper: Why the SAGMOTO i9 Fits Municipal Fleets

Municipal cleaning departments across Europe, the Gulf and Southeast Asia are replacing diesel road sweepers with battery-electric alternatives because city councils now set hard deadlines for emission-free public service vehicles. The SAGMOTO i9 electric chassis gives body builders a ready-made heavy urban platform with a high-voltage power take-off, so a sweeping body can run its brushes, vacuum fan and water pump without a separate engine. That single change removes the loudest and dirtiest component from the vehicle and lets operators work in pedestrian zones before sunrise.

Converting the i9 into a street sweeper starts with the cab and wheelbase. The i9 uses a low-entry cab layout suited to stop-and-go collection routes, and its rated gross vehicle weight leaves enough margin for a stainless water tank, a debris hopper and the brush assembly. Because the chassis is engineered as an electric truck from the ground up rather than a diesel truck with a battery bolted on, the sweep body installer gets clean CAN signals and a stable 540V or 600V feed for the e-PTO motor.

Key point: the i9 eliminates the secondary diesel engine found on conventional sweepers, so a single battery pack powers both the drive wheels and every body function from brushing to suction.

Electric Drivetrain Benefits for Urban Road Cleaning

A battery-electric sweeper produces no exhaust at the curb where children walk to school and where open-air markets set up each morning. The absence of a running combustion engine also means the cab stays cooler and the driver is not breathing filtered diesel fumes for an eight-hour shift. Fleets that track total cost of ownership find that the electricity needed to recharge a 280kWh pack costs a fraction of the diesel a comparable mechanical sweeper burns across the same route.

Regenerative braking on the i9 recovers energy each time the driver lifts off the pedal in residential streets, and that recovered charge extends the practical range when the vehicle is crawling at sweeping speed. Sweeping itself is a low-speed duty cycle, which is the exact profile where electric trucks beat diesel on efficiency. Quiet Night Operation and Resident Relations

City cleaning that once had to wait for daylight can now run between 23:00 and 05:00 because the i9 sweeper operates at a sound level low enough to satisfy residential noise ordinances. Quiet night operation clears busy shopping streets before commuters arrive and avoids closing lanes during peak traffic. A supervisor in Riyadh reported that moving sweeping to overnight slots cut daytime congestion complaints by roughly 40 percent within the first season of electric deployment.

The low acoustic signature also matters near hospitals and universities, where even a short diesel idling sweep triggers noise grievances. With the i9, the only noticeable sound is the brush contact and a soft fan tone, both of which fall inside most municipal night-work limits. Operators can add a pedestrian warning speaker that switches off automatically above 10 km/h.

Key point: night cleaning with the i9 keeps main roads clear during business hours and keeps noise complaints inside the limits set by residential districts.

e-PTO and Body Integration for Sweep Equipment

The electric power take-off is the feature that makes the i9 attractive to sweeper body manufacturers. Instead of mounting a belt-driven pump, the builder connects the body controller to the chassis high-voltage bus and draws exactly the kilowatts the vacuum fan needs at any moment. This demand-based supply avoids the constant fuel burn of an idling donkey engine and lets the main battery meter show true remaining cleaning time.

Body options range from twin-engine regenerative air sweepers to compact mechanical broom units for narrow old-town lanes. Because the e-PTO can deliver instant torque, the side brushes spin up without the lag of a clutch engagement, and the water spray pump modulates cleanly to keep dust down on dry summer days. Installers typically reserve 30 to 50 kW of continuous e-PTO capacity for body work while keeping the rest for propulsion.

Battery Capacity and Daily Range Planning

Fleet planners should size the battery against the longest single shift rather than the longest route, because sweeping speed and brush load vary more than road distance. A 280kWh pack comfortably covers a standard eight-hour municipal circuit with suction running, while a 450kWh pack suits regions where the sweeper also tow a leaf collector or runs a high-flow water recycling system. Opportunity charging during the lunch break at a 120kW depot charger restores enough charge for a second shift.

Cold-climate operators in northern Europe should note that battery range falls in winter, so specifying the larger pack or a cab pre-heat schedule protects the duty cycle. The i9 thermal management keeps cells in the safe band, and the body controller can be set to pause suction if state of charge drops below a configurable reserve, guaranteeing the truck can always drive back to base.

Total Cost of Ownership Against Diesel Sweepers

Fleet directors who approve the i9 sweeper usually face a higher upfront price than a diesel equivalent, because the battery pack and electric drivetrain cost more to build today. The financial case rests on operating savings that accumulate every shift: electricity per kilometre runs far cheaper than diesel, the regenerative braking cuts brake wear, and the absence of a donkey engine removes an entire maintenance schedule of oil, filters and belts. Over a five-year municipal contract these savings commonly close the purchase gap and turn the electric option into the cheaper life-cycle choice.

Labour cost also shifts in the i9's favour, because drivers report lower fatigue in a silent cab and the cleaner working environment reduces sick days linked to exhaust exposure. When a city already runs electric buses, the sweeper shares the same charging bays and technician training, spreading the infrastructure investment across more vehicles. A simple payback model that counts fuel, maintenance and health savings usually shows the i9 beating diesel inside three to four years of typical urban use, after which every further year is pure saving.

Depot Charging Infrastructure and Setup

Setting up a depot for the i9 sweeper starts with a load assessment, because several trucks charging overnight need a thicker supply than a single passenger car point. Most municipalities install a 60 to 120 kW DC charger for opportunity top-ups and an overnight AC bank for the full fleet, sized so the last truck finishes charging before the first shift leaves. The i9 accepts standard CCS2 or GB/T connectors depending on the export market, and the body builder should confirm the local plug standard before shipment to avoid a costly adapter retrofit.

Cable management and a weather-protected charging lane keep the morning dispatch smooth, and a simple charge scheduler prevents all trucks drawing peak current at once. Fleets in regions with cheap night tariffs programme the bulk charge into the small-hours window, which both lowers cost and supports grid balance. Regional Incentives and Zero-Emission Mandates

Many national and city governments now subsidise electric municipal vehicles through grants that offset the i9's higher sticker price, and some forbid new diesel sweepers in clean-air zones entirely. Importers should check whether their destination offers a purchase rebate, a reduced road tax, or access to restricted lanes, because these benefits change the total cost calculation in the i9's favour. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading can advise on pairing the i9 sweeper with other models in the SAGMOTO new energy line so a mixed fleet captures the maximum available incentive without duplicating charging standards.

Cold-climate operators should also factor heating energy into the incentive maths, because cabin and battery warming draws from the pack in winter. Specifying a heat-pump cabin and a scheduled pre-condition while still on grid power keeps the on-route range intact, and the i9 controller logs energy split so the finance team can prove the saving to grant auditors. Planning the incentive claim alongside the technical spec, rather than after delivery, is the step that turns a subsidy from a nice-to-have into a confirmed line on the budget.
