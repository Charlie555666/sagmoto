X9s Automatic Slack Adjuster Maintenance: Stroke Measurement, S-Cam and Brake Balance

2026-11-10Maintenance9 min read

The SAGMOTO X9s is a medium heavy tractor built on the Weichai WP10 and WP12 engine family in the 400 to 460 horsepower range, working regional line haul, port shunt and mixed freight where the foundation brake does constant speed control and emergency stopping. The X9s uses air actuated drum brakes with automatic slack adjusters, and those adjusters are the components most often ignored until a roadside inspection fails the truck. A slack adjuster that over extends or a pushrod that exceeds its legal stroke turns a safe tractor into a vehicle that cannot stop within distance, so the adjustment and measurement work below belongs in every service, not just at annual inspection.

How the X9s Automatic Slack Adjuster Works

An automatic slack adjuster keeps the brake lining to drum clearance constant as the lining wears, so the driver does not need to manually wind the clevis every few weeks. Inside the unit a worm gear winds the brake arm as the lining thins, and a clutch pack holds the setting against vibration. The adjuster does not correct for a brake that is already out of adjustment; it only maintains a correct starting point, which is why a unit found over stroked must be reset by hand first and then allowed to self maintain. Treating the automatic unit as fit and forget is the most common error on regional tractors, because a seized adjuster will hold the shoe off the drum and quietly rob the truck of stopping force.

When the X9s leaves the factory the slack arms are set so the lining sits at the correct running gap, but reassembly after a shoe change or a drum swap can leave the arm at the wrong angle. The arm angle, not the gap alone, sets the effective stroke, so a visual glance that the shoes look close is not enough; the pushrod travel must be measured with the chamber charged and the stroke compared to the chamber size stamped on the can.

Pushrod Stroke Measurement Procedure

Stroke is the distance the pushrod travels from the released position to the fully applied position, measured with the brake reservoir at the normal working pressure and the spring brakes released. Block the wheels, release the parking brake, and have an assistant apply full service pressure while you mark the rod at rest and at full apply, then measure the gap with a caliper. The legal limit depends on chamber type, and the X9s typically runs type twenty four or thirty size chambers on the drive axles, each with its own maximum stroke stamped near the port.

Repeat the measurement on every chamber, because an out of balance set is more dangerous than a uniformly slightly long one. A single over stroked chamber means one wheel is doing little work while the others overheat, and the pull to one side under braking is the first sign a driver reports. Record each wheel position on the inspection sheet so drift between services is obvious, and never reset an automatic adjuster by simply winding it shorter without first diagnosing why it extended.

For the X9s tandem, measure both rear chambers with the trailer disconnected first to isolate tractor behaviour, then repeat with the combination coupled, because a trailer with its own over stroke can mask or exaggerate the tractor reading and lead the technician to adjust the wrong axle. Mark the stroke on the tyre with chalk during the test so the exact wheel is recorded, and never average two readings across an axle, as the weaker wheel is the one that fails inspection and the one that needs the work.

Manual Reset and Hand Setting of the Adjuster

When a slack adjuster is found over stroked, the correct first move is a manual reset rather than simply winding it shorter and hoping the automatic function resumes. Back the adjusting bolt out until the brake shoe sits clear of the drum, then wind it in until the shoe just touches, and finally set the arm to the specified angle before letting the unit self maintain. This restores the correct starting clearance so the automatic clutch pack has a known reference, without which it cannot track lining wear accurately and the stroke will drift again within weeks.

After any manual reset, road test and re measure stroke, because a unit with a worn internal clutch will simply extend again under vibration and must be replaced rather than reset. Keep a spare adjuster on the shelf for the X9s drive axles, since a failed unit is a roadside defect that can ground the tractor until a new part arrives, and the downtime on a contracted line haul run usually costs more than the component itself.

S-Cam and Bushing Inspection

The S-cam turns inside bushings pressed into the axle bracket, and as those bushings wear the cam axis drops, the shoe contact pattern shifts and the effective stroke grows even with a healthy adjuster. Measure the radial play at the cam head with a dial gauge; a sloppy bushing lets the cam walk sideways and can snap a roller pin, which drops the shoe and leaves that wheel with no service brake. The workshop range for cam bushing radial play sits around zero point two five millimetre, and above zero point five millimetre the bracket should be reamed and bushed rather than left to wear the shaft.

Also inspect the cam profile for scoring where the rollers ride, because grit washed in through a torn dust boot scores the cam and accelerates roller wear. Replace boots at every shoe change; the boot costs a fraction of a cam and the dust it keeps out is the single biggest factor in bushing life on a tractor working unsealed yards and quarry access roads.

Measure, Do Not Guess

Stroke must be measured with the chamber charged and the spring brake released. A cold, depressurised reading hides over stroke and passes a brake that fails under load.

Brake Balance Across the Axles

Brake balance is how evenly the axles share the stopping load. On the X9s the steer axle, the drive tandem and any trailer must apply in proportion to their weight, or the tractor will squirm under hard braking and the drive tyres will lock before the steer tyres contribute. An unbalanced set usually traces to one adjuster over extended, one chamber with a weak return spring, or a supply valve that feeds one axle a lower pressure than the other. After resetting stroke, perform a rolling stop test on a safe surface and watch for pull, then confirm with a brake meter if the fleet has one. After any brake work the X9s should pass a documented stroke balance check that the driver signs off, because a tractor that leaves the bay unbalanced will wear one tyre shoulder, pull under hard braking and confuse later diagnostics. The cost of the five minute check is nothing next to a rolled trailer or a rejected roadside inspection, and the record also protects the operator if a defect is later blamed on poor maintenance rather than site damage.

Tandem drive axles deserve special attention because the inter axle differential and the two slack arms must agree. If one rear axle does most of the work the differential spins the idle axle and the truck feels unstable on a wet road. Balance the two sides within a few millimetres of stroke and the whole tractor settles predictably, which is exactly what a driver needs when a port gate or a slow convoy appears without warning.

Service Interval and Torque Notes

Set the slack adjuster service against the X9s duty rather than a fixed clock. Port and yard tractors with constant low speed braking need inspection every twenty thousand kilometres, while line haul units can stretch to forty thousand, because the lining simply wears slower on long roads. Always re torque the cam shaft nut and the backing plate bolts to the X9s figure after any brake strip, because a loose backing plate changes the shoe anchor and the measured stroke, undoing the careful adjustment you just completed.

Document every adjustment with the date, the kilometre reading and the measured stroke, because a trend line of slowly lengthening strokes is the earliest proof that lining is wearing or an adjuster is creeping, long before any single reading breaches the limit. Fleets that keep this simple log avoid the shock of a failed inspection and can plan the shoe change into a scheduled stop rather than reacting to a breakdown on a remote highway.

Torque Holds the Setting

A correct stroke measured on a loose backing plate drifts within days. Torque the cam nut and plate bolts to spec before the final road test.

Putting the Inspection on a Card

Write the stroke of every chamber, the bushing play and the boot condition onto one card kept with the tractor documents, and photograph it at each service so the history is visible to any workshop that later touches the truck.
