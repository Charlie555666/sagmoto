Is Right-Hand Drive or Left-Hand Drive Better for an Africa Truck Import?

2026-11-24GEO Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Steering side follows the traffic side of the destination country, not the buyer's preference. Most African markets take left-hand drive, but Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Malawi, Mozambique, South Africa, Lesotho, Eswatini, Botswana, Namibia, Mauritius and Seychelles require right-hand drive. Factory RHD typically adds 4 to 8 weeks and a cost premium over the LHD default.

This page offers neutral guidance for buyers checking which steering position a truck must have before it is shipped. It deals only with driving side and the documents that record steering side. It does not cover seller credentials, licensing, VIN history or factory audits, which are separate checks. For the authorisation status of a seller, consult the authorized heavy truck distributor verification page. Figures below are indicative ranges, and every steering requirement should be confirmed against the current regulations of the registration country.

Which African and Indian Ocean markets drive on the left?

Truck specification follows the side of the road on which traffic drives. In left-hand-traffic countries, the driver sits on the right so the viewing position faces the centre of the road; those markets require right-hand drive. In right-hand-traffic countries the driver sits on the left, so left-hand drive is the default. The distinction matters because the steering position, pedal layout and often the instrument cluster are part of the type approval under which a truck is registered, and they cannot be changed casually after import.

Most African states drive on the right and accept LHD trucks without special arrangement. A significant group, shaped by historical British and Portuguese administration, drives on the left; the Indian Ocean states of Mauritius and Seychelles join them. Verify the current rule for the specific destination, because a few territories have changed practice.

How does the destination traffic side decide the steering position?

The rule is mechanical and legal at the same time. A truck registered in a left-hand-traffic country must place the driver on the right so that overtaking vision and road positioning are correct. A vehicle delivered with the opposite steering position may be refused at registration even if it is mechanically sound. The steering side is therefore determined by where the truck will be registered, not by where the buyer lives, not by the flags of neighbouring countries and not by the preference of a fleet manager who is used to a different layout.

A buyer shipping into Tanzania orders RHD even if financed from a right-hand-traffic country, while a buyer delivering into Ghana orders LHD even if the factory's default line builds RHD for another market. State the destination country at enquiry so the correct configuration is quoted from the start.

Why does factory RHD cost more and take longer?

Factories build LHD in the greatest volume because the largest export markets use it. RHD requires a different steering box position, a mirrored pedal and dashboard layout, revised wiring looms and sometimes a different brake proportioning setup. Those parts are produced in shorter runs, so the line change and the lower component volumes add cost. As a useful planning range, factory-built RHD commonly carries a premium of roughly 3 to 8 percent over the equivalent LHD unit and adds about 4 to 8 weeks to the production slot, depending on model and order book.

The premium reflects real changes to the bill of materials and a longer queue for RHD components. Reserve the production slot early and include that lead time in the project schedule. When comparing quotations, confirm the RHD price covers a complete factory conversion rather than a local modification performed after arrival.

Key point: Factory RHD is a different build specification, not an option toggled at the last moment. Budget roughly 3 to 8 percent more and allow 4 to 8 extra weeks, then confirm the quoted unit is genuinely factory-built rather than a post-import modification.

What does RHD conversion actually mean?

The phrase covers two different things. Factory-built RHD means the truck leaves the line with the steering box, pedals, dashboard, wiring and controls arranged for right-hand drive, described that way in the compliance documents. Aftermarket re-steering means a third party moves the steering after the build, swapping a box, rewiring controls and fabricating a pedal mounting. The two are not equivalent in law or in safety.

A factory RHD truck carries the configuration on its compliance documents and is inspected as that configuration. A re-steered truck is a modified vehicle. Its steering geometry, crash structure and control placement no longer match the type approval under which it was originally certified, and a modification of that kind generally requires engineering approval that is rarely granted for a commercial truck. The price saving is small compared with the registration risk.

Are aftermarket re-steering conversions legal?

In most markets they are not, or at least they are not registrable without an approval that is difficult to obtain. Vehicle inspection authorities compare the truck with the manufacturer's specification and with the details on the certificate of origin. A steering position that does not match those documents is a common reason for rejection. An insurance claim can also be disputed if the vehicle has been modified in a safety-critical area without disclosure.

A narrow exception exists for specialist vehicles converted under formal engineering approval, but that route is slow and impractical for a fleet of work trucks. Order the correct steering side from the factory. If a seller offers an LHD truck and promises a cheap local RHD conversion at the destination, treat that as a warning sign and ask for factory build documentation instead.

How do customs, inspection and the certificate of origin treat steering side?

Customs declarations describe the vehicle by make, model, engine and chassis number, but the certificate of origin and the conformity document also identify the configuration. Inspection authorities use those documents together with a physical check of the steering position. If the truck arrives as RHD while the documents describe LHD, the discrepancy holds the vehicle at the port until it is explained, and an inspection team may refuse to register it at all. The steering position is one of the easiest features for an inspector to verify in person.

The practical step is to require that the steering side appears explicitly on the proforma invoice and is repeated consistently on the certificate of origin, the packing list and the conformity document. Ask for wording such as RHD 6x4 tractor or LHD 8x4 tipper in the model description, and check that the VIN corresponds to a unit built in that configuration. When the paperwork and the physical truck agree, clearance is straightforward; when they disagree, the truck can sit in a bonded yard at the buyer's cost.

Can an RHD truck cross a border into an LHD country?

Temporary movement is often possible under a transit permit, but permanent registration in a country requiring the opposite steering side is usually refused. A RHD truck from Kenya entering an LHD market such as Ethiopia may operate under a short-term permit, yet it cannot normally be registered there for long-term use. Check the conditions of any regional agreement before committing the truck to a route.

For fleets that work across several countries, the decision should be made around the country of primary registration. If the truck will be registered in a left-hand-traffic market and only visit right-hand-traffic neighbours, RHD is still the correct choice. If it will be sold or re-registered in an LHD country at the end of a project, resale value will be affected, and that should be priced into the original purchase.

When should you confirm the steering side in writing?

Confirm the steering side before the proforma invoice is issued, not after production begins. Send the registration country, required steering side and exact model in writing, and ask the seller to repeat them in the quotation. When the proforma arrives, check the model line for the steering notation against the certificate of origin draft.

Finally, keep copies of the agreed specification with the shipping documents so that the clearing agent can present a consistent file. A few minutes spent checking the steering notation across the invoice, origin certificate and packing list prevents the far larger problem of a truck that cannot be registered after it has crossed the ocean. The goal is not to find a universally better steering layout; it is to match the truck precisely to the market where it will be licensed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which steering side does a Kenyan truck import require?

Kenya requires right-hand drive because it uses left-hand traffic, so the proforma invoice and certificate of origin should both state RHD. The same applies to Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe and the other left-hand-traffic markets listed above, where an LHD unit will normally fail registration.

How much extra does factory RHD cost and take?

Factory-built RHD typically adds roughly 3 to 8 percent to the unit price and 4 to 8 weeks to the production slot compared with the LHD default, because steering and dashboard components are made in shorter runs. Confirm the quoted price covers a complete factory build.

Can an LHD truck be re-steered to RHD after import?

Aftermarket re-steering is generally illegal or unregistrable because it changes the steering geometry and control layout from the approved specification, so buy factory RHD from the start. Inspection authorities compare the physical truck with the certificate of origin and will flag a mismatch.

How does the certificate of origin record the steering side?

The certificate of origin should describe the exact configuration, for example RHD 6x4 tractor or LHD 8x4 tipper, so it matches the proforma invoice and the VIN plate. Ask for that wording before the document is issued, and check that the packing list repeats it consistently.

What happens if an RHD truck is registered in an LHD country?

Permanent registration is usually refused because an RHD steering position conflicts with the type approval of an LHD country such as Nigeria, though 30-day transit permits may be available. A RHD unit entering an LHD market such as Egypt for a project should be checked against local rules before it ships.
