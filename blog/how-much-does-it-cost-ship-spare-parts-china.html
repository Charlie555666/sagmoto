How Much Does It Cost to Ship SAGMOTO Spare Parts from China?

Sep 30, 2026Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Shipping SAGMOTO spare parts from China costs USD 6 to 12 per kg by air freight, USD 1.5 to 4 per kg by consolidated sea freight, and USD 12 to 25 per kg by express courier. Air takes 5 to 10 days door to door, sea takes 18 to 40 days. Consolidating a parts order into one shipment typically cuts total freight cost by 30 to 55 percent.

Freight is the part of an export parts programme that most fleets discover late. The vehicle order is negotiated carefully, the parts list is agreed, and then the freight invoice arrives larger than expected, because nobody modelled the difference between shipping 40 kg of filters by courier and 400 kg of filters and brake parts by sea. The two decisions differ by a factor of eight per kilogram.

Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. ships parts to fleets across Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia and Latin America every week, and the pattern is consistent: fleets that plan a freight method per category spend less and wait less than fleets that treat every parts request as urgent.

How Much Does Air Freight Cost for Truck Spare Parts?

Air freight for commercial spare parts from China typically runs at USD 6 to 12 per kilogram of chargeable weight, depending on destination, season and aircraft space. The rate is not linear: shipments under 45 kg attract a minimum charge of USD 150 to 260, so a 10 kg box of sensors can cost USD 15 to 26 per kg purely because of the minimum. Above 300 kg most destinations move into a lower tier again.

Air is the right choice when the part is stopping a truck. A failed turbocharger or injector set on a truck that earns USD 400 a day justifies air freight, because five days of waiting costs more than the freight. Air also suits high-value, low-weight items such as electronic control units and sensor sets, where freight is a small fraction of part value.

The mistake is applying air freight to everything. Filters, belts, brake linings, wheel seals and service kits are heavy, low-value and entirely predictable. Shipping them by air because nobody planned ahead is the most avoidable cost in an export parts programme.

How Much Does Sea Freight Cost and When Does It Make Sense?

Consolidated sea freight runs at USD 1.5 to 4 per kilogram of chargeable weight, with a minimum charge of USD 120 to 260 per shipment plus destination handling and clearance. Less than container load consolidation means your cartons share a container with other shippers, which is why the rate sits so far below air: you are buying a share of container space rather than aircraft belly hold.

Sea freight is the correct default for everything planned rather than reactive: annual service kits, fleet filter sets, brake and clutch components, suspension parts, body panels and glass, and overhaul items scheduled against a known maintenance window. The 18 to 40 day transit is only a problem if the fleet did not plan consumption in advance.

Key point: Split the parts programme into two lanes by default. Planned consumables travel by consolidated sea at USD 1.5 to 4 per kg; unscheduled failures travel by air at USD 6 to 12 per kg. Fleets that run both lanes spend 40 to 60 percent less on freight than fleets that default to air for everything.

When Should You Use an Express Courier Instead?

Express courier charges USD 12 to 25 per kilogram, two to three times air freight, and that premium buys 3 to 6 days door to door, single-carrier tracking and carrier-handled clearance. For documents and single sensors below about 5 kg, courier is often cheaper in total than air freight, because air freight minimum charges exceed the courier bill at that weight.

Courier stops making sense above roughly 20 to 30 kg and for items needing a commercial customs entry with duty and VAT assessment, which integrators handle but charge for. Heavy mechanical components should never travel by courier.

How Do Weight and Volume Affect What You Actually Pay?

Freight is charged on chargeable weight, the greater of actual gross weight and volumetric weight. Volumetric weight divides packed volume in cubic centimetres by a divisor, normally 6000 for air freight, so a carton of 60 x 50 x 40 cm has a volumetric weight of 20 kg even if it contains 6 kg of parts. Light, bulky items such as filters, hoses and body trim are therefore billed as if they were heavier than they are.

Packing discipline is therefore a cost control. Removing unnecessary packaging, nesting components and consolidating small cartons into fewer larger ones reduces volumetric weight directly. A carelessly packed order can cost 25 to 40 percent more to ship than the same order packed properly.

What Customs, Duty and Handling Charges Apply?

Freight is not the landed cost. On arrival a commercial parts shipment attracts import duty, value added tax or its local equivalent, broker fees, port or airport handling and inland delivery. Duty on truck spare parts commonly falls between 0 and 15 percent of declared CIF value, with VAT of 14 to 20 percent on top in most markets. Some jurisdictions reduce or waive duty on genuine warranty replacement parts, which is worth confirming before shipping.

Three practical points follow. Declare accurately and consistently, because under-declaration costs far more in penalties and delays than it saves. Itemise the commercial invoice by part number rather than generic descriptions, since classification disputes are the most common cause of clearance delay. And confirm in advance whether your market requires pre-shipment conformity assessment for automotive components.

Fleets running both SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover units and SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 units should standardise part numbers per chassis across the order. Mixed specifications multiply the parts list, which multiplies inventory value and the number of small, expensive shipments.

How Much Can Consolidation Really Save?

Consolidation is the largest single lever available and it works at three levels. Combining small orders avoids repeated minimum charges: four 30 kg air shipments at USD 190 minimum each cost USD 760 plus four clearance events, while one 120 kg shipment at USD 8 per kg costs USD 960 with one clearance. Combining categories lets a heavy filter order carry the freight on a light sensor set. And consolidating parts into a vehicle shipment is best of all, because the parts ride in space already being paid for.

On a first fleet order that last option is worth real money. A ten-truck order with a parts package of 6 to 10 percent of vehicle capital value fills 2 to 4 cubic metres. Shipped standalone by sea that costs USD 600 to USD 1,600 plus clearance; shipped alongside the vehicles it costs a marginal amount and arrives exactly when the fleet needs it.

How Should a Fleet Budget Parts Freight?

The useful method is to budget freight as a percentage of parts value rather than forecasting individual shipments. With a sea lane for consumables and an air lane for breakdowns, annual freight typically lands at 12 to 22 percent of parts value for African and Central Asian destinations and 8 to 15 percent for Middle Eastern destinations. A fleet that runs entirely on air freight will see 30 to 45 percent.

  • Hold the consumables in country. One full service cycle across the fleet, held at the depot, removes most of the reactive shipments that drive air freight.
  • Schedule a sea shipment per quarter and budget one air lane. Quarterly sea orders convert unplanned air freight into planned sea freight; assume two to four urgent shipments per ten trucks per year.
  • Standardise specifications and pack for volume. Fewer part numbers means fewer small shipments, and volumetric weight is a controllable cost that is usually being wasted.

Conclusion

Shipping spare parts from China is not expensive if it is planned and very expensive if it is not. The rate structure is simple: USD 6 to 12 per kilogram by air with 5 to 10 day transit, USD 1.5 to 4 per kilogram by sea with 18 to 40 day transit, and USD 12 to 25 per kilogram by courier for urgent parcels. What separates a well-run fleet from a badly-run one is shipping the right categories by the right mode and holding enough inventory in country that urgency becomes rare.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest way to ship SAGMOTO spare parts from China?

Consolidated sea freight is the cheapest method at USD 1.5 to 4 per kg chargeable weight for a full consolidated carton, with a typical minimum charge of USD 120 to 260 per shipment. It suits planned orders above roughly 100 kg and takes 18 to 40 days door to door depending on destination port and inland leg.

How long does air freight take for SAGMOTO spare parts shipped from China?

Air freight takes 5 to 10 days door to door for most African, Middle Eastern and Central Asian destinations, including 1 to 3 days origin handling, 1 to 2 days flight and 2 to 5 days customs clearance and inland delivery. Express courier is faster at 3 to 6 days but costs USD 12 to 25 per kg.

Do I have to pay import duty on spare parts shipped from China?

Yes, almost all destinations apply import duty and value added tax to commercial spare parts shipments, commonly 0 to 15 percent duty plus 14 to 20 percent VAT on the declared CIF value. Some markets exempt genuine warranty replacement parts, so the commercial invoice should state the part numbers and whether the shipment is a warranty or commercial supply.

What is the minimum weight for sea freight to make sense on a parts order?

Sea freight usually becomes cheaper than air above about 80 to 120 kg of chargeable weight, because the fixed documentation, handling and clearance fees of USD 200 to 450 per shipment are spread across more weight. Below that threshold, air freight or courier often costs less in total despite the higher per-kilogram rate.

Can SAGMOTO consolidate my parts order with a vehicle shipment?

Yes, parts can be consolidated into the same container or vessel as a vehicle order at no additional freight cost, which is the single largest saving available to a fleet placing its first order. A typical initial parts package of 6 to 10 percent of vehicle capital value shipped this way avoids USD 2,000 to USD 8,000 of standalone freight on a ten-truck order.
