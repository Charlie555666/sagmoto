SAGMOTO E3 MAX Bulk Feed Tanker: Powder Body and Pneumatic Discharge Application

2026-11-26Industry Application9 min read

Bulk feed is delivered as a free-flowing powder or meal from a mill to a farm silo, and the truck that carries it is a specialised piece of equipment rather than a cargo body with a roof. The product must stay dry and uncontaminated in transit, then be moved out of the tank and up into a silo through a sealed pipeline without manual handling. That is done pneumatically: a blower supplies compressed air that fluidises the bed of material and carries it through the discharge line to the storage bin. The chassis underneath must carry a dense load, provide power for the blower, and spread the weight across its axles within legal limits.

The SAGMOTO E3 MAX is a heavy platform offered as an eight-by-four tipper chassis with a Cummins or Weichai engine in the 430 to 520 horsepower band, torque of roughly 2,000 to 2,400 N m, a Fast twelve-speed gearbox and a gross vehicle weight around 31 tonnes. Those figures make it a sound base for a bulk feed tanker, provided the tank, blower and subframe are engineered as one package. This article works through the load, the powder body, the pneumatic system and the axle distribution that determine whether a feed delivery fleet is productive and legal on every trip.

Key point: Feed is weight-limited, not volume-limited. Size the tank to the payload the eight-by-four chassis can legally carry, and calculate the empty weight of the body and blower first so the saleable tonnes are not eaten by equipment.

Feed types, density and the payload calculation

Not all feed weighs the same. A fine mash or meal can be comparatively light and bulky, while a denser pelleted product or a mineral premix packs far more mass into the same volume. The tank must be sized around the densest product the fleet will carry, because a body that looks only half full can already be at the weight limit with a heavy ration. The buyer should list the products, their bulk densities and the typical order size, then work backward to the tank volume that makes sense. Adding compartments lets one truck carry several rations to different farms on the same run, but each partition adds weight and complicates cleaning between products.

Payload is the gross vehicle weight minus the chassis and the complete fitted body, including the tank, blower, piping, walkways and any mounting hardware. A tanker body is heavier than a tipper box, and the blower and its drive are not trivial in weight, so the calculation must include every component before the permissible feed tonnage is stated. Axle distribution is just as important as the total. A front-mounted blower or a tank loaded heavily toward the cab can overload the front axle, while a long tank can leave the rear group over its limit. Mark the load positions on the body and confirm the axle loads with a weighbridge once the truck is built.

Powder body design

A bulk feed tank is a cylindrical or slightly oval vessel mounted along the chassis frame, usually divided into two or more compartments so several products can be carried at once. The body is built from sheet steel or aluminium, with the choice driven by payload and corrosion. A lighter alloy body allows more feed to be carried, while steel is tougher and easier to repair where product is abrasive. The interior must be smooth and free of ledges where material can hang up, because feed that remains in a corner will contaminate the next load and attract moisture. Access hatches, ladders and walkways must be safe for a driver to use at a farm silo.

The floor of each compartment is the working part of the system. Pneumatic discharge relies on a fluidised bed, usually a porous membrane or a series of aeration pads along the bottom, through which air is introduced to make the powder behave like a liquid. The air bleeds through the material, reducing friction so it flows toward the discharge outlet. The angle and shape of the hopper bottom must suit the product: a steep, clean funnel empties meal well, while a sticky or high-fat ration may need a different lining or a vibrator. Buyers can review the SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 to understand the frame and axle architecture of the eight-by-four chassis before the tank body is designed around it.

Key point: Size the blower for the longest discharge line and the densest product, not the nearest silo. A unit that empties a tank in ten minutes at the mill may take far longer when the farm bin is across a field and the line runs uphill.

Pneumatic discharge and blower sizing

The blower is the heart of the discharge system. It supplies a steady flow of clean, dry air at the pressure needed to lift feed through the pipeline into the silo. Two broad approaches are common: a positive-displacement blower that delivers a high volume at moderate pressure, and a compressor that delivers a smaller volume at higher pressure. Feed work usually favours the high-volume blower, because the material is moved by entraining it in a stream of air rather than by pushing it as a dense plug. The required pressure rises with the vertical lift and the length of the line, so the fleet should be specified for the worst farm on the route rather than an easy one.

Air must be clean and dry. Moisture in the supply line can wet the product, cause clumping in the membrane and lead to blockages that take hours to clear. An intake filter, a reliable separator and a drain that the driver checks each day are simple protections. The blower is driven from the chassis, commonly through a power take-off or a belt-driven auxiliary drive, and the engine must have enough reserve to run it while the truck is stationary. Heat is a concern during long discharges on a hot day, so the blower and its drive need ventilation and the engine cooling system must cope with a parked truck. A discharge that slows markedly toward the end of a load is often a sign of inadequate airflow or a leaking line rather than a fault in the tank.

Eight-by-four chassis and axle distribution

An eight-by-four E3 MAX chassis is chosen for payload and stability. With a gross vehicle weight around 31 tonnes and a strong frame, it can carry a substantial tank and still leave room for saleable feed. The wheelbase must suit the tank length and the sites served: a long body empties efficiently but needs space to manoeuvre at a farm, while a shorter tank sacrifices a little capacity for access. The subframe must spread the concentrated load of the tank saddles and the blower into the chassis rails rather than into a light body structure, and its mounting points should be accessible for inspection.

Axle distribution has to be checked in the loaded condition with each compartment full. Feed loaded unevenly across compartments will shift the centre of gravity sideways and load one rear axle more than the other, which affects handling on a country road and can breach an axle limit even when the total weight is legal. Drivers should follow a marked loading sequence and avoid filling a single compartment to one side. Braking deserves attention too: a dense load and a long wheelbase mean significant energy to absorb on a descent, so the full air brake system and its adjustment schedule must be maintained without shortcuts. A weighbridge check at the start of service is a cheap way to confirm the design assumptions.

Loading hygiene, maintenance and a closing checklist

Feed quality is easily compromised. The tank and hatches must seal against rain, the interior must stay dry, and compartments must be cleaned thoroughly when the fleet changes product. A residue of a medicated ration in a compartment destined for another farm is a serious problem, so the body should be designed for washing and complete drainage, with no pockets where material can sit. Operators should keep spare gaskets, a membrane repair kit and a couple of hoses on hand, because a small leak in the discharge line can end a delivery and leave a farmer waiting with an empty silo.

Write the specification down before ordering. Record the products and their bulk densities, the compartments and the volume of each, the blower type and its pressure and flow rating, the discharge line length and lift, the eight-by-four chassis rating and wheelbase, and the axle loads with every compartment full. Confirm who supplies the tank and subframe and who signs off the complete vehicle. An E3 MAX bulk feed tanker that is specified around the real product, the worst discharge line and the legal payload will deliver reliably and return to the mill ready for the next load. One bought on tank volume alone will spend its life overweight, slow to unload or both.
