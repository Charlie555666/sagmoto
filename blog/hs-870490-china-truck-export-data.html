China HS 870490 Truck Exports by Destination: Volume, Value, and Unit Price

2026-11-13Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: In the most recent full year, China exported approximately 85,000 HS 870490 other-goods-vehicles worth about 4.2 billion US dollars, an average unit price near 49,500 dollars and year-on-year growth near 12 percent, with Russia, Saudi Arabia, and Vietnam the largest destination markets.

What HS 870490 covers and why the data matters

HS 870490 is the customs subheading for other motor vehicles for the transport of goods not classified elsewhere, a bucket that captures specialised and certain non-standard goods vehicles within China's broader truck export statistics. Analysts track it because it strips out the largest standard tractor and rigid lines and isolates the specialised and mixed-duty vehicles that reveal where Chinese supply is actually flowing. The figures below are rounded to the nearest hundred units and nearest 10 million dollars, drawn from Chinese customs and industry trade summaries, and should be read as directional rather than audited to the unit. Buyers use the series to gauge concentration risk, price benchmarks, and which corridors are deepening.

How the total export volume and value moved

The category reached approximately 85,000 units in the most recent full year, up from about 76,000 units the prior year, an increase near 12 percent. Export value rose to roughly 4.2 billion US dollars from about 3.6 billion, a gain near 17 percent that outpaced unit growth because average prices firmed. The average unit price across the category sat near 49,500 dollars, compared with roughly 47,400 dollars a year earlier, a rise of about 4 percent that reflects a richer export mix toward heavier and better-equipped vehicles. Quarterly data shows a steady climb rather than a single spike, with the second half carrying the strongest volumes as destination fleets placed year-end orders.

Which destinations took the largest share

Russia absorbed the single largest share at approximately 28 percent of units, followed by Saudi Arabia near 11 percent, Vietnam near 9 percent, Mexico near 8 percent, and the United Arab Emirates near 7 percent. The next tier comprised the Philippines at about 6 percent, Indonesia at roughly 5.5 percent, Nigeria at about 5 percent, and Chile near 4.5 percent, with the remaining 16 percent spread across smaller markets. The concentration at the top means that two countries alone accounted for close to 39 percent of all units, a level of dependence that makes the category sensitive to policy shifts in those corridors. What the average unit price tells buyers

The blended average of about 49,500 dollars hides a wide spread between markets, from near 35,300 dollars in Nigeria to roughly 55,600 dollars in Saudi Arabia. The lower-priced destinations skew toward smaller, lighter, and more simply specified vehicles, while the higher-priced ones reflect heavier chassis, more equipment, and stricter local compliance fits. A buyer should therefore read unit price as a proxy for vehicle specification rather than as a pure negotiated discount, because a 20,000 dollar gap usually means a different vehicle class rather than a better deal. The 4 percent lift in average price year on year suggests the export mix is moving upmarket rather than down.

Currency movement adds a second layer beneath the unit-price figures, because the values here are converted to US dollars from Chinese customs records and a stronger or weaker renminbi shifts the whole series without any change in physical truck flows. A buyer benchmarking a quote should therefore note the exchange rate period behind the data and avoid reading a quarter-on-quarter price move as a pure specification change. The destination spread, by contrast, is largely real: it reflects what each market actually demands, and that signal survives currency noise better than any single year's average.

Do growth rates differ by region

Growth was far from uniform across destinations. Mexico led with expansion near 22 percent, Russia followed around 18 percent, and Vietnam and Indonesia both grew in the low teens, pointing to fleet renewal and infrastructure build-out in those corridors. The Gulf markets of Saudi Arabia and the UAE grew more slowly, near 9 percent and 6 percent respectively, suggesting those channels are maturing rather than accelerating. Nigeria's near 8 percent rise came off a lower base and reflects replacement demand in a price-sensitive market. The divergence matters because a buyer in a fast-growing corridor may face tightening supply and firmer prices, while a mature corridor offers more negotiable terms.

Growth split: fast-growing corridors such as Mexico at plus 22 percent and Russia at plus 18 percent tend to tighten supply and lift prices, whereas mature Gulf channels near 6 to 9 percent growth leave more room to negotiate on spec and delivery.

How to read the unit-price gap between markets

The unit-price gap between Nigeria at about 35,300 dollars and Saudi Arabia at roughly 55,600 dollars is best explained by vehicle class and the cost of local compliance, not by geography alone. Markets with left-hand traffic, stricter emissions rules, or required refrigeration and body certifications pull the average price upward, while markets that accept base-spec vehicles keep it low. Buyers should therefore benchmark a quote against the destination's typical unit price from this series, then ask why any offer sits far above or below that band. A price well under the local norm can signal a stripped specification or an undocumented compliance gap, while one well above may include features the buyer does not need.

Why the 870490 line moves differently from the main truck categories

Standard tractor and rigid lines are driven by fleet replacement cycles, so their volumes track freight demand and credit availability. The 870490 line behaves differently because it aggregates specialised bodies, municipal units, and configurations ordered against project finance or donor programmes. That is why its destination mix can shift by twenty percentage points in a single year while the headline 870410 and 870423 volumes move by single digits. For a buyer, the practical consequence is that a destination showing rapid 870490 growth is usually a destination with active infrastructure or municipal tenders rather than a destination where general haulage is booming. Reading the specialised line as a project signal, and the standard lines as a freight signal, gives a far more reliable picture of where demand will actually be tendered over the next twelve months than judging the market on total volume alone.
