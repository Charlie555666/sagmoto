X6s Crane Truck Applications: Mounting Knuckle-Boom Cranes on a 6x4 Tipper Chassis

2026-10-30Industry Application8 min read

Mounting a knuckle-boom loader crane on a tipper chassis converts one 6x4 vehicle into a machine that both hauls aggregate and sets loads on site, and the SAGMOTO X6s 6x4 platform is well suited to this dual role because its 270-300 hp Cummins ISD or Weichai WP7 powerplant leaves useful reserve after the tipper body and crane are fitted. Contractors who currently run a separate tipper and a separate crane truck can consolidate two assignments onto a single chassis, cutting the number of units to licence, insure and park at the depot. The economics work best where the crane is used for perhaps 30 to 50 percent of the working day and the tipper body earns the rest, rather than where a dedicated crane is in constant rotation.

Why the X6s 6x4 Works as a Dual-Purpose Tipper and Crane Carrier

The X6s is built around a reinforced ladder frame originally developed for construction tipping duty, and that same frame provides the longitudinal stiffness a rear-mounted crane needs when it lifts at an offset radius. A 6x4 wheelbase in the 4,600 to 5,600 mm band positions the rear bogie so that the front axle retains at least 20 percent of gross weight while the crane is stowed, which keeps the unit legal for road travel between sites. The 13-tonne rear axles and the Fast 10JSD or 12JSD gearbox are already proven in tipper service, so the only structure added for crane duty is local reinforcement at the subframe and outrigger pick-up points. Because the cab, wiring loom and air system are shared with the standard tipper, parts commonality is high and a fleet already running X6s tippers can add crane bodies without stocking a separate spares profile.

Operators who want a pure tipper on some days and a crane on others can specify a detachable or demountable tipper, though most buyers accept a fixed steel body because it is simpler and cheaper to certify. The key advantage over a 4x2 crane truck is payload at the rear axle group: a 6x4 carries a heavier combined tipper-plus-crane mass within the same 25-tonne GVW envelope, which is why the X6s is chosen for 8-25 tonne-metre class cranes rather than the lighter 4x2 units.

Engine and PTO Options for Crane Hydraulics

A knuckle-boom crane draws its hydraulic power from a pump driven either by a gearbox-mounted power take-off or by a dedicated engine-driven pump, and the X6s supports both routes. The most common fitment is a gearbox PTO on the Fast 12JSD that drives a fixed or variable displacement pump rated between 45 and 90 litres per minute at 1,800 rpm, which is enough for cranes up to about 18 tonne-metres when the engine is held at crane idle. For larger 20-25 tonne-metre cranes, an engine-mounted PTO with a dedicated oil cooler is preferred because it lets the crane operate while the chassis is stationary with the gearbox in neutral, reducing clutch wear and heat load.

Engine choice affects crane duty as much as road performance. The Cummins ISD 270-300 hp unit delivers peak torque of around 1,000 to 1,150 Nm between 1,200 and 1,600 rpm, which suits a crane that needs steady mid-range output more than a high-revving profile. The Weichai WP7 option covers the same 270-300 hp band with comparable torque and is often selected for markets where Weichai service networks are stronger. Either way the alternator should be uprated to at least 150 A so that the crane's stabiliser warning lights, radio remote and work lamps do not flatten the battery during long static lifts.

Frame Rail Strength and Reinforcement for 8-25 Tonne-Metre Cranes

The standard X6s frame uses high-tensile side rails of roughly 280 to 320 mm depth with 8 to 10 mm wall thickness, which is adequate for a tipper but marginal for the local bending introduced by a crane subframe. The remedy is a bolted or welded subframe that spreads the crane's overturning moment across at least four frame cross-members rather than concentrating it at two. For cranes above 15 tonne-metres, fabricators add fishplates inside the rails over a 1,200 to 1,800 mm zone and reinforce the cross-member directly beneath the crane column. Outrigger mounts must pick up on the frame, never on the tipper body, and the front outriggers should reach forward of the rear axle to balance the load when lifting over the cab side.

Reinforce the frame where the crane column and outriggers attach, not the tipper body. A crane mounted only to the body will crack the body and void the tipper warranty within a few hundred cycles.

Chassis Weight Budget Calculation

Before ordering, a weight budget prevents the classic mistake of a crane so heavy that the tipper can no longer carry a useful load. Start from the 25,000 kg GVW limit and subtract the kerb items: chassis and cab about 6,800 kg, tipper body 2,200 to 3,000 kg depending on steel grade and length, crane and subframe 1,600 to 3,400 kg, fuel and driver about 600 kg, and a 10 percent reserve of 2,500 kg for uneven loads and accessories. The remainder is the permitted payload, which for a 13-18 tonne-metre crane typically lands near 8 to 9 tonnes of aggregate. Fleets in markets with a 24,000 kg limit should deduct a further tonne and may choose a shorter 4,200 mm wheelbase to save frame mass.

Stability at Working Radius

A loader crane's safe load falls sharply with radius, so the rated 25 tonne-metres at 2 metres becomes perhaps 6 tonne-metres at 8 metres, and the X6s must be stabilised on its outriggers for any lift beyond the vertical. The chassis itself contributes stability through its 2,500 mm track width and low centre of gravity when the tipper is empty, but the crane's own moment limiter is the final authority and should be set with the actual subframe and tyre data loaded by the installer. On soft ground a 6x4 with 11R22.5 tyres and 1,000 by 1,000 mm outrigger pads distributes roughly 4 tonnes per pad, so a ground bearing check is needed before lifting near trenches or fill.

Never lift on tyres alone with a 6x4 crane truck. Deploy all four outriggers on rated pads, and keep the limiter active even for short lifts near the cab.

Typical Jobs: Utility Poles, Precast, Machinery and Solar Farms

The combined tipper-crane X6s finds its strongest applications where material must be delivered and placed in one visit. Utility contractors set wooden and concrete poles of 9 to 15 metres by lifting them from the tipper bed and lowering them into the auger hole, removing the need for a separate pole trailer. Precast yards use the crane to offload beams and manholes at the lay-down area while the tipper brings the bedding gravel in the same trip. Machinery dealers deliver compact excavators and rollers and place them directly on site, and solar-farm builders carry mounting posts and battery boxes then set them along the array rows without a second machine.

Loader-Crane Legal Weight Distribution

Road legality depends on keeping each axle within its rating whether the crane is stowed or deployed, and the 6x4 layout helps because the rear bogie can absorb the crane mass near the trunnion while the front axle stays above its 7,000 kg limit. When the crane is folded and travelling, most of its mass sits between the rear axles, so a builder should place the tipper body centre of gravity slightly forward to balance the combined unit. National rules differ: in the Gulf and parts of Africa a 25,000 kg GVW with 10,000 kg on the front and 15,000 kg on the rear bogie is commonly accepted, while in the EU the same chassis must meet 8,000 and 11,500 kg limits and may need a longer wheelbase. A certified scale ticket at first build confirms the as-delivered distribution before the unit is registered.

Comparison With a Dedicated 4x2 Crane Carrier

A dedicated 4x2 crane carrier weighs less and can mount a slightly larger crane per tonne of chassis, but it cannot tip and it gives up off-road capability, so it suits dense urban work where a tipper is not needed. The X6s 6x4 trades some on-road efficiency for the ability to enter muddy quarry access roads and to deliver and place in a single movement, which is why plant hire firms and regional utilities prefer it. The lighter 4x2 X6 platform is an option where the crane stays below 10 tonne-metres and the body is a flatbed rather than a tipper, but it loses the dumping function that pays for the chassis on construction days. Buyers should weigh the share of crane versus tipper work: above roughly 40 percent crane use, a dedicated carrier may be cheaper overall, while below that the dual X6s wins on asset use.
