How to Import SAGMOTO Z3 Tractor Trucks to Paraguay: Specs, Taxes and Route Planning

2026-10-19Buying Guide12 min read

Paraguay is landlocked, and that decides the whole procurement plan

Paraguay is a landlocked country in the centre of South America, and that single geographical fact shapes every procurement decision a fleet makes about prime movers. There is no coastline, so a tractor built in Xi'an does not arrive by a single ocean voyage to a Paraguayan port. It arrives through a neighbouring country's port and crosses an international border by road, river or a combination of both before it reaches a base in Asuncion, Ciudad del Este or a soybean logistics hub in the south. For soybean and beef exporters moving product to the Atlantic, and for construction, fuel and container logistics operators serving the interior, the SAGMOTO Z3 520HP Cummins M13 tractor is a strong candidate, but it must be specified, routed and cleared with the inland geometry in mind rather than with a coastal-market assumption. This guide walks the Paraguayan buyer from engine specification through Mercosur duty, route planning, financing, customs documentation and the parts pipeline that keeps a tractor working far from the factory.

The dominant Paraguayan duty cycle for a tractor is the export corridor: grain and beef from the interior to the Atlantic, empty returns, and a secondary market in container and fuel distribution along the routes that feed the ports. These are long, mostly flat runs with the occasional grade, and they reward a relaxed, fuel-efficient powertrain over raw peak horsepower. The SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover range is built around exactly this profile, and the Z3 is the unit most Paraguayan fleet managers should shortlist first.

Step one: specify the Z3 for flat lowland and grade work

The first decision is technical, not commercial. The Z3 is configured as a 6x4 long-haul tractor with a 520 hp Cummins M13 engine that holds 2,500 Nm of torque between 1,000 and 1,400 rpm, paired with a Fast Gear 12JSD240TA twelve-speed manual transmission. The torque plateau is the number that matters: it means the driver can hold the engine inside the band where brake-specific fuel consumption is lowest across the flat soybean corridors, and it means the driveline can be geared so that motorway cruise sits at the bottom of the plateau rather than above it.

Two final-drive options cover Paraguayan work. A 3.7 ratio suits the flat export corridors between the interior and the southern border, where cruise economy dominates and gradeability is rarely challenged. A 4.1 to 4.3 ratio suits routes that climb toward the eastern hills or that pull heavier bulk combinations on softer secondary roads. Tyres are 315/80R22.5 in an 18-ply rating with a heat- and UV-resistant casing, because Paraguayan summer and unpaved yard surfaces are both hard on rubber. Specify the fifth-wheel height and chassis frame to suit the semi-trailer fleet already in service, and state the kingpin height and trailer type in the order.

Step two: Mercosur tariff, origin and licence plates

Paraguay is a full member of Mercosur, the Southern Common Market, alongside Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay, with associated states including Bolivia and Chile. This membership changes the duty arithmetic in two ways. First, vehicles of Mercosur origin benefit from the common external tariff treatment and, where a preferential origin certificate applies, reduced or zero internal duty on qualifying goods. A SAGMOTO tractor is of Chinese origin, so it is treated under the common external tariff when it enters through a Mercosur port and is then nationalised in Paraguay, but the unit is not exposed to the additional bilateral surcharges that non-member imports can face.

Second, the Mercosur member states share a standard licence-plate format and a framework for vehicle homologation and circulation, which simplifies the moment the truck crosses from Argentina or Brazil into Paraguay. The truck must still be nationalised and registered with the Paraguayan transit authority, and the paperwork must show the correct HS classification, the customs value and the origin declaration. Paraguay maintains its own import duty and VAT regime on top of the Mercosur framework, and the combined burden on a new heavy tractor typically lands in the region of 10 to 18 percent of CIF value once duty and VAT are stacked, with a used-vehicle importation regime that is far less favourable. Buying new therefore avoids the age-based surcharge and arrives with warranty intact.

Step three: route planning is the real cost driver

Because Paraguay has no port, the discharge port and the crossing route determine both cost and lead time. The two practical ocean gateways are Santos in Brazil, the largest container and RoRo port in South America, and Buenos Aires in Argentina, which is closer to the Paraguayan border and connects directly to the river system. A third option is the Hidrovia Paraguay-Parana, the inland waterway that carries barges from the Atlantic up the Parana and Paraguay rivers to Puerto Aguirre in Bolivia and to points near the southern Paraguayan border, which is especially attractive for bulk grain logistics that already move by water.

Each route trades distance against handling. Santos offers the most frequent sailings and the deepest RoRo capability but is the farthest from the border, so the overland leg is long. Buenos Aires is closer and connects to the river, but Argentine border transit can be slower and the cartage cost reflects the crossing. The river barge option is cheapest per tonne for bulk but slowest and most dependent on draft and seasonal water levels. A fleet moving soybeans may use the river for the bulk leg and a Z3 tractor-and-trailer for the final road distribution, which is exactly the mixed duty the Z3 is built for.

Step four: left-hand drive and cab specification

Paraguay drives on the right and registers left-hand-drive vehicles, which matches the standard SAGMOTO export configuration and avoids the RHD premium and lead-time penalty that right-hand-drive markets face. The cab should be specified for long, hot corridor work: a high-roof sleeper with an effective stationary air-conditioning system, good cab insulation and acceptable noise at 90 km/h. Paraguayan soybean corridors involve multi-day round trips, and a comfortable sleeper directly affects driver retention, which is a persistent cost for long-haul fleets in the region.

Specify the two-stage air filtration with a cyclonic pre-cleaner, because unpaved yards, harvest dust and the red soil of the interior cut main-element life hard without it. An uprated radiator core, a silicone hose set and a fan drive sized for sustained low-speed operation protect the engine during yard queuing and slow grading. None of these items costs much at order, and their absence shows up as downtime in the second year.

Step five: financing and insurance

Settle on a 30 percent telegraphic transfer deposit with 70 percent against bill of lading, or a 30/70 letter of credit at sight for first-time importers. The Z3 ticket is large enough that letter-of-credit confirmation charges of 1.0 to 1.6 percent are tolerable as a percentage, and document protection is worth more here than on a medium-duty unit. The contract must name the engine model, the 12JSD240TA transmission, the final drive, the fifth-wheel height, the tyre size, the cab type and colour, a stated lead time of 30 to 55 days, and a late-delivery penalty. State the discharge port, because that determines the routing documents the factory prepares.

Insurance is two lines, not one. Marine insurance at 110 percent of invoice value covers the sea and river leg and should be confirmed as included under CIF terms or arranged under FOB. Separate inland transit and the nationalisation risk with a Paraguayan broker, because the crossing leg and the border storage are where loss and damage actually occur. Paraguayan border storage and demurrage run USD 50 to USD 110 per day per unit, so a delayed document set is a direct cost, not a nuisance.

Step six: customs documentation and clearance

Paraguayan customs requires a coherent document set for nationalisation. Your clearing agent needs the commercial invoice, the packing list, the bill of lading or river waybill, the certificate of origin, the technical data sheet naming the Cummins M13 and its emissions level, the Mercosur transit paperwork where the unit crosses from Argentina or Brazil, and the import declaration with the correct HS code. Document errors are the main cause of frontier delay, and a truck sitting at the border accrues storage daily.

Step seven: parts pipeline and aftersales

The Z3 carries a 12 to 24 month or 150,000 to 250,000 km powertrain warranty by agreement. The Cummins M13 shares parts and diagnostics with the wider Cummins family supported across South America, and the 12JSD240TA is mechanically conventional and locally serviceable. Order a first-year critical kit with the truck: two filter sets, a belt set, a clutch kit, an alternator, a starter motor, a water pump, a turbocharger, and a set of wheel bearings and seals. For a five-unit fleet this runs roughly USD 9,000 to USD 14,000 and prevents a cheap-part wait grounding a tractor on a corridor where recovery is slow.

Conclusion

Importing a SAGMOTO Z3 tractor to Paraguay starts with engineering, not price. Choose the Cummins M13 and the 12JSD240TA, set the final drive for your corridor, and confirm left-hand drive and the fifth-wheel height. Then manage the commercial mechanics: name Santos, Buenos Aires or the Hidrovia as the discharge route, settle a 30/70 letter of credit or inspected transfer, allow 30 to 55 days of build, plan the inland crossing as a separate cartage with its own customs step, and clear the truck on a 10 to 18 percent duty-and-VAT band. A Z3 lands in Paraguay in the region of USD 80,000 to USD 102,000 all-in.

Against the used-European parc that supplies part of the regional market, the Z3's case is warranty cover, known configuration, no used-vehicle surcharge, and a parts kit that keeps a working tractor working where recovery is slow.
