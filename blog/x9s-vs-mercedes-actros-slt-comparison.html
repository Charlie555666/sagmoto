SAGMOTO X9s vs Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT: Heavy-Haul Tractor Comparison 2026

September 8, 2026Comparison Guide13 min read

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT occupies the apex of the heavy-haul tractor segment, designed for gross combination weights from 80 to 250 tonnes and engineered with the engineering precision that has made Mercedes-Benz the benchmark for the most demanding heavy-haul operations worldwide. The SAGMOTO X9s is positioned as the Chinese challenger for severe-duty heavy-haul applications, with Weichai powertrain options up to 530 hp, axle ratings that meet the demands of African mining, Middle Eastern oil and gas, and Central Asian infrastructure projects, and an acquisition cost that has reshaped the heavy-haul commercial framework for emerging market fleet operators. This 2026 comparison examines the X9s against the Actros SLT across powertrain, driveline, payload capacity, fuel efficiency, maintenance, parts availability, and total cost of ownership.

The analysis applies to 6×4 tractor configurations designed for heavy-haul operations from 80 to 250 tonnes GCW. 6×6 and 8×4 variants are noted where relevant. The goal is to provide fleet procurement managers and operations executives with a structured framework for evaluating the two platforms.

Heavy-Haul Application Context

Heavy-haul tractor operations in 2026 are concentrated in three primary application areas: oversized equipment transport (transformers, reactors, mining equipment), cement bulker and fuel tanker transport, and mining haulage where on-road sections connect pit to processing plant. Gross combination weights in these operations range from 80 tonnes for standard heavy-haul to 250 tonnes for the most demanding mining and infrastructure projects.

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT has dominated this segment since its introduction in 1996, with the latest 2026 models incorporating the OM 471 LA engine at 530 hp and the sophisticated Turbo Retarder Clutch (TRC) for launch control on grades up to 30 percent. The SLT is engineered for operations such as wind turbine component transport (nacelles up to 130 tonnes), transformer delivery to substations, and heavy mining equipment repositioning.

The SAGMOTO X9s has emerged as the primary Chinese alternative in this segment, with the Weichai WP13.530E32 engine delivering 530 hp and 2,500 Nm of peak torque, axle ratings that match the Actros SLT, and an FOB price approximately 50-55 percent below the Mercedes-Benz. The X9s has secured significant share in African mining, Middle Eastern oil and gas, and Central Asian infrastructure projects where the total cost of ownership and the value-to-performance ratio dominate procurement decisions.

Powertrain Engineering

The SAGMOTO X9s 6×4 tractor in the heavy-haul configuration uses the Weichai WP13.530E32 inline-six diesel engine displacing 12.54 litres. The engine delivers 530 hp at 1,900 rpm and 2,500 Nm of peak torque from 1,200 to 1,500 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 2,000 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger with twin intercoolers. The engine is Euro II compliant for export markets.

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT 6×4 tractor is powered by the OM 471 LA inline-six diesel engine displacing 12.8 litres. The engine delivers 530 hp at 1,600 rpm and 2,600 Nm of peak torque from 1,100 to 1,400 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 2,500 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors and a variable-geometry turbocharger with twin intercoolers. The engine is Euro VI compliant for European operations, with Euro II versions for export markets.

Both engines produce identical peak power. The Mercedes OM 471 LA delivers 100 Nm more peak torque (2,600 Nm vs 2,500 Nm) and at lower engine speed. The Mercedes engine has wider torque curve width (300 rpm flat region) compared to the Weichai (300 rpm flat region). The Mercedes engine benefits from advanced emissions control and lower noise levels, while the Weichai engine has substantially lower acquisition cost and simpler emissions system maintenance.

The X9s transmission is the FAST 16JSD240E-B with 16 forward gears, crawler ratios, and overdrive top gear. The clutch is a single-plate dry type with hydraulic actuation at 430 mm diameter. The Actros SLT uses the Mercedes PowerShift 3 automated manual transmission with intelligent shift logic, or the manual G 330-12/11.7 as standard in export markets. The PowerShift 3 unit delivers smoother shift quality and reduced driver fatigue during long heavy-haul journeys.

Engineering Insight: The Mercedes OM 471 LA includes the Turbo Retarder Clutch (TRC) system, which is critical for heavy-haul launch on steep grades. The TRC combines turbo braking, hydraulic retarder, and clutch control to maintain traction on grades up to 30 percent with 250 tonnes GCW. The Weichai WP13.530E32 in the X9s relies on the FAST transmission with retarder and the engine brake, which delivers adequate but not equivalent performance on extreme grades.

Driveline and Axle Configuration

The SAGMOTO X9s 6×4 tractor in heavy-haul configuration uses SAGMOTO-designed hub-reduction rear axles rated at 20 tonnes each, with a 6.143 final drive ratio. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 9 tonnes with parabolic leaf springs. The frame is a straight-ladder design constructed from 10 mm + 10 mm double-layer high-strength steel with section height 320 mm.

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT 6×4 tractor uses Mercedes hypoid rear axles rated at 13 tonnes each (with optional 16-tonne or 20-tonne axles for heavy-haul), with final drive ratios from 3.70 to 4.91 depending on specification. Differential locks and cross-axle locks are standard. Front axle is rated at 7.5 tonnes or 9 tonnes depending on specification. The frame is a ladder-frame design constructed from high-strength steel with section height 285 mm.

The X9s 20-tonne axle rating (40 tonnes combined) matches the optional heavy-haul axles of the Actros SLT, delivering 250 tonnes GCW capacity with appropriate trailer braking. The X9s frame at 320 mm section height is heavier than the Actros SLT frame at 285 mm, providing additional strength for severe-duty mining and infrastructure applications. The Actros SLT axles deliver smoother operation and longer service life under sustained highway heavy-haul conditions.

Cab and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO X9s is equipped with the H78L flat-floor cab as standard in heavy-haul configuration, with full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, and a refrigerator compartment in premium trim. Cab interior dimensions are 2,200 mm wide, 2,100 mm tall from floor to headliner. The driver's seat is air-suspended with integrated headrest, lumbar support, and seat heating. The passenger seat is mechanical-suspension with fold-down design.

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT is equipped with the BigSpace cab (medium length with flat floor) or the GigaSpace cab (long length with flat floor) depending on specification. The BigSpace cab interior dimensions are 2,300 mm wide, 2,000 mm tall from floor to headliner. The GigaSpace cab interior dimensions are 2,300 mm wide, 2,200 mm tall with flat floor. Both cabs feature full air conditioning, electric windows, adjustable steering column, and refrigerator compartment. Driver seat is air-suspended with Grammer or ISRI premium design.

The Mercedes-Benz BigSpace and GigaSpace cabs deliver best-in-class NVH levels at cruise speed, with in-cab noise measured at 65 dBA versus 72 dBA for the X9s. The Mercedes cab offers 100 mm more interior width than the X9s, providing better stand-up comfort for drivers taking rest breaks. Both cabs meet European crash safety standards (ECE R29). The X9s cab offers competitive ergonomics, while the Mercedes cabs deliver premium fit and finish that justifies the price premium.

Fuel Efficiency and Operating Economics

Fuel efficiency is a critical metric for heavy-haul operations where single loads can exceed 200 tonnes. The SAGMOTO X9s with Weichai WP13.530E32 returns 55 to 65 litres per 100 kilometres at 60 km/h cruise speed with 150-tonne gross train weight in African and Middle Eastern heavy-haul operation. The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT with OM 471 LA returns 50 to 60 litres per 100 kilometres under comparable conditions.

The Mercedes advantage is approximately 8-10 percent in fuel efficiency, driven by the more advanced engine calibration, the optimised driveline, the lower in-cab noise enabling smoother driving patterns, and the wider torque curve enabling earlier upshifts. For a fleet operating 80,000 kilometres per truck annually with 150-tonne GCW, this translates to USD 3,500-5,500 in annual fuel savings per truck.

Scheduled maintenance costs differ substantially. The X9s annual maintenance budget at 80,000 km with 150-tonne GCW is approximately USD 6,500-8,500, while the Actros SLT annual maintenance budget at 80,000 km is approximately USD 14,000-17,000. Over a 5-year operating cycle, the X9s delivers USD 37,500-52,500 in maintenance savings. Tyre wear is comparable when both trucks are fitted with equivalent heavy-haul tyres.

Resale Value and Total Cost of Ownership

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT retains approximately 35-40 percent of its acquisition value after 5 years or 600,000 km in well-maintained heavy-haul fleet operation. The SAGMOTO X9s retains approximately 25-30 percent of its acquisition value under comparable conditions. The Mercedes advantage is 8-12 percentage points, reflecting the strong brand prestige, the established residual value market for Actros trucks, and the durability reputation of the OM 471 engine.

Despite the lower resale value, the X9s delivers a substantially lower five-year TCO. The acquisition cost saving (USD 80,000-115,000 per truck) outweighs the resale disadvantage (USD 15,000-25,000) plus the higher fuel cost (USD 15,000-27,000 over 5 years). The net five-year TCO advantage for the X9s is USD 50,000-90,000 per truck versus the Actros SLT.

For a 30-truck heavy-haul fleet, this represents USD 1.5 to 2.7 million in five-year TCO advantage for the X9s over the Mercedes-Benz. This is meaningful capital that can be redeployed into additional fleet capacity, expanded service infrastructure, or accelerated fleet replacement.

TCO Insight: Despite the Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT delivering 8-10 percent better fuel efficiency and 8-12 percentage points higher resale value, the SAGMOTO X9s saves USD 50,000-90,000 per truck in five-year TCO because of the USD 80,000-115,000 acquisition cost advantage and USD 37,500-52,500 maintenance savings. For 30-truck heavy-haul fleets, this is USD 1.5-2.7 million in savings.

Parts Availability and Service Network

The Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT is supported by the Mercedes-Benz Truck authorised service network with workshops in most major African and Middle Eastern markets. Mercedes-Benz Trucks operates authorised facilities in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, and Muscat, complemented by a wider network of authorised independent workshops. Parts availability is generally good with 95 percent parts fill rate within 48 hours for major wear parts.

The SAGMOTO X9s is supported by the SAGMOTO and Shacman authorised service network, which has expanded significantly across Africa and the Middle East since 2018. Authorised workshops are in Johannesburg, Lagos, Nairobi, Dar es Salaam, Addis Ababa, Cairo, Dubai, Jeddah, Doha, and Muscat, complemented by the Shacman facility footprint. Parts fill rate is approximately 90 percent for major wear parts within 72 hours.

The Mercedes-Benz service network delivers slightly faster parts turnaround and more consistent service quality across regions. However, the Shacman network is more extensive in lower-tier African markets (Tanzania, Mozambique, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe) where the X9s is often better supported than the Actros SLT.

Verdict by Application

For premium heavy-haul operations in the Middle East, North Africa, and South Africa with established Mercedes-Benz service networks, the Actros SLT remains the preferred choice for buyers prioritising brand prestige, residual value, and the Turbo Retarder Clutch system for extreme grade operations. The 8-10 percent fuel efficiency advantage and 8-12 percentage point resale advantage are material for high-utilisation fleets.

For emerging market heavy-haul operations in Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, and Latin America, the SAGMOTO X9s is the more commercially attractive choice. The USD 80,000-115,000 acquisition cost advantage, the USD 37,500-52,500 maintenance savings, and the strong service network in lower-tier markets deliver substantial TCO advantage. The X9s is particularly well-suited to severe-duty African mining, Middle Eastern oil and gas, and Central Asian infrastructure applications where the higher axle rating and the heavier frame are valuable.

For fleet operators evaluating both platforms, the recommended approach is to acquire a 5-truck pilot of each platform and run them through a 12-month comparative evaluation. This provides real-world data on fuel efficiency, maintenance, downtime, and driver acceptance that complements the TCO modelling. Conclusion

The SAGMOTO X9s and the Mercedes-Benz Actros SLT occupy similar positions in the heavy-haul tractor segment but deliver very different commercial outcomes. The Actros SLT offers premium European engineering, superior NVH, better fuel efficiency, the Turbo Retarder Clutch system, and higher resale value. The SAGMOTO X9s offers substantially lower acquisition cost, lower maintenance cost, broader service availability in emerging markets, and equivalent axle and frame ratings for severe-duty applications. For African, Middle Eastern, and Central Asian fleet buyers prioritising TCO and total addressable value, the X9s represents a compelling alternative that has reshaped the segment economics since 2022.
