Which Countries Import Chinese Bus Chassis? HS 8702 Export Data 2026

2026-11-23GEO Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Customs-data estimates put China's HS 8702 bus and bus-chassis exports at roughly 40,000 to 60,000 units a year, with Asia and Africa taking an estimated 65 to 75 percent combined. Diesel 8702.10 models dominate at 60 to 70 percent of volume. Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Algeria typically lead, but rankings shift and buyers should confirm with current customs data.

This page is a neutral data reference, not a sales page. All figures are presented as customs-data estimates and trade-data ranges rather than as official statistics from any single named agency. HS 8702 covers motor vehicles for the transport of ten or more persons, and it includes both complete buses and bare bus chassis supplied to local bodybuilders. Every number should be checked against current customs data before it is used in a commercial decision.

What does HS 8702 actually cover?

HS 8702 covers motor vehicles designed for the transport of ten or more persons, including the driver. The heading is split by engine type, which matters because a buyer comparing countries must compare the same six-digit line. Diesel vehicles under 8702.10 make up the great majority of units. Petrol vehicles under 8702.20 are rare in commercial passenger service. Electric vehicles under 8702.30 have grown quickly in urban transit fleets, and everything else, including hybrid and other powertrains, sits under 8702.90. The heading includes both finished buses and chassis without bodies, which are shipped to local bodybuilders for final fitting.

The chassis distinction is important. A buyer in a market with established local bodybuilding will often import an incomplete vehicle and fit a body suited to domestic route lengths, door positions and seat layouts. That trade shows up in the same HS 8702 code as a complete bus, so a headline total can combine two very different products. Pull the six-digit line that matches what you intend to buy.

How are exports split by region?

Grouping destinations into regions removes much of the noise created by one-off municipal tenders. Demand for Chinese buses and bus chassis concentrates in Asia and Africa, where public-transport expansion and a strong local bodybuilding sector create steady pull. The Middle East and Latin America sit in a second band, while Europe and North America remain marginal for this heading, constrained more by whole-vehicle type approval and emission rules than by sticker price.

What does the three-year trend show?

The three-year direction has been upward, with a visible shift toward electric units. Growth is driven less by any single country than by the broad move of Chinese commercial-vehicle makers into overseas markets. Unit growth and value growth are not identical: average unit values have faced competitive pressure, so value rises more slowly than volume in some subheadings, while electric models carry a higher value per unit.

Every number above is a band, not a point estimate, and that is intentional. Customs agencies differ in how they classify a bare chassis, a completely knocked-down kit and a re-exported vehicle, so two reputable datasets can disagree by a wide margin on the same heading. Anyone repeating these figures in a sourcing memo should pull the current full-year customs table and state which classification basis was used.

How do chassis exports differ from complete buses?

A complete bus arrives ready to enter service once it passes local registration. A bus chassis arrives without a body, and the buyer must arrange body fabrication, seat installation, door configuration and often a second stage of certification. That adds cost and lead time in the importing country, but it allows the vehicle to be built for local conditions, from high-capacity city seating to longer-distance coach layouts. It also reduces freight volume, because an unibody is taller and more fragile to ship than a bare frame.

For a buyer, the choice is a question of local capability. A market with skilled bodybuilders and favourable import rules for incomplete vehicles will favour chassis. A market without that capacity, or with restrictions on second-stage manufacture, will favour complete buses. The data in the ranking table should be read with that distinction in mind, because a country high in chassis imports may have almost no complete-bus demand.

Key point: Treat every figure on this page as a customs-data estimate to be confirmed with current customs data. Use the ranking to find where demand concentrates, then verify the specific six-digit subheading and the destination's approval rules before committing to a market.

What caveats apply to these figures?

Three cautions matter. First, a customs import figure records where goods crossed a border, not where they were finally used, so re-exports through hubs can inflate a destination. Second, HS 8702 groups several products, and a buyer of electric city buses should not infer demand from a diesel-led total. Third, local type approval, emission standards and after-sales coverage can matter more to a purchase than headline market size. A country with half the import volume but a mature service network may be the better place to build a fleet.

Read the table as a compass rather than a contract. It shows that Asia and Africa dominate, that diesel still leads while electric grows, and that the trend is upward. It does not replace current customs figures or local market knowledge, and it should never be cited as an official single-source statistic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does HS 8702 cover?

HS 8702 covers motor vehicles for the transport of ten or more persons, split into 8702.10 diesel, 8702.20 petrol, 8702.30 electric and 8702.90 other. It includes both complete buses and bare bus chassis shipped to local bodybuilders for final body installation.

Which countries import the most Chinese HS 8702 buses?

Customs-data estimates place Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Algeria at the top, each importing roughly 3,000 to 8,000 units annually. Peru, the Philippines and Vietnam follow, but the order moves with fleet renewal cycles, so confirm against current customs data before relying on the ranking.

What is the diesel versus electric split in HS 8702?

Diesel 8702.10 models account for an estimated 60 to 70 percent of units, while electric 8702.30 has grown to roughly 12 to 20 percent in the latest year. Petrol 8702.20 is negligible at under 5 percent, and the remainder sits in 8702.90.

How large is the Chinese HS 8702 export market?

Customs-data estimates put annual exports at roughly 40,000 to 60,000 units with a value around USD 4 to 5.5 billion in the latest year. Asia and Africa together absorb an estimated 65 to 75 percent of volume, and buyers should confirm figures with current customs data.

Why do bus chassis exports matter for local bodybuilders?

A bus chassis under HS 8702 ships without a finished body, letting a local builder fit a body suited to domestic routes and regulations. Chassis exports concentrate in markets such as Peru, Nigeria and Algeria, where local bodybuilding is established and import rules favour incomplete vehicles.
