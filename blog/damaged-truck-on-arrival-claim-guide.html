What Should You Do If Your SAGMOTO Truck Arrives Damaged?

2026-10-21Buyer Decision Guide10 min read

Quick Answer

Quick answer: Inspect the truck before signing the delivery receipt and photograph every dent, scratch and missing part. Notify your marine cargo insurer and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading within 3-7 days, obtain a joint survey report, and file a marine cargo claim with the survey findings and bill of lading attached.

Why Arrival Damage Happens

A SAGMOTO truck is built to a reinforced 320 mm frame and Hardox-rated bodies, but ocean transit is a different hazard from the road. Most arrival damage is not manufacturing defect — it is handling and stowage damage during loading, the sea voyage, and unloading at the destination port. Understanding the failure points helps you both claim correctly and, next time, prevent the loss. The two dominant shipping methods produce different damage patterns, and the pattern tells you who is liable.

Key point: Containerised chassis arrive with crush and forklift damage at the ports, while RORO (roll-on/roll-off) units more often show tyre flat-spots, battery drain, and corrosion from sea spray — the pattern is your first clue to the cause and the liable party.

Which Documents Prove Pre-Shipment Condition?

The single most powerful document is the pre-shipment inspection report (PSI) issued by an independent surveyor at the Chinese factory or port of loading. It records the truck's condition, mileage, and any existing marks with dated photographs before it leaves SAGMOTO custody. Pair this with the clean bill of lading — a document the carrier issues confirming they received the goods in apparent good order. If the bill of lading is "clean" (no clauses noting damage) but the truck arrives dented, the carrier cannot later claim the damage pre-existed. A "claused" bill of lading, by contrast, already notes the defect and shifts the burden onto the shipper. Keep the commercial invoice, packing list, and insurance certificate alongside these, because together they establish both value and condition.

How Do You File a Marine Cargo Claim?

Filing is a sequence, not a single form. First, notify your cargo insurer and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading within the policy window — typically 3-7 days of arrival. Second, request a joint survey: an appointed surveyor inspects the unit with the carrier's agent present, producing a survey report with cause, extent, and repair cost estimate. Third, obtain repair quotations from a local authorised workshop using genuine SAGMOTO parts pricing, because the insurer settles on documented local repair cost, not guesswork. Fourth, submit the full claim package — survey report, photographs, bill of lading, invoice, insurance certificate, and repair quotes — to the insurer. The carrier's liability is capped under the Hague-Visby Rules at roughly 666.67 special drawing rights per package or unit, so for a complete vehicle the cargo policy, not the carrier, is usually the real payer.

What Does Marine Cargo Insurance Actually Cover?

Marine cargo cover is written on Institute Cargo Clauses (ICC). ICC A is the widest — all risks of loss or damage except a few named exclusions, and it is what most fleet buyers should hold for a vehicle shipment. ICC B covers named perils including fire, sinking, and collision but excludes theft and many handling losses. ICC C is the narrowest, covering only major casualties like vessel sinking or fire, and is unsuitable for a finished vehicle. For a truck, ICC A is the only clause that reliably pays for the dented fender, the crushed headlight, or the forklift-pierced radiator that are the everyday arrival claims. The policy value should be the CIF (cost, insurance, freight) amount plus a margin, so the settlement restores you rather than leaving a shortfall.

Key point: Always ship under ICC A "all risks" cover set at 110% of the CIF invoice value; ICC B or ICC C will reject the routine handling and theft losses that account for most arrival-damage claims on finished vehicles.

Should I Sign the Delivery Receipt If I See Damage?

No — signing a clean delivery receipt is the fastest way to lose your claim. The receipt is the carrier's evidence that they delivered the goods in good order, and a clean signature forfeits your right to hold them liable for visible damage. If you find faults at unloading, write "received damaged — subject to survey" on the receipt, note the nature and location of each defect, and have the driver or terminal agent countersign. For concealed damage discovered only after unpacking a container, you still have a claim, but you must notify the insurer within the policy window and preserve the packaging and lashing as evidence until the survey is complete. Photograph the truck and its surroundings before it is moved from the delivery point.

Who Pays — Insurer, Carrier, or Supplier?

Liability splits by cause. The marine cargo insurer pays for fortuitous loss or damage covered under ICC A, which is the broadest and most common payer. The carrier pays only within the strict Hague-Visby limits and only where they caused the loss through negligent handling — and they routinely escape via the clean-bill defence. The supplier (Shaanxi Fenghan Trading) is liable only for pre-existing manufacturing or packing defects proven against the pre-shipment inspection report; they are not liable for transit handling. In practice, a well-documented ICC A claim settles with the insurer, who may then subrogate against the carrier. Knowing this split stops buyers wasting weeks chasing the wrong party.

How Can You Prevent Damage on the Next Shipment?

Prevention is cheaper than any claim. Specify a lashing specification in the sales contract: trucks must be secured with rated chains or webbing to container or RORO deck anchor points, with wheel chocks on all wheels and a minimum of four restraint points per axle. Set the fuel tank to roughly one-quarter full (enough for unloading, not a fire-load surplus), disconnect the battery to avoid parasitic drain and electrical fault, and fit desiccant packs inside the cab and body to control condensation on long, humid voyages. For container shipments, require a pre-stow photograph showing lashing, and for RORO require documented underdeck stowage away from the sea-chest to limit corrosion. Timeline: From Arrival to Settlement

A realistic claim timeline runs as follows: day 0, truck arrives and you clause the receipt and photograph; days 1-7, you notify insurer and supplier and book the joint survey; days 3-14, the surveyor reports and the workshop prices repairs; days 14-30, you submit the full package; days 30-90, the insurer assesses and settles, often by bank transfer for the agreed amount or by directing the repair cost to the workshop. Delays almost always trace to a missing document — usually the pre-shipment report or a claused receipt — so assembling the file completely on day one is the single biggest accelerator. If the insurer subrogates against the carrier, that recovery sits with the insurer and does not affect your settlement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately if my SAGMOTO truck arrives damaged?

Immediately inspect the truck before signing and clause the delivery receipt with "received damaged — subject to survey" plus a countersignature, then photograph every fault; this preserves your claim because a clean signature forfeits carrier liability for visible damage.

Who pays for the damage — the insurer, the carrier, or the supplier?

The marine cargo insurer under ICC A pays for fortuitous loss in roughly 80-90% of cases, the carrier pays only within Hague-Visby limits of about 666.67 SDR per unit for proven handling fault, and the supplier is liable only for pre-existing defects shown on the pre-shipment report.

How long do I have to file a marine cargo claim?

You must notify your insurer and Shaanxi Fenghan Trading within 3 to 7 days of arrival and submit the full claim package, including the joint survey report and repair quotations, typically within 14 to 30 days per the policy wording to avoid rejection.

What documents are required to support a damage claim?

You need five core documents: the clean or claused bill of lading, the pre-shipment inspection report, the insurance certificate, dated photographs of every fault, and local repair quotations using genuine parts pricing to prove the settled amount.

How can I prevent truck damage during ocean shipping?

Specify in the contract a lashing plan with four restraint points per axle and wheel chocks, disconnect the battery, set fuel to one-quarter, add desiccant packs, and require underdeck RORO stowage or a pre-stow photograph for containers to cut loss by over 70%.
