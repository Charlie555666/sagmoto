How to Import Chinese Trucks into Peru in 2026: Callao Port, Customs and Homologation

📅 2026-11-06 ✍️ Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd. 📖 11 min read
Direct answer: To import a Chinese truck into Peru in 2026: (1) confirm the model passes MTC homologation (emissions + safety type-approval); (2) order with correct commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and certificate of origin; (3) ship to the Port of Callao; (4) clear SUNAT customs paying import duty plus 18% IGV on CIF+duty; (5) register and plate the unit. Specify left-hand drive and the current emission tier at order. Buying through the authorized exporter ensures documentation and homologation support are right the first time.

Peru is one of Latin America's steadier truck markets. Mining drives heavy demand in the highlands and south, construction and reconstruction keep dump and cargo units busy in Lima and the coast, and a growing consumer economy fuels distribution fleets. For buyers priced out of European premium trucks — or simply seeking better capital efficiency — Chinese-manufactured trucks have become a mainstream, rational choice. But "China cheap" becomes "Peru problem" if the import is done without the homologation and documentation discipline Peruvian customs require.

This guide walks the 2026 import process end to end, then matches SAGMOTO platforms to Peruvian duty cycles. It is practical, not promotional: the steps that get a truck cleared at Callao are the same regardless of brand.

Step 1 — Confirm Homologation Before You Pay a Deposit

Peru's Ministerio de Transportes y Comunicaciones (MTC) requires new vehicles to hold type-approval for emissions and safety. This is the gate that stops non-compliant units at the border. Before ordering:

  • Confirm the exact model + variant is homologable for 2026 (emission tier and safety).
  • Specify left-hand drive — Peru drives on the right.
  • Lock the emission standard (commonly Euro 4/5 for new units) at order; it cannot be cheaply changed after build.
Deposit rule: never pay a production deposit until the model's homologation status for Peru is confirmed in writing. A non-homologated truck is a very expensive paperweight in a Callao warehouse.

Peruvian customs (SUNAT) is document-driven. The supplier must provide:

Step 3 — Ship to the Port of Callao

Most units arrive at the Port of Callao, Peru's principal maritime gateway near Lima. Plan for:

  • Ocean transit from a Chinese port (typically ~30–45 days depending on routing).
  • Pre-shipment inspection photos/video from the supplier — your evidence the unit left in spec.
  • Marine insurance to CIF (cost, insurance, freight) so the declared value includes landed cost for duty calculation.

Buyers in the south or highlands should budget inland transport from Callao separately — a truck that clears customs in Lima still has to reach Arequipa, Cusco or the mining belt.

Step 4 — Clear SUNAT Customs and Pay Taxes

At Callao, SUNAT assesses duties on the CIF value. The typical cost stack:

Cost elementBasisNote
Import duty (ad valorem)% of CIF by HS codeVaries; trade-agreement preference may reduce
IGV (VAT)18% of (CIF + duty)General Sales Tax, applies to most imports
Municipal / other leviesMinor, port-specificConfirm with your agent
Storage / handlingCallao dwell timeAvoid delays — storage accrues daily

The single most common budgeting mistake is forgetting that IGV is calculated on CIF plus duty, not on the truck's invoice alone. Build the full stack before you commit, and use a Peruvian customs agent who files the HS code correctly — misclassification is the fastest way to a hold.

Step 5 — Register, Plate and Operate

Once cleared, the unit is registered and plated through the transport authority, then enters service. Keep the homologation certificate and import paperwork on file — they are needed for future resale and for any road-side or insurance verification.

Common Pitfalls That Delay or Block Clearance

Most Peruvian import problems are avoidable and fall into a short list:

  • Homologation assumed, not confirmed. A buyer orders a variant the MTC has not approved for 2026, and the unit sits at Callao. Confirm the exact model+variant before deposit.
  • Documentation mismatch. The B/L consignee, invoice buyer and RUC (tax ID) do not align, and SUNAT holds the file. Align all party names before sailing.
  • HS code guessed. Misclassification triggers a re-assessment, penalty risk and storage accrual. Use a Peruvian agent to lock the correct code for your model.
  • Duty/IGV under-budgeted. Forgetting IGV on CIF+duty blows the financing plan at the worst moment. Build the full tax stack first.
  • Drive-side wrong. A right-hand-drive unit cannot be registered in Peru. Specify LHD explicitly.
