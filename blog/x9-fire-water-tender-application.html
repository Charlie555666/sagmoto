X9 Fire Water Tender: Specifying an All-Wheel-Drive Light Support Chassis

2026-10-30Industry Application8 min read

Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd., the Xi'an headquartered exporter behind the SAGMOTO badge, configures the X9 4x4 light chassis for municipal and wildland fire support bodies built on the 160 horsepower Yuchai YC4E platform. The X9 spans a 4.5 to 25 tonne gross combination weight envelope, which lets a body-builder fit anything from a compact 3,000 litre village tender to an 8,000 litre plantation unit without changing the cab and drivetrain. Fire services in rural, forestry and plantation regions choose the X9 because its all-wheel drive reaches firebreaks and access tracks that a two-wheel-drive municipal appliance cannot. This article specifies the water tank classes, the pump and PTO interface, the winch and clearance needs, the crew cab and tropical build, the 4x4 advantage over a 6x4 municipal chassis, and the body-builder dimensions an operator should request. Fleets comparing all-terrain platforms can review the SAGMOTO off-road 4x4 mining trucks range for heavier duties beyond the fire role.

Why the X9 4x4 suits fire support roles

A fire support vehicle spends more time on ungraded tracks, through shallow drifts and across firebreaks than on sealed road, and that is exactly where a 4x4 light chassis earns its place. The X9's selectable all-wheel drive and 160 horsepower Yuchai YC4E give usable low-speed control for creeping along a smoking plantation road or a forestry break without boiling the clutch, and its compact footprint fits the narrow access gates that exclude a larger appliance. For first-response in villages and on estates the X9 carries enough water to knock down a start before the heavy pumper arrives, and its lower purchase and running cost put an appliance in depots that could not fund a full-size unit. The 4.5 to 25 tonne GCW band also lets the same chassis serve as a light rescue or a water bowser by swapping the body, which simplifies fleet training and spares holding for a small service.

Water tank capacity classes on the 4.5-25T GCW

On the X9 the practical tank envelope runs from 3,000 litres on the light 4.5 to 9 tonne chassis to 8,000 litres on the heavier 18 to 25 tonne variant, with 5,000 and 6,000 litre builds the common municipal and plantation sizes. The tank is usually a polypropylene or stainless module mounted behind the cab, and its fill and dump valves, the pump suction and the hose reel all hang off the same frame rails, so the chosen capacity drives the wheelbase and the rear axle rating. A 3,000 litre unit stays within the lighter licence class and threads village lanes, while an 8,000 litre unit needs the heavier axle and a longer wheelbase but covers a plantation sector without a refill. Match the capacity to the response radius, because a tank too large for the track cannot reach the fire and a tank too small sends the crew back for water mid-incident. Keep the tank low and baffled so the moving water does not shift the handling on a side slope, because a half-full 8,000 litre load sloshing on a firebreak is a rollover risk the driver cannot ignore.

Because the tank weight dominates the payload, the body-builder should model the full-water axle split before cutting steel, and the heavier tanks should use a lighter polypropylene shell rather than steel so more of the permitted weight stays as water instead of as the vessel.

Pump and PTO drive interface

The pump is driven through a power take-off off the gearbox or engine, and the X9 interface supports a side or rear PTO rated for a centrifugal pump moving roughly 1,500 to 3,000 litres per minute at the pressures a hose reel and a monitor need. The body-builder must align the PTO output speed and rotation with the pump catalogue figure, because a mismatch overspeeds the pump or starves it and shortens its life. Plumbing runs from the tank through a strainer and a non-return valve to the pump, then to the discharges, and the chassis wiring must carry the pump engagement interlock so the truck cannot move with the PTO live. Confirm the PTO engages only in neutral or park, and that the cab has the pump pressure gauge and the engine governor override for sustained pump-and-roll if the service doctrine allows it.

Front winch and high ground clearance access

A front winch rated around 5 to 9 tonnes is the single most useful recovery aid on a fire tender, because a water-laden 4x4 that slides off a firebreak rarely has room to be recovered by another appliance. Mount the winch on a structured front bumper with the fairlead clear of the number plate and the radiator, and route the control to the cab and a remote so the operator stays clear of the cable. Ground clearance matters as much as the winch: specify taller tyres within the gearing limit, protect the diff and transfer case, and keep the pump and tank low and centred so the higher ride does not push the centre of gravity into a rollover on a side slope. Firebreaks and plantation roads are graded loose, so the X9's approach and departure angles should be checked against the typical ditch and log the crew will cross on the way in.

Crew cab and tropical dry-season specification

The crew cab for a fire tender carries a driver and two to five firefighters depending on the body, and the X9 offers a single or crew-cab option with the rear seats folded to stow breathing sets and packs. For tropical and dry-season duty the specification should add an upgraded cooling package, a secondary battery for the pump lights and radio, a cabin fresh-air filter against smoke, and a sunshield or white paint to cut heat load during a long standby. Dust seals on the doors and the air intake protect the engine on the return from a fire, and a roof-mounted beacon bar and rear work lights are mandatory for night and smoke operations. Specify the cab to seat the whole team in harness so no one rides in the body, because a firefighter in the tank compartment is a casualty waiting to happen.

Seat the whole crew in the cab in harness and never let anyone ride in the tank or pump module, because the slosh and the terrain make the body compartment a casualty risk on a firebreak.

Dry-season specification also means extra fuel and a longer service interval between filters, because a fire season keeps the tender running long shifts far from a base, and a clogged filter at the wrong moment strands the only appliance in the sector.

4x4 versus 6x4 municipal chassis in rural response

A 6x4 municipal appliance carries more water and a bigger pump, but its length, wheelbase and weight keep it on sealed or well-graded roads, and it cannot follow a fire up a plantation track or across a washed-out bridge that a 4x4 clears. The X9 4x4 trades payload for reach: it arrives first, knocks down the start, and protects the access while the heavy unit is summoned over the long way round. For rural response time the decisive factor is the distance the appliance can travel off the main road in the first ten minutes, and there the lighter 4x4 wins by reaching the fire instead of staging at the gate. Many services pair the two: the X9 as the rapid attack and the 6x4 as the sustained pumper, and the X9's lower cost lets a district field two rapid units for the price of one heavy one.

Body-builder interface dimensions

The chassis is supplied with a body-builder manual giving the frame rail section, the mounting hole pattern, the PTO pad, the electrical and air take-offs and the permissible rear overhang, and these dimensions decide whether a standard tank and pump module will bolt on or needs a custom subframe. Provide the builder the wheelbase, the cab-to-axle dimension and the GVW so the tank sits within the axle loads and the rear overhang stays legal, and confirm the exhaust, the battery and the filler positions before cutting the module. The X9 keeps the same rail height across the 4.5 to 25 tonne variants, which lets one tank tooling serve several build weights and reduces the builder's setup cost. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading issues the interface drawing with the VIN so the builder works from the actual unit rather than a generic model, avoiding the on-site surprises that delay a fire order.

Specifying and ordering the build

To specify an X9 fire tender, fix the tank capacity and the pump flow first, because those two numbers drive the axle rating, the wheelbase and the PTO size, then choose the cab style, the winch and the tropical package against the duty climate. Send the body-builder interface drawing to your tank and pump supplier before committing, and confirm the local fire standards for beacon, labelling and pump certification so the finished appliance passes acceptance.
