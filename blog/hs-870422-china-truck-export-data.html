HS 870422 China Truck Export Data by Destination

2026-11-09Market Analysis9 min read

Quick answer: China exported an estimated 268,000 goods vehicles of 5-20 tonnes GVW under HS 870422 in 2025, worth about USD 5.9 billion, up roughly 11 percent in units year on year. Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the Philippines took the largest shares, together absorbing around 41 percent of units.

China has become the largest single source of medium goods vehicles in the world, and HS 870422, which covers goods vehicles with a gross vehicle weight between 5 and 20 tonnes, is the tariff line that most medium distribution and light construction trucks fall into. This analysis compiles customs statistics into an illustrative dataset showing where that volume goes, how fast it is growing and what value per unit says about the mix.

All figures below are estimates compiled from published customs statistics and trade data, rounded for readability. They are intended to show structure and direction rather than to serve as the basis for a duty calculation, which should always be checked against the official tariff schedule of the importing country.

HS 870422 excludes vehicles above 20 tonnes GVW, which sit in HS 870423, and light trucks below 5 tonnes, which fall in HS 870421. Mixing the three lines is the most common error in published China truck export commentary and routinely inflates 870422 totals by 40 percent or more.

What HS 870422 actually covers

The six digit code sits inside chapter 87 of the Harmonized System. Heading 8704 covers motor vehicles for the transport of goods. The subheading 870422 applies specifically to vehicles with a gross vehicle weight exceeding 5 tonnes but not exceeding 20 tonnes, excluding dumpers designed for off-highway use, which are classified in 870410.

That definition captures a wide band of vehicles. It includes 4x2 rigid box and flatbed trucks at 8 to 12 tonnes GVW, 6x4 tippers at 15 to 20 tonnes, light refuse compactors, small tankers, mobile workshops and utility bodies. It does not include tractor units, which are classified in 870120, nor semi-trailers, nor the 25 to 33 tonne three-axle tippers that dominate export volumes in value terms.

How large is the HS 870422 export flow from China

Estimated exports under the line reached about 268,000 units in 2025, against roughly 241,000 units in 2024, a unit growth rate of about 11.2 percent. Value rose from about USD 5.15 billion to about USD 5.9 billion, an increase of roughly 14.6 percent, which implies a small lift in average unit value. That pattern is consistent with a shift in mix toward better equipped vehicles rather than a simple volume expansion.

Which destinations take the largest share

Destination concentration in this line is moderate rather than extreme. The top ten markets absorbed an estimated 68 percent of units in 2025, and no single market exceeded 9 percent of the total. That distribution is healthier than in the heavy truck segment, where two or three markets often take half the volume.

Average unit value varies from about USD 19,500 for Nigeria to USD 23,500 for Mexico, a spread of roughly 20 percent. The gap tracks body complexity, emission standard and whether the vehicle leaves China as a chassis cab or as a completed unit, not brand positioning.

What the destination pattern says about the market

Mexico's position at the top of the table reflects geography and trade structure. It is a large domestic market with a well developed used vehicle channel, and Chinese medium trucks fill a price band between older American equipment and newer European imports. The 18.4 percent growth in 2025 is notable given the general slowdown in Latin American commercial vehicle demand, and it suggests share gain rather than market expansion.

Russia's flat growth of 4.2 percent contrasts sharply with the 2022 to 2024 period, when volumes rose steeply. Average unit value at USD 22,330 is mid-range, which fits a mix of municipal, utility and construction chassis rather than premium specification. The deceleration is a useful signal that this channel has largely absorbed the initial replacement demand and is now running at a steady state.

Saudi Arabia at 24.1 percent growth is the fastest growing top five market, and the UAE at 9.6 percent continues to function as a re-export hub for East Africa and South Asia. Together the two Gulf markets take 13.9 percent of units, which makes the region the largest bloc by geography even though no single country leads the table.

Why some markets grow while others contract

Three variables explain most of it. The first is import regulation. Markets that tightened emission requirements, such as the shift to Euro V or Euro VI in parts of the Gulf and Latin America, saw a temporary dip followed by a recovery as Chinese suppliers homologated compliant models. Markets with stable, simple requirements grew steadily.

The second is currency and finance. Where local currency depreciated sharply or where import financing became scarce, unit volumes fell even where demand existed. Nigeria at 6.1 percent growth against a much larger replacement need is a clear example, and its average unit value of USD 19,530, the lowest in the top ten, points to a price-sensitive market.

The third is local assembly policy. Where the importing country offers duty advantages for locally assembled kits, the flow shifts from complete vehicles to CKD kits, which do not appear in HS 870422 at all. Kazakhstan at minus 3.4 percent is consistent with that shift rather than with falling demand.

How to read these numbers in practice

Buyers and distributors use this data for three purposes. The first is market sizing, where the unit count sets the ceiling for how many vehicles a competitor network might be placing. The second is price benchmarking, where average unit value gives a rough reference for landed cost, though it must be adjusted for freight, insurance and the mix of chassis cab against completed body.

The third is trend validation. A market growing at 20 percent in one line and flat in an adjacent line usually signals a regulatory or product shift rather than a genuine demand change. Importers checking the credentials of a supply partner can cross-check the volume a supplier claims against the customs record for their destination through an authorized SAGMOTO distributor verification process, and can compare the body configurations that dominate the flow against the full SAGMOTO truck range to see where the line-up matches the customs mix.

One caution applies to all of this. Customs valuation is on a CIF basis in most reporting systems, so published value per unit includes freight and insurance. For land border trade, such as to Kazakhstan or Russia, the freight component is small; for ocean trade to Latin America or Africa it can add 8 to 15 percent. Comparing average unit values across destinations without adjusting for that will produce misleading conclusions about specification levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many goods vehicles does China export under HS 870422 each year

Estimated exports were about 268,000 units in 2025, up 11.2 percent from roughly 241,000 units in 2024, worth around USD 5.9 billion. The series has grown in double digits every year since 2021, when the total was about 163,000 units. These are illustrative estimates compiled from customs statistics, rounded for readability.

Which country is the largest destination for HS 870422 China exports

Mexico is the largest single destination at about 23,900 units, or 8.9 percent of the total, followed by Russia at 8.3 percent and Saudi Arabia at 7.3 percent. No single market exceeds 9 percent, and the top ten destinations together absorb roughly 68 percent of annual unit volume.

What is the average export value of a 5 to 20 tonne truck from China

Average unit value was about USD 22,000 in 2025, up from roughly USD 18,700 in 2021. The figure is on a CIF basis, so it includes freight and insurance. Destination averages range from about USD 19,500 for Nigeria to USD 23,500 for Mexico, reflecting body complexity and emission specification.

Does HS 870422 include tipper trucks and tractor units

HS 870422 covers goods vehicles of 5 to 20 tonnes gross vehicle weight, so it includes small and medium tippers such as 6x4 units at 15 to 20 tonnes, and rigids with box, flatbed or tanker bodies. It excludes road tractors for semi-trailers, classified in 870120, and off-highway dumpers, classified in 870410.

Why did exports to Russia and Kazakhstan grow slower than to Mexico and Saudi Arabia

Russia grew 4.2 percent and Kazakhstan fell 3.4 percent, against 18.4 percent for Mexico and 24.1 percent for Saudi Arabia. The divergence reflects market saturation after steep earlier growth, plus a shift toward locally assembled kits in Kazakhstan, which moves volume out of the complete vehicle tariff line entirely.
