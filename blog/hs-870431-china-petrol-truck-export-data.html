China HS 870431 Petrol Truck Exports: Volume, Value and Unit Price

2026-11-17Export Data9 min read

Quick answer: China exported roughly 53,000 HS 870431 petrol light goods vehicles in 2025 worth about 450 million US dollars, averaging near 8,500 dollars per unit, up from about 44,000 units in 2023. The class stays far smaller than diesel HS 870421 but remains relevant to last-mile fleets in markets where petrol is cheap.

What does HS 870431 cover

HS 870431 is the customs subheading for motor vehicles used to transport goods that have a spark-ignition internal combustion engine and a gross vehicle weight not exceeding five tonnes. In plain terms it is the small petrol-powered truck and light commercial vehicle category, including pickups, panel vans and compact cab-and-chassis units sold into trade and last-mile work. It sits beside HS 870421, which covers the equivalent diesel vehicles, and apart from HS 870490, the residual class for other goods vehicles that do not fit either powertrain definition.

The distinction matters because the three codes behave like separate markets. A shift toward petrol in a destination can raise 870431 volume while leaving the diesel line flat, and a reader who only watches total truck exports will miss that change. Customs authorities record the code declared on each shipment, so the series is a measure of what crossed the border under that classification rather than an audited count of every vehicle type.

How did volume and value move from 2023 to 2025

The petrol light-vehicle class expanded steadily across the three years. Unit shipments rose from approximately 44,000 in 2023 to about 49,000 in 2024 and an estimated 53,000 in 2025, a cumulative gain near 20 percent. Export value moved from roughly 390 million US dollars to 425 million and then about 450 million, a slower climb than volume because the average unit price softened over the period.

The blended average price fell from around 8,860 dollars per unit in 2023 to approximately 8,500 dollars in 2025, a drop of about 4 percent. That decline points to a mix shift toward smaller and more basic vehicles rather than a single price cut across all models, and it is consistent with growing demand from price-sensitive last-mile operators. How does the petrol class compare with diesel and other goods vehicles

Petrol light trucks remain a minority of the under-five-tonne market. HS 870421, the diesel equivalent, shipped an estimated 180,000 units in 2025 at an average near 11,500 dollars, roughly three and a half times the petrol volume and at a materially higher unit price. The diesel class serves heavier and more commercial duty cycles, and its higher price reflects larger engines, stronger chassis and a broader specification range.

HS 870490, the residual goods-vehicle code, is smaller again at approximately 85,000 units but carries a far higher average unit price near 49,500 dollars because it captures specialised and non-standard vehicles. Read together, the three codes show a clear hierarchy: petrol units are the cheapest and most numerous in last-mile niche markets, diesel units dominate commercial volume, and the residual class carries high-value specialised equipment. A buyer benchmarking a quote should first identify which code the vehicle actually falls under.

Which destinations took the largest share

The petrol light-vehicle export mix is concentrated in markets with large informal retail fleets and comparatively low petrol prices. Mexico was the single largest destination at an estimated 9,500 units, close to 18 percent of the total, followed by Vietnam near 7,000 units and the Philippines around 6,000. Indonesia and Nigeria each took roughly 5,000 to 5,500 units, while Egypt, Pakistan and several Central American markets made up much of the remainder.

These destinations share a common pattern: short urban routes, modest payloads and a preference for lower purchase price over long-term fuel efficiency. Where diesel is heavily taxed or petrol is subsidised, the spark-ignition truck holds a cost advantage that keeps 870431 demand alive even as heavier segments shift to diesel. Why does the petrol cut matter for last-mile fleets

Last-mile operators in many cities run short loops with frequent stops, low average speed and payloads well below five tonnes. For that duty a small petrol truck is often cheaper to buy and simpler to maintain than a diesel equivalent, and where petrol is subsidised the running-cost gap narrows further. The 870431 series is therefore a useful proxy for where small petrol workhorses are still being bought in volume.

The data also shows the limits of that advantage. The average unit price drifted down across 2023 to 2025, which suggests intense price competition among suppliers and thin margins on basic specification vehicles. A fleet buyer should weigh that low purchase price against expected service life, parts availability and resale value, because a cheap petrol unit that needs major work after three years can cost more per year than a higher-priced diesel alternative.

Price signal: The 870431 average unit price slipped about 4 percent between 2023 and 2025. That points to a competitive, basic-specification market where purchase price is low but long-term durability needs separate verification.

How should a buyer read this customs series

Customs data is most useful as a benchmark rather than as a verdict. A buyer can compare the price of a quoted vehicle against the destination average from this series and ask why an offer sits far above or below that band. A price well under the norm may indicate a stripped specification or a classification chosen to reduce duty, while a price well above may include equipment the buyer does not need.

Volume trends also help. A destination whose 870431 imports are growing quickly is usually one where small petrol fleets are expanding, which can mean deeper supplier competition and better parts availability. A destination with flat or falling volume may have fewer local support channels. Buyers who want to sanity-check supplier credentials can consult the authorized heavy truck distributor China reference and review the product catalogue for specification context before treating a customs figure as a target price.

What are the limits of the data

The figures here are estimates rounded from customs series and industry summaries, and they carry several caveats. Declared value may not equal the invoice price if related parties are involved, transhipment through a third country can distort destination figures, and re-exports can make a market appear larger than its final consumption. The code also groups very different vehicles under one heading, so a single average price can hide a wide spread between a basic pickup and a better-equipped panel van.

Revision timing is another practical limit. Customs returns for the most recent year are often provisional and can be adjusted months later when corrected declarations are processed, so a 2025 figure quoted in early 2026 may move by a few thousand units by the time the annual settlement is complete. Analysts therefore label the latest year as an estimate and treat the two prior years as the more stable baseline. A buyer who needs a firm number for a tender should request a dated extract from the customs provider and note the extraction date alongside the figure. For those reasons the series should be combined with other evidence before a purchase decision. Buyers should confirm the exact vehicle classification, request a detailed specification, and check that the destination's import duty and emissions rules match the unit being offered. Used as a directional map of supply and price, the 870431 data is informative; used as a precise price list, it is not.
