How to Import SAGMOTO Z3 Tractor Trucks to Jordan: Complete Buyer's Guide 2026

2026-10-20SAGMOTO Buying Guide14 min read

Jordan is a natural market for the SAGMOTO Z3

Jordan sits at the crossroads of the Levant, with a compact but strategically important logistics network built around the Port of Aqaba on the Red Sea, the Aqaba Special Economic Zone (ASEZA), and an inland highway spine running north through Ma'an, the capital Amman, Zarqa and Irbid to the Syrian and Israeli borders. For a fleet operator, that geography makes a single, reliable, comfortable long-haul prime mover enormously valuable: the truck works the Aqaba-to-Amman corridor, feeds the free zones, and backs up cross-border general freight. The SAGMOTO Z3 tractor truck 520HP Cummins M13 is positioned exactly for this role, and this guide explains how a Jordanian buyer imports it from China, what it costs landed, and how to specify the right configuration for local duty.

The Z3 is SAGMOTO's premium express-logistics tractor, built on the same Shaanxi Commercial Vehicle platform that underpins the SHACMAN range. It pairs a Cummins M13 engine producing up to 520 horsepower with a flat-floor sleeper cab engineered to the European ECE R29-03 cab safety standard. For Jordan — a left-hand-drive, European-standard market — that combination of LHD layout, European safety certification and Cummins global-serviceability makes the Z3 a low-risk import compared with lesser-known brands.

The import route: Aqaba and the ASEZA free zone

Almost every SAGMOTO imported to Jordan arrives through the Port of Aqaba. The typical flow is: factory in Xi'an / Shaanxi → container or roll-on/roll-off (RoRo) vessel from Shanghai, Tianjin or Lianyungang → transit through the Red Sea → discharge at Aqaba Container Terminal or the Aqaba RoRo berth. From Aqaba, the tractor is either cleared into the Aqaba Special Economic Zone (where many transport and re-export companies base their fleets) or driven/transported north to Amman and Zarqa for registration.

RoRo is usually the most cost-effective method for a complete tractor unit, because the truck is driven on and off the ship under its own power and avoids container stuffing costs. For a single prototype or a small fleet of under ten units, sharing a consolidated RoRo booking with a freight forwarder is the cheapest route. For 20+ units, a dedicated shipment or a mix of flat-rack and RoRo becomes competitive, and SAGMOTO's export desk can coordinate the booking directly.

Duties, VAT and landed cost in Jordan

Jordan's tariff treatment of commercial goods vehicles is comparatively favourable. Completely built-up (CBU) trucks and tractors for commercial use are generally admitted at a low or zero most-favoured-nation customs duty, because the Kingdom does not have a domestic heavy-truck assembly industry to protect. The charges that actually move the landed price are the General Sales Tax (GST, currently 16%) and the various port and clearance fees. Buyers should always confirm the live figures with Jordan Customs and ASEZA before committing, but the structure below is the working model we use for Jordan quotations.

The single largest variable is the GST, which is assessed on the CIF (cost, insurance, freight) value plus any duty. A buyer planning fleet procurement should budget the GST as working capital, because it is payable at clearance and is the difference between a "cheap FOB quote" and the real cash outlay. ASEZA-based operators may qualify for duty and tax deferment or exemption where the vehicle is used within the zone for re-export, so fleet operators with a free-zone footprint should ask their zone authority about the specific relief available.

Homologation and standards compliance

Jordan does not require a full Gulf-standard (GCC) conformity certificate, but it does expect imported vehicles to meet recognised international standards. For the Z3, the relevant evidence pack is: the ECE R29-03 cab strength certificate, an EURO V emissions declaration, a lighting/electrical compliance statement (LHD with E-mark lamps), and a manufacturer Certificate of Conformity (CoC). Because the Z3 is built on the SHACMAN platform, these documents are standard SAGMOTO export documentation and are issued with every shipment.

Two practical points. First, Jordan is left-hand drive, and the Z3 is delivered LHD as standard for Levant and African markets — no conversion is required. Second, the odometer, documentation and VIN plate should be in a form Jordanian inspectors accept; SAGMOTO exports with English/Chinese bilingual plates and can add Arabic labelling on request for a small lead-time adjustment. Confirm tyre load ratings and the fifth-wheel coupling standard (Jordan uses the standard 2-inch kingpin, which the Z3 carries) before shipment.

Specifying the right Z3 for Jordanian duty

Most Jordanian operators should order the Z3 in the 500–520 HP band with the 12-speed Fast Gear manual, EURO V, and the long-wheelbase 6×4 tractor chassis suited to a 3-axle curtainside or container semitrailer. A wheelbase around 3,300 + 1,350 mm places the rear axle and the fifth wheel correctly for a 13.6 m trailer without exceeding Jordan's combination length limits. For operators running heavier project or bulk freight, the 100-tonne GCW ceiling allows a higher-rated suspension and a reinforced frame at the order stage.

Cooling is not the limiting factor it is in the Gulf, but Jordanian summers in the Rift Valley and Aqaba can exceed 42°C, so the standard cooling package is adequate while the uprated desert option is optional rather than mandatory. We recommend the uprated cabin air-conditioning compressor as a comfort and retention measure for two-driver regional rotation.

Buyer checklist for a Jordan Z3 import

  1. Confirm the duty cycle: Aqaba–Amman corridor, free-zone shuttle, or cross-border freight — this sets GCW and cab spec.
  2. Fix the engine band (500–520 HP recommended) and EURO V emission.
  3. Request the ECE R29-03 cab certificate and CoC in the export document pack.
  4. Obtain a written landed-cost estimate including 16% GST from your forwarder.
  5. Confirm LHD, E-mark lighting, 2-inch kingpin fifth wheel and bilingual VIN plate.
  6. Agree Cummins service and spare-parts provisioning with a Jordan-based Cummins outlet before arrival.
  7. Plan payment on a 30% deposit / 70% against bill of lading or irrevocable LC.
Key point: The cheapest Z3 quotation is the FOB number; the real cost is CIF plus 16% GST plus clearance. Always compare landed cost, and never release the final 70% payment without a clean bill of lading and the CoC in hand.

Payment, delivery and warranty terms

SAGMOTO's standard export terms for Jordan are a 30 percent deposit to schedule production and the balance against a clean bill of lading, or against an irrevocable letter of credit confirmed by a first-class bank. For established Jordanian fleet operators and free-zone companies, staged terms and consignment-style demonstration units can be negotiated on a case-by-case basis. Production for a stock Z3 is typically 15 to 20 days; a custom colour or cab configuration extends this to 30 to 45 days. Sea transit from a Chinese port to Aqaba runs 18 to 22 days, so a realistic door-to-door horizon for a first order is roughly six to nine weeks from deposit to clearance.

The Z3 carries SAGMOTO's standard 24-month / 150,000 km vehicle warranty, with the Cummins M13 engine covered under Cummins' own international warranty framework, which is a meaningful advantage in Jordan given the local Cummins service infrastructure. Fenghan Trading provides the export certificate pack, the CoC and remote diagnostic support, and can arrange a factory technician visit for fleet commissioning and driver training where order volume justifies it. Buyers should treat the warranty and the local Cummins service relationship as two separate but complementary safety nets: the manufacturer warranty covers defects, while the Cummins network covers the day-to-day serviceability that keeps the truck earning.

After-sales and parts strategy

The Z3's Cummins M13 engine is the strongest argument for Jordanian fleet reliability. Cummins has a long-established service presence across the Levant, so filters, turbochargers, injectors and the aftertreatment module are obtainable without waiting on a container from China. SAGMOTO ships a recommended critical-spare kit (filters, belts, brake kits, a clutch kit) with fleet orders, and Fenghan Trading provides remote technical advisory and wiring/diagnostic support. For a 10-unit fleet, pre-positioning one shared kit in Amman is the most cost-effective insurance against a stranded tractor. Beyond the kit, Jordanian operators should build a relationship with a Cummins-authorized outlet before the first truck arrives, because the fastest way to lose a profitable contract is a three-week wait for a single imported seal.

Conclusion

Importing the SAGMOTO Z3 to Jordan is straightforward because the product already matches the market: LHD, European-standard cab, Cummins global serviceability, and a power band that comfortably covers the Aqaba–Amman corridor. The work for a buyer is less about the truck and more about the paperwork — confirm the EURO V and ECE R29-03 documents, budget the 16% GST as real cash, and use Aqaba and ASEZA efficiently. Done properly, a Z3 lands in the USD 56,000–82,000 range, which positions it as a strong-value premium prime mover for Jordanian logistics and free-zone fleets.
