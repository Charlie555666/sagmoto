Buying SAGMOTO E6 Dump Trucks for Togo: Pricing, Shipping and Customs

2026-10-21Buying & Import Guide12 min read

Lomé as a Regional Gateway

The Port of Lomé is the most important deep-water hub on the Gulf of Guinea's western flank, and it is the natural entry point for any truck fleet serving Togo plus the landlocked hinterland of Burkina Faso, Niger, and Mali. Importers choose Lomé because its draft handles geared vessels without lightering, and because the transit corridors north are paved and relatively well policed compared with alternatives. A SAGMOTO E6 tipper bought for Togo is therefore frequently re-routed to Ouagadougou, Niamey, or Bamako under bilateral transit arrangements, which makes the E6's modular engine and body choices directly relevant to resale value.

For aggregate and laterite haulage — the dominant work in Togo's road-building and urban expansion — the E6 4x2 and 6x4 tipper sits in the sweet spot: enough payload for a profitable load, small enough to plate and insure cheaply, and light enough for the weaker secondary roads that feed the quarries around Atakpamé and Kara.

The Cummins option is preferred by fleets that already run Cummins in the region and want shared filters and injector stock; the Yuchai option saves acquisition cost for owner-operators who prioritize price. Body and Payload

Standard E6 tipper bodies run 5-8 m³. The 4x2 with ISD180 carries roughly 8-9 t of aggregate in a 5 m³ box, while the 6x4 with ISD260 carries 12-14 t in an 8 m³ reinforced steel body. Laterite is denser than pit sand, so volume and weight must be balanced against the GVW plate — overloading a 5 m³ body with wet laterite is the fastest way to twist a medium-duty frame.

Used-Truck Age Limits in the Hinterland

If you intend to register an E6 in Burkina Faso, Niger, or Mali rather than Togo, check the age limit before you buy. Several Sahel states cap imported used commercial vehicles at 5 to 10 years from first registration, and a truck that clears Lomé easily can be refused plates 800 km inland. Buy new or near-new for transit-corridor resale, and keep the build-date plate and original invoice unambiguous.

Body steel choice: Specify Hardox 450 wear plate on the floor and lower side walls of the tipper. The small premium over mild steel pays back within two wet seasons of laterite and granite-chip loading, and it keeps the empty tare weight stable so your payload maths stay legal.

Togolese Duty and TVA

Togo applies customs duty on the CIF value, plus TVA and a community levy tied to the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) schedule. Indicative bands for a complete dump truck:

  • Customs duty: 5-10% of CIF (vehicles often sit at the lower band)
  • UEMOA community levy: roughly 1% of CIF
  • TVA: 18% applied on CIF plus duty plus levies
  • Statistical and excise fees: 1-3% combined
  • Local clearance and transit bond: EUR 500-1,000

Because TVA is assessed on the post-duty base, the effective landed multiplier on a CIF of USD 34,000 is typically 30-40%, putting delivered yard cost near USD 45,000-48,000 before plates and insurance. If the truck continues to a hinterland country, a transit bond covers the Togolese leg until the destination customs clears it.

Pre-Shipment Inspection

Some destination regimes require a pre-shipment inspection (PSI) certificate issued at the load port. Even when not mandatory for Togo itself, a PSI report smooths clearance and supports your TVA calculation. Ask Shaanxi Fenghan Trading to schedule a third-party PSI at Tianjin or Shanghai before sealing the container; the fee is typically USD 250-450 and saves days at Lomé.

Parts kit sizing: For a 10-truck E6 batch, hold one shared kit per five units: 10 oil-filter sets, 10 fuel-filter sets, two clutch kits, two sets of brake shoes, one turbo, and one hydraulic tipper cylinder seal kit. The Fast Gear 9-speed and Cummins ISD share parts with many regional trucks, so Lomé and Cotonou stockists can back you up between shipments.

CFA Franc Payment Mechanics

Togo uses the West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged to the euro, so your supplier still quotes and invoices in USD but you settle through a Togolese bank that converts at the official rate. For a first order use a confirmed LC to protect funds; for repeats, 30% TT deposit and 70% against bill of lading is standard. Note that CFA convertibility makes repatriation and supplier payment straightforward, but build in 2-4 banking days for the USD-to-XOF leg and LC confirmation charges of about 1% per six months.

Operator Training and Tyre Strategy

A tipper is only as reliable as the operator who loads and tips it. Specify a half-day loading discipline for new drivers: never load beyond the GVW plate, tip only on level ground with the handbrake set, and grease the tipper hinge and cylinder pins weekly. Tyres are the second-largest running cost after fuel — fit 11R22.5 or 12R22.5 depending on GVW, carry two spares per unit, and rotate by position every 10,000 km. On laterite and granite-chip haul roads, under-inflation causes sidewall splits that a correct pressure check would prevent, so fit a governed tyre-pressure monitoring routine at the yard gate.

Insurance and Resale in the Hinterland

Togolese commercial vehicle insurance is readily available through Lomé brokers, but if the truck will operate in Burkina Faso, Niger, or Mali, extend the policy to name each transit state and confirm cross-border validity before the first trip. Resale value is strongest when the build-date plate, original invoice, and French or Arabic manuals are complete, because buyers in the Sahel check age limits strictly. A clean, low-mileage E6 with full documentation typically retains 55-65% of landed value at three years, versus 40-50% for a truck with gaps in its papers. Keep a digital folder per unit from day one.

Customs Clearance Step by Step

The Lomé clearance sequence runs: submit the import declaration with commercial invoice and bill of lading, pay the computed duty and TVA through your broker, present the PSI certificate if required, pass the physical inspection at the port terminal, and collect the release order. Build in three to seven working days from vessel discharge to yard exit, longer if the VAT assessment is queried. A transit bond is mandatory if the truck leaves Togo for a hinterland country before local duty is paid, and that bond must be discharged by the destination customs within the stated window or you forfeit it. Engage a Lomé licensed clearance agent before the vessel sails so the paperwork packet is pre-built and the only variable is the physical exam.

New E6 vs a Used Japanese Tipper

Many Togolese buyers compare a new E6 against a three-to-five-year-old Japanese tipper at similar price. The used unit carries brand familiarity and an established local workshop network, but it also brings unknown service history, likely worn bushings and brakes, and a clock that is already against the Sahel age limits for onward registration. The new E6 brings a full warranty, current emission compliance, and a known maintenance start point. For a first fleet where uptime and financeability matter, the new E6's predictable cost usually beats the false economy of a cheap used import, while a second-hand Japanese unit can still make sense as a low-cost standby for a mature operator.
