Reading a Chinese Truck Proforma Invoice With Confidence

A proforma invoice is not a demand for final payment but a formal quotation that locks the commercial terms before production begins. For overseas fleet owners and import distributors buying SAGMOTO or SHACMAN trucks, this single document decides whether the order is safe, customs-clearable and priced as agreed. The guidance below uses the export practice of Shaanxi Fenghan Trading, an authorized distributor, so you can benchmark any supplier's paperwork against a known-good standard. You can compare the underlying SAGMOTO truck models against the lines listed on your invoice to confirm the specification matches the price.

The most common buyer mistake is treating the proforma as a formality. In reality it is the contract of intent that customs, your bank and the factory all rely on. A missing HS code, a vague Incoterm or an unverified authorization certificate can stall a shipment for weeks and cost far more than the truck itself. Reading the document line by line before signing is the cheapest risk control in the entire import process.

What Details Does a Valid Proforma Invoice Have to Contain?

A complete proforma names the exact model, such as the X3s 8x4, the E9 light truck or the E3 MAX, together with the engine model, the emission standard and the gross vehicle weight. It shows the unit price, the total amount, the currency, the Incoterm (FOB Qingdao or CIF destination port), the HS code, the VIN or chassis number series, the production lead time and the validity period, normally 30 days from the issue date. Without these fields the document cannot support a letter of credit or a customs valuation.

Buyers should also expect the supplier's full legal name, registered address and bank details, including the SWIFT code and account number, plus the consignee information and the packing summary. The packing details, such as chassis count and dimensions, let your forwarder book the right vessel and declare the cargo correctly. Treat any proforma that omits the engine model or the HS code as incomplete and refuse to pay against it until the gaps are closed.

How Can You Verify the Supplier Is Authorized?

Authorization is the cheapest insurance against a fake listing. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading holds SAGMOTO and SHACMAN distributor certificates numbered SQIDACN 28 and SAG-2025-201, and a genuine supplier will show these without hesitation. Ask for the certificate scan, then cross-check the company name on it against the bank account and the proforma header, because a mismatch between those three is the classic scam pattern that traps first-time importers.

Beyond the certificate, verify the business through the official export records, the factory's published distributor list and a live video call at the warehouse showing the trucks. A supplier who refuses a warehouse video or who cannot name the authorization number should not receive a deposit. The few minutes of checking protect a deposit that often runs into tens of thousands of dollars across a multi-unit order.

Always match the authorization certificate name, the proforma header company and the bank account to one legal entity. If SQIDACN 28 or SAG-2025-201 is absent or the names differ, stop and re-verify before any payment leaves your account.

Which Incoterm and Payment Terms Protect the Buyer?

For a first order, FOB keeps freight transparent because you book and control the ocean leg, while CIF suits buyers who want the supplier to handle insurance to the destination. Payment is normally 30 percent deposit by telegraphic transfer to secure production, with the remaining 70 percent paid before shipment against a clean inspection report, or an LC at sight that releases funds only on compliant documents presented to your bank.

Avoid any request for the full amount up front, especially to a personal account or a third-party name. The balanced 30/70 structure protects you if the truck fails pre-shipment inspection, because the final 70 percent is still held. An LC at sight adds bank oversight but costs a fee, so weigh it against order value; for a single E9 light truck the fee may outweigh the benefit, while for ten X3s units it is worthwhile.

When Should You Treat a Proforma Invoice as a Red Flag?

Walk away the moment the proforma demands full payment to a personal account, omits the Incoterm, lists no HS code, or carries an authorization number you cannot verify. Prices far below market for an X3s or E3 MAX, pressure to pay within hours, and a refusal to provide a factory video are all classic warning signs that the document is not backed by a real truck on a real line.

Also beware vague model descriptions such as "heavy truck" without the exact code, and bank details in a country different from where the supplier claims to be based. Legitimate export proformas are detailed and patient; scam documents are rushed and thin. Trust the completeness of the paperwork as much as the price, because a too-good price with no verifiable detail is the most expensive mistake in truck importing.

If the proforma shows no Incoterm, no HS code and no verifiable certificate such as SAG-2025-201, treat it as a scam and do not transfer funds. Genuine SAGMOTO export orders always carry all three on the face of the document.

What Happens After You Sign the Proforma

Once the proforma is countersigned, production is scheduled against the stated lead time, typically 30 to 60 days for standard SAGMOTO models and longer for custom spec. The supplier issues a commercial invoice and packing list at completion, followed by the bill of lading, certificate of origin and any conformity documents your market requires for the E9, X3s or E3 MAX. Keep the proforma with the shipment file because customs may compare the declared value against it during clearance.

Arrange pre-shipment inspection at around 80 percent completion so you can withhold the 70 percent balance if the truck fails specification. This step is where the disciplined buyer protects the deposit and the final payment alike, and it is standard practice for authorized distributors exporting through established channels rather than informal traders.

Frequently Asked Questions

What payment terms appear on a legitimate SAGMOTO proforma invoice?

A legitimate SAGMOTO proforma invoice states payment as a 30 percent TT deposit with the remaining 70 percent paid before shipment, or an LC at sight, and never requests a full advance to a personal account. This balanced structure lets you withhold the final 70 percent if pre-shipment inspection fails. Authorized distributors such as Shaanxi Fenghan Trading print both the deposit ratio and the bank SWIFT details on the document face for transparency.

How do you confirm the supplier holds SAGMOTO authorization?

Confirm authorization by requesting certificate numbers SQIDACN 28 and SAG-2025-201, which Shaanxi Fenghan Trading holds as an authorized SAGMOTO and SHACMAN distributor. Cross-check that the certificate name matches the proforma header and the bank account exactly. A supplier who cannot show a valid certificate or who uses a different legal name for the account should not receive any deposit under any circumstance.

Which Incoterm should a first-time buyer choose?

A first-time buyer should choose FOB for freight control or CIF when the supplier arranges cover, with the term printed beside the 30 percent deposit line on the proforma. FOB suits importers with their own forwarder, while CIF reduces first-order complexity. Either term must appear in the Incoterm field alongside the named port such as Qingdao to be valid.

What HS code and VIN details must the invoice show?

The proforma must list the truck HS code from 8704.21 to 8704.90 by gross weight and the VIN or chassis series for each unit, because customs rejects entries missing these fields. For an X3s 8x4 the code differs from a light E9, so the correct figure protects you from duty reclassification. The VIN series ties the physical truck to the document at the loading port.

When is a proforma invoice a scam warning?

Treat the proforma as a scam warning the moment it demands 100 percent upfront to a personal account, omits an Incoterm, or shows no authorization certificate such as SQIDACN 28. Other signs include prices far below market for an E3 MAX and refusal to provide a live warehouse video. Genuine export invoices are detailed and patient, never rushed or thin on critical fields.
