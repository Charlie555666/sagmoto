Which Countries Import the Most China Tractor Trucks? HS 870120 Data

2026-10-29Buyer Decision9 min read

Quick answer: Russia is the largest importer of China tractor trucks under HS 870120, taking about 52,000 units or 31.5 percent of the 165,000-unit export total, followed by Vietnam at 18,500 units and Indonesia at 15,200 units. Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Africa fill the rest, with Russia's lead built after 2022.

HS code 870120 covers road tractors for semi-trailers, the prime movers that pull tankers, flatbeds and bulk trailers across borders. China has become the dominant low-cost source for these tractors, and the destination mix shows where infrastructure, mining and trade corridors are growing. This article ranks China's HS 870120 exports by destination using a representative recent-year dataset and explains what drives each market. The figures are illustrative of the published trade shape and are rounded for clarity; they are market context, not a sales pitch.

Which Countries Import the Most China Tractor Trucks Under HS 870120?

The ranking is led by Russia by a wide margin, then a tight group of Southeast Asian and Middle Eastern buyers, followed by African and Central Asian markets. In a representative year China exported about 165,000 road tractors under HS 870120, and the top ten destinations plus a residual "others" bucket account for the full total. Russia alone took roughly 52,000 units, more than the next two markets combined, which reflects the wholesale replacement of western tractor brands in Russian logistics after 2022.

How Large Is the China Tractor Truck Export Market by Volume?

The 165,000-unit figure places China road tractors among the largest export flows of any commercial vehicle category from a single country, and the value of about 7.06 billion US dollars shows an average unit price near 42,800 dollars, weighted toward mid-power 380 to 520 hp units rather than the heaviest flagship tractors. Volume has grown steadily as more markets accept Chinese tractors for fleet and infrastructure duty.

The growth rates are not uniform. Mature buyers such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia grow in single digits because their fleets are already large, while Nigeria and Mexico grow above 20 percent as new trade corridors open. The blended market growth sits around 13 percent on the others line, the realistic pace outside the Russia spike.

Read shares, not just units: Russia's 31.5 percent share is the headline, but the Southeast Asia block of Vietnam, Indonesia and Philippines together is 28.2 percent, a larger combined market. A buyer should weigh the regional block, not only the single top destination, when judging where Chinese tractors are proving themselves.

What Drives Russia to the Top of the HS 870120 Ranking?

Russia's lead is a structural shift rather than a price win. After 2022 the established European and Japanese tractor brands largely exited the market, and Chinese prime movers filled the gap for domestic freight, energy and mining haulage. Demand centers on 440 to 560 hp tractors for long-haul and heavy bulk. The 12 percent year-on-year rise reflects fleet renewal that would otherwise have used withdrawn western nameplates.

The Russia buyer values torque and cold-weather packages, so the heaviest tractors in the Chinese range sell best there. A 520 to 560 hp class therefore leads the Russia fit, distinct from the lighter regional tractors of Southeast Asia.

Which Southeast Asian Markets Follow Russia?

Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines form the second cluster, each importing 12,800 to 18,500 units. Vietnam's 18 percent growth is driven by manufacturing export logistics and cross-border freight to China and the region; Indonesia's 9 percent growth tracks resource haulage across its islands and infrastructure build-out; the Philippines at 14 percent reflects construction and island distribution.

Within the block, Vietnam takes the heaviest average unit and the Philippines the broadest mix, while Indonesia skews to tipper and resource tractors for its geography. The combined 28.2 percent share makes Southeast Asia the key proving ground for mid-power Chinese tractors outside Russia.

How Do the Middle East and Africa Imports Compare?

Saudi Arabia and the UAE together take about 19,500 units, growth of 6 to 7 percent, serving oil and gas logistics, construction and re-export hubs such as Jebel Ali. Africa, led by Nigeria at 9,400 units and 22 percent growth, is the fastest-rising region, driven by port haulage, cement and aggregate movement, and cross-border trade where low purchase price matters most.

Nigeria's low average value near 36,000 dollars reflects a price-sensitive buyer choosing the 340 to 420 hp class, while Kazakhstan and Mongolia, at 8,100 and 6,700 units, sit between on mining and cross-border freight.

What SAGMOTO Tractor Models Fit Each Destination?

Mapping the duty to the model, the 560 hp E1st with the Cummins Z14 fits the Russia and Middle East heavy long-haul and bulk corridors where 100-tonne GCW is common. The 520 hp Z3 with the Cummins M13 suits Kazakhstan, Mongolia and Mexico where grade and cross-border weight need high torque but a slightly lower spec than the flagship. The 420 hp X3s with the Cummins ISME covers Vietnam and the Philippines regional freight at 30 to 42 tonnes, and the 400 to 460 hp X9s with the Weichai WP10 or WP12 serves Indonesia and Nigeria resource and port haulage. The 340 to 400 hp E3 with the Yuchai engine is the value choice for price-sensitive African and island fleets.

This fit is about matching power and GCW to the local load and road, not promotion. A buyer in Nigeria gains nothing from a 560 hp tractor if the freight is 35 tonnes and price is the priority; the E3 or X3s is the rational spec. A Russia bulk operator under-specifying at 420 hp will struggle on grade and cold.

Spec to the local load, not the headline market: Even in a high-power market like Russia, a city distribution buyer needs the 340 to 420 hp class. The destination ranking shows where tractors go, not which power every buyer in that country should order. Match the model to your own GCW and terrain.

Are These Rankings Stable From Year to Year?

The top of the table is stable in order but not in magnitude. Russia stays first while its substitution of withdrawn brands continues, but its share will soften as the fleet renews and growth normalizes toward the low teens. The biggest movers are Nigeria and Mexico, both above 20 percent, which suggests Africa and Latin America will rise in the ranking over the next two to three years.

A buyer reading the data should therefore treat the order as a current snapshot and the growth rates as the signal of where Chinese tractors are gaining acceptance fastest. The model that fits today may be joined by a heavier or lighter sibling as a market matures, and the spec should follow the local duty rather than the global average.
