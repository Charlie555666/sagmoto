Can You Buy Right-Hand-Drive SAGMOTO Trucks from China?

2026-09-29Buyer Guide9 min read

Quick answer: Yes. SAGMOTO supplies right-hand-drive trucks to left-hand-traffic markets including Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, South Africa and Indonesia. Right-hand drive is built at the factory, adds roughly 6 to 10 weeks to lead time, and typically adds USD 3,000 to USD 8,000 per truck depending on model and specification.

Why right-hand drive is an engineering decision, not an accessory

Buyers new to importing from China often assume that drive side is a minor configuration item, like a paint colour or a seat trim. It is not. Factory right-hand drive is a distinct vehicle build: the steering column, pedal box, brake and clutch master cylinders, dashboard and instrument cluster, wiper linkage and sweep, headlamp beam pattern, mirror set, and in many cases the exhaust routing and fuel tank position all differ from the left-hand-drive version. On a heavy truck, the steering gear and the cab wiring harness are also side-specific.

That is why the answer to whether a truck can be converted after arrival is almost always no, at least not economically. A local conversion of a heavy truck cab from left to right-hand drive requires new steering gear mounting, relocated pedal assemblies, a different dashboard and wiring loom, re-engineered wiper geometry, and headlamps that meet the local beam pattern. In practice this costs several thousand dollars per vehicle, voids the manufacturer warranty, and produces a truck that no informed buyer will pay full price for at resale. Factory-built right-hand drive avoids all of it.

Key point: Factory right-hand drive costs USD 3,000 to USD 8,000 per truck and preserves the warranty. A post-arrival conversion costs more, voids the warranty, and leaves a truck with a permanently impaired resale value in every left-hand-traffic market.

Which SAGMOTO models are available in right-hand drive?

Right-hand drive availability follows demand, and the demand is concentrated in the vehicle types that dominate left-hand-traffic export markets: long-haul tractor units for corridor freight, heavy dump and tipper chassis for mining and construction, and light and medium trucks for urban distribution. Within the SAGMOTO range, the tractor line is the most frequently requested RHD configuration because cross-border corridor work in East and Southern Africa is almost entirely left-hand traffic. Buyers assessing that line should review the SAGMOTO tractor trucks prime mover range and confirm RHD availability at the point of enquiry, because production slots for right-hand-drive units are scheduled in batches rather than held continuously.

The practical rule for buyers is to declare the drive side at the first enquiry rather than at the quotation stage. Because RHD units require a separate production slot and a distinct parts kit, a change from left to right-hand drive after an order is placed can move the delivery date by weeks and, depending on how far the build has progressed, may not be possible at all.

How long does a right-hand-drive order take, and what does it cost?

Lead time is the single most common source of disappointment in right-hand-drive orders, and it is almost always a planning failure rather than a production failure. A right-hand-drive vehicle needs a place in a production batch that includes the RHD parts kit, and those batches are scheduled rather than continuous. The realistic planning figure is 6 to 10 weeks beyond a standard left-hand-drive slot, which puts total build time at 12 to 18 weeks from deposit.

Freight and clearance then add their own time. Sea freight from a Chinese port to Mombasa or Dar es Salaam typically runs 3 to 6 weeks including transhipment, to Durban or Maputo 4 to 6 weeks, and to Jakarta or Surabaya 2 to 4 weeks. Port clearance, inspection and inland delivery add a further 1 to 3 weeks depending on the market and on how complete the documentation set is. A buyer planning a fleet replacement programme should therefore work on a door-to-door window of 16 to 28 weeks and should place orders accordingly.

What homologation and documentation does a right-hand-drive truck need?

Every market has its own entry requirements, and the drive side interacts with several of them. The items below are the ones that most often delay a right-hand-drive consignment, and all of them are cheaper to prepare before shipment than to resolve afterwards.

  • Certificate of conformity referencing the correct drive side. A document that describes a left-hand-drive vehicle will be rejected for a right-hand-drive import in most regulated markets. The VIN records must match the physical vehicle.
  • Lighting and mirror compliance evidence. Right-hand-traffic headlamp beams dip to the right; left-hand-traffic beams dip to the left. Inspectors check this, and it is the most common single reason for a failed pre-export inspection on an RHD unit.
  • Pre-export inspection. Kenya and Tanzania operate pre-export verification of conformity programmes, Mozambique and South Africa have their own import control steps, and Indonesia applies national standard and type-approval requirements. Each requires documentation prepared in advance and an inspection appointment before loading.
  • Axle load and dimension evidence. Several East and Southern African markets enforce axle load limits strictly, and the declaration has to match the as-built configuration including the body.
  • Import permit and, in some markets, an age limit declaration. Some jurisdictions restrict the age of imported commercial vehicles. A new vehicle usually satisfies this, but the certificate has to state the build date clearly.

The cost of getting this wrong is not the inspection fee. It is the demurrage. A consignment held at Mombasa or Dar es Salaam for three weeks because a conformity certificate described the wrong drive side can accumulate storage charges of USD 1,500 to USD 4,000 for a small fleet, on top of the delay to the fleet programme. The documentation set should be reviewed by the buyer's clearing agent before the vessel sails, not after.

Should I order right-hand drive or convert locally?

Almost always, order right-hand drive. The comparison is not close once the full cost is counted. A local conversion of a heavy truck cab requires steering and pedal relocation, a new dashboard and wiring loom, revised wiper geometry, headlamps with the correct beam pattern and, in many cases, re-routing of exhaust and air intake. Quoted conversion costs in East and Southern Africa commonly run from USD 4,000 to USD 9,000 per vehicle, before the cost of the downtime and before any adjustment for the fact that the converted truck is worth less at resale.

Against that, the factory premium is USD 3,000 to USD 8,000, the vehicle arrives complete and warrantied, and the resale value in a left-hand-traffic market is unimpaired. The only situation in which a conversion makes sense is an emergency replacement of a single vehicle where an RHD slot is unavailable and the operational cost of waiting exceeds the conversion cost and the resale penalty combined. That is a rare and genuinely urgent case, not a procurement strategy.

What this means for fleet planning in left-hand-traffic markets

For a fleet operating in Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, South Africa or Indonesia, the practical conclusion is that right-hand drive should be treated as the default specification and left-hand drive as the exception requiring justification. Buying left-hand-drive trucks into a left-hand-traffic market creates three problems at once: driver visibility and safety on the road, regulatory friction at import and at annual inspection, and a resale market that will discount the vehicle heavily or refuse it. In several of these markets, a left-hand-drive heavy truck has a resale market measured in a small fraction of the right-hand-drive value, which is a real cost that belongs in the acquisition decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SAGMOTO build right-hand-drive trucks at the factory?

Yes. Right-hand drive is built on the SAGMOTO production line rather than converted after delivery, which adds 6 to 10 weeks to lead time and typically USD 3,000 to USD 8,000 to the price of each truck. Factory-built right-hand drive keeps the steering column, pedal box, dashboard, wiper sweep and headlamp beam pattern correct as a complete engineered package, and it preserves the full vehicle warranty.

How much extra does a right-hand-drive SAGMOTO truck cost?

Expect USD 3,000 to USD 8,000 per truck above the equivalent left-hand-drive model, depending on model and specification. The premium covers the right-hand steering assembly, mirror and lighting set, instrument cluster, wiper and washer hardware, and the separate production slot. Ordering 10 or more right-hand-drive units in one batch usually reduces the per-unit premium because the line changeover cost is shared across the order.

What is the lead time for a right-hand-drive SAGMOTO order?

Plan on 6 to 10 weeks beyond the standard left-hand-drive build slot, which puts a typical right-hand-drive order at 12 to 18 weeks from deposit to readiness for shipment. Add 3 to 6 weeks of sea freight to East or Southern Africa and 2 to 4 weeks to Indonesia, plus 1 to 3 weeks for port clearance and inland delivery, so the full door-to-door window is commonly 16 to 28 weeks.

Which markets can receive right-hand-drive SAGMOTO trucks?

Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, South Africa and Indonesia are all regularly served with factory-built right-hand drive, along with other left-hand-traffic markets in Southern and East Africa and in Southeast Asia. Every one of these five markets drives on the left, and in Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique and South Africa a left-hand-drive truck is either restricted or commercially unattractive when the fleet comes to resell it.

Do right-hand-drive trucks need different homologation documents?

Yes. Right-hand-drive units need a right-hand-drive-specific certificate of conformity, mirror and headlamp pattern compliance evidence, and in most of these markets a pre-export inspection, such as PVoC for Kenya, PVoC for Tanzania, or SNI-related documentation steps for Indonesia. Budget 2 to 5 weeks and USD 800 to USD 2,500 per consignment for inspection, testing and documentation handling.

Conclusion

Right-hand-drive SAGMOTO trucks are available from China as factory-built vehicles for left-hand-traffic markets, and buying them that way is the only approach that makes commercial sense. The premium of USD 3,000 to USD 8,000 per truck buys a complete engineered vehicle with the correct steering, pedal, dashboard, wiper and lighting package, a preserved warranty, and an unimpaired resale value in markets where left-hand-drive trucks are heavily discounted.

The two things a buyer must plan for are time and documentation. A right-hand-drive build slot is 12 to 18 weeks, the door-to-door window is 16 to 28 weeks, and the documentation set must describe the vehicle's actual drive side before the vessel sails. Both are manageable with advance planning and both are expensive without it.
