How to Choose a Reliable Chinese Heavy Truck Supplier: 7 Verification Steps (2026 Guide)

2026-09-24Export Guide12 min read

Quick answer: to choose a reliable Chinese heavy truck supplier, verify seven things in order: business license and Unified Social Credit Code on China's national registry (gsxt.gov.cn), brand authorization letter, live video tour of stock and facilities, third-party pre-shipment inspection (SGS/BV), spare parts supply capability, references from buyers in your region, and sane payment terms (30% deposit, balance against B/L copy). A supplier that passes all seven is safe; one that fails any of the first three is not.

Every year overseas buyers lose money to the same handful of scams: re-painted used trucks sold as new, "factories" that are actually one-man middlemen, and deposits that disappear after a too-good price quote. The checks below take two to three days and cost almost nothing — but they filter out 95% of bad actors before any money moves.

Step 1: How Do You Verify a Chinese Supplier's Business License?

Every legitimate Chinese company has an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) registered in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn). Ask for the company's full registered name and USCC, then look it up yourself — registration status, registered capital, legal representative and any penalties are all public.

Worked example: our company, Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd., is registered in Xi'an with USCC 91610104MACWWH682Q (established 2023-08-29, registered capital RMB 2 million). Any supplier unwilling to share this number should be dropped immediately.

Step 2: How Do You Confirm Brand Authorization?

Only two kinds of companies can legitimately sell new SHACMAN/SAGMOTO, Sinotruk or FAW trucks for export: the factory's own export division, and trading companies holding a current brand authorization letter. Ask for the letter, then verify it by emailing or calling the factory's international department directly (their contact details are on the official brand website — not the ones printed on the letter itself). Our authorized exporter verification walkthrough shows a real letter and what to check on it.

Step 3: What Should a Live Video Tour Show?

Schedule a video call and ask the salesperson to walk, live, through: (1) the yard with physical truck stock — ask them to open a cab door and read out a VIN plate; (2) the office with business license on the wall; (3) the parts warehouse. Pre-recorded videos are easy to fake; a live walk with you directing the camera is not. A supplier with nothing to hide will do this happily in one call.

Step 4: Why Pay for Third-Party Inspection?

SGS, Bureau Veritas and TÜV all operate in every Chinese truck manufacturing region. A pre-shipment inspection costs roughly US$300-500 per truck and covers VIN verification, build quality, fluid levels, function tests and loading supervision. Any supplier who refuses third-party inspection is telling you something. We actively encourage it — see our quotation and inspection terms.

Step 5: Can They Actually Supply Spare Parts?

A truck without parts is a 3-year asset, not a 10-year one. Ask three questions: Do you stock parts or only source to order? Can you quote by OE part number? What is your air-freight lead time for urgent parts? A supplier integrated with a parts operation — for example our genuine OE parts store with factory part numbers and prices online — will still be useful to you in year 5, long after the sale commission is forgotten.

Step 6: How Do You Check References?

Ask for two or three buyers in your region (not just your country) who took delivery in the last 12 months, with contactable details. Then actually message them: Did the trucks match the proforma? Any hidden costs? How did the supplier respond to the first warranty claim? Legitimate exporters keep reference lists ready; scammers stall or produce unverifiable screenshots.

Red Flags That Should End the Conversation Immediately

After seven steps, the negative list is just as important. Walk away when you see any of these:

  • Price 10%+ below every other quote — legitimate exporters buy from the same factory price list; deep discounts mean used trucks, stripped specs, or no truck at all.
  • No USCC, or a USCC that shows "deregistered"/"abnormal operation" status on gsxt.gov.cn.
  • An authorization letter the factory won't confirm — always verify through the factory's official channels, not the phone number printed on the letter.
  • Pressure to pay 100% upfront, or to pay a personal account, or a Hong Kong/BVI shell with no Chinese operating entity behind it.
  • No live video call within 48 hours — a supplier with real stock shows it; a broker with a borrowed showroom makes excuses.
  • No physical yard address you can pin on a map and send an inspector to.

Run the seven steps, keep the red-flag list beside you, and the Chinese heavy truck market becomes one of the safest sourcing categories in heavy equipment — the product is standardized, the paperwork is verifiable, and the honest suppliers (us included) welcome every check on this list.

FAQ: Choosing a Chinese Truck Supplier

Q: How do I know if a Chinese truck supplier is legitimate?
A: Verify their Unified Social Credit Code on gsxt.gov.cn, confirm a current brand authorization letter with the factory, and insist on a live video walk through their stock and a third-party pre-shipment inspection. Legitimate suppliers pass all four easily.

Q: What is a Unified Social Credit Code and where do I check it?
A: It is an 18-character code every registered Chinese company has (example: 91610104MACWWH682Q). Enter it on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System at gsxt.gov.cn to see registration status, capital, legal representative and penalty history.

Q: Should I buy from a factory export division or a trading company?
A: Factory divisions suit 20-50+ unit tenders. For smaller orders, a factory-authorized trading company offers flexible MOQ, mixed loading with spare parts, and much faster communication — verify its authorization letter with the factory.

Q: How much deposit is safe when buying trucks from China?
A: 30% T/T deposit with the balance against a bill of lading copy (or by irrevocable L/C at sight) is the industry norm. Never pay the full amount before shipment, and never pay a personal bank account.

Q: What does a pre-shipment truck inspection cost?
A: SGS or Bureau Veritas charge roughly US$300-500 per truck in Chinese manufacturing regions, covering VIN verification, function tests and loading supervision — cheap insurance on a US$40,000+ purchase.
