Buying the SAGMOTO i5 Electric Truck in Uruguay: 2026 Import Guide

2026-10-28Buying Guide9 min read

Uruguay has built one of the most EV-friendly policy environments in Latin America, and fleet operators in Montevideo, Canelones and Maldonado are now asking a practical question: which electric light truck actually fits the daily delivery work? The SAGMOTO i5 answers that question with a roughly 98 kWh lithium iron phosphate pack and a realistic 200 km operating range that lands squarely inside the typical Uruguayan urban and peri-urban duty cycle. This guide walks procurement managers through the incentive backdrop, charging reality on the ground, the diesel-versus-electric cost math, homologation steps and the exact specification we recommend for a first Uruguay shipment.

1. Uruguay's Electric Vehicle Incentive Environment

Uruguay applies reduced or waived import duties on certain electric vehicles under its broader clean-energy strategy, and the country's almost fully renewable electricity grid makes every kilometre driven on battery genuinely low carbon. Importers should treat the exact percentage as a moving target confirmed with a Montevideo customs broker, because the relevant decrees and values change with each budget cycle. What is stable is the direction: battery-electric trucks are treated more favourably than diesel equivalents at the point of entry, and there is no fuel excise disadvantage once the truck is registered and running.

The practical effect is that the landed cost gap between an i5 and a comparable diesel light truck narrows at the border, then disappears during the first two years of operation thanks to energy and maintenance savings. For a fleet doing urban distribution, that arithmetic is the real reason to import now rather than wait.

Broker first: Before you commit a deposit, ask your Uruguayan customs agent for the current EV duty treatment, the VAT base calculation and whether the i5 body configuration qualifies as a goods vehicle or a special vehicle. Those three numbers decide your final landed price, and they are the only figures that should go into your board presentation.

2. Charging Infrastructure in Montevideo, Carrasco and Punta del Este

Montevideo and the metropolitan area already host a usable mix of public AC chargers and an expanding set of DC fast chargers, concentrated along the waterfront, in shopping centres and at logistics yards. Carrasco, home to the international airport and many distribution depots, has depot-charging potential that makes overnight fill trivial. Punta del Este is seasonal: capacity is ample in summer when tourism peaks and quieter in winter, but a depot-installed charger removes any dependence on public infrastructure for a commercial operator.

We recommend that every Uruguay i5 buyer install at least one 60 kW DC charger at their own yard. The i5 accepts DC charging that restores the bulk of the 98 kWh pack in well under two hours, and a single overnight AC session is enough for the next day's urban loop. A depot charger turns the truck into a predictable fixed asset instead of a guest on someone else's network.

3. Urban Duty Cycles That Fit Inside 200 km

Most Uruguayan light-truck work is short-radius. A Montevideo grocery run, a Canelones dairy collection, a Punta del Este beverage delivery in season, or an airport-side parcel loop all fall between 40 and 140 km per day including return legs. Even a double-shift courier pattern rarely exceeds 180 km before the truck is back on charge. The i5 200 km realistic range therefore covers the overwhelming majority of applicants with margin for detours, traffic on Avenida 18 de Julio, and a hot summer cab load.

Where the i5 does not fit is intercity long-haul between Montevideo and Salto or Rivera in a single shift. For those routes a diesel or the larger SAGMOTO i9 is the right tool. The i5 is deliberately a city and peri-urban truck, and matching it to that role is what makes the business case clean.

4. i5 Specification Summary

The i5 is a left-hand-drive light electric truck built on a conventional ladder chassis so that local body builders can fit box, curtainside or refrigerated bodies without re-engineering. The permanent-magnet drive motor delivers smooth low-end torque suited to stop-start city driving, and the 98 kWh LFP pack is chosen for safety and cycle life rather than maximum density. Regenerative braking recovers energy on the constant braking of urban routes, directly extending real range.

5. Five-Year Total Cost Comparison With Diesel

The headline purchase price of a diesel light truck is lower, but the total cost of ownership picture reverses within the first 36 months for high-utilisation urban work. Electricity in Uruguay, drawn from a renewable grid, costs a fraction of diesel per kilometre. The i5 also removes engine oil, diesel filters, exhaust after-treatment and most clutch wear from the maintenance plan. Brake wear drops because regen does much of the slowing.

Using a representative urban duty of 120 km per day and 300 working days per year, the i5 saves enough on energy and service across five years to offset the initial price premium in most configurations. The exact crossover point depends on your negotiated duty rate and your electricity tariff, which is why the broker figures in section 1 matter.

6. Battery Warranty and State of Health

SAGMOTO warrants the i5 LFP pack for a defined period and cycle count that we confirm per order, with a state-of-health threshold below which a module is serviced. LFP chemistry is forgiving: it tolerates daily full charging and shows slow capacity fade, so an operator who keeps the pack between 20 and 90 percent state of charge will see the best longevity. We supply the charger settings and a simple driver checklist so the pack is never deep-discharged or cooked by a hot summer charge session.

Warranty action: Register the i5 with our export team within 30 days of arrival in Montevideo. That activates the regional service record, qualifies your depot for firmware updates, and makes any future battery claim straightforward instead of a dispute about usage history.

7. Recommended First-Shipment Specification

For a typical Uruguay operator we recommend the i5 box body at 4 t GVW class, left-hand drive, with a depot-mounted 60 kW DC charger included in the shipment, a reinforced rear suspension for crate loading, and a cabin pre-cooling option for summer. Add the spare parts kit below so the first year runs without a single emergency import.

The spare parts starter kit should contain one set of brake pads, two wiper arms, a 12 V auxiliary battery, a tyre repair kit, one set of light bulbs, and a spare DC charge connector. These are the items that cause downtime in the first 12 months, and stocking them locally removes the wait on international freight.

9. Homologation and Left-Hand Drive

Uruguay drives on the right and requires left-hand-drive vehicles, which the i5 is. Homologation follows the national type-approval route; our export team supplies the technical dossier, battery safety documentation and charging compliance papers as part of the order. Because the i5 is a series-production model rather than a conversion, the paperwork is standard and the lead time is predictable.

10. Insurance, Registration and Operator Notes

Uruguayan commercial insurance for an electric goods vehicle follows the standard fleet pattern, and because the i5 has fewer moving parts the mechanical breakdown premium is often comparable to or lower than a diesel equivalent once the battery is covered under the manufacturer warranty. Registration requires the technical dossier we supply, proof of left-hand drive compliance, and the charging safety certificate for the depot unit. We recommend photographing the VIN plate and the battery serial at delivery so any future warranty or insurance claim is documented from day one.

Driver adoption is straightforward because the i5 drives like an automatic, so teams moving off manual diesels adapt within a single shift. The only new habit is the plug-in-on-return rule, and a simple wall chart in the depot removes confusion. Most Uruguayan operators report that drivers prefer the quiet, vibration-free cab within the first week of use.

For fleets running a mixed diesel and electric yard we suggest labelling the i5 charge bay clearly and reserving it for the electric unit so a diesel never blocks the connector. Small yard discipline like this prevents the most common real-world downtime, which is not mechanical but simply a flat battery because the charger was occupied at the wrong moment.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO i5 is the right electric light truck for Uruguayan urban and peri-urban fleets in 2026: its 200 km range covers the daily work, the renewable grid makes every kilometre clean and cheap, and the reduced EV duty treatment improves the landed cost. Confirm the current broker figures, install a depot charger, stock the starter parts kit, and the i5 will deliver a five-year cost profile that beats diesel on high-frequency local routes.
