How to Buy SAGMOTO E6 Trucks for Cambodia: Spec, Import and Cost Guide

2026-10-14Buying Guide10 min read

Cambodia is an RHD medium-duty market with a used-import habit

Cambodia's freight market is built on right-hand-drive vehicles, a legacy of its position in the regional used-truck trade that flows in from Japan, Korea and Thailand. For a new-truck buyer that history is both an opportunity and a trap. The opportunity is that a new, correctly specified RHD truck beats a worn-out Japanese import on total cost once downtime and rebuilds are counted. The trap is that most Cambodian fleets have never bought new and do not know how to specify, import and land a truck on correct terms. This guide closes that gap for the SAGMOTO E6.

The SAGMOTO cargo truck flatbed box stake built on the E6 chassis is the right tool for Cambodian distribution: provincial goods movement between Phnom Penh and the provinces, container shuttle into and out of the Port of Sihanoukville, and construction-material delivery on the road network that Belt and Road infrastructure investment has been upgrading since the mid-2010s. The E6 is powered by a Cummins ISD engine in 180, 210 or 260 hp ratings, producing 680 to 860 Nm of torque, paired with a Fast Gear 6-speed or 9-speed transmission. That is a medium-duty specification tuned for 8 to 18 tonne GVW work, which is exactly the Cambodian provincial cargo envelope.

The buyer's job is to turn that specification into a delivered, legal, serviceable asset in Phnom Penh or Sihanoukville. The steps below do that in order.

Step 1: specify the E6 for Cambodian duty

Cambodian cargo work splits into three patterns, and each wants a different E6 variant. Provincial distribution of consumer goods between Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, Battambang and the border towns runs 200 to 450 km per day on sealed but often potholed roads, with 8 to 12 tonne payloads. Sihanoukville container shuttle is short-haul but stop-start, with 14 to 18 tonne gross on a chassis rated for it. Construction material movement, sand, gravel and brick, is the hardest cycle and wants the 260 hp rating and the 9-speed box. Match the engine to the work; over-specifying adds cost, under-specifying adds downtime.

Engine and transmission choice

The Cummins ISD180 suits 8 to 10 tonne provincial work and returns the best fuel figure. The ISD210 is the general-purpose choice for 10 to 14 tonne mixed duty. The ISD260 is the correct call for container shuttle and construction material where the truck spends its life near maximum GVW. Transmission choice follows: the 6-speed is adequate below 12 tonne, but the 9-speed earns its cost above that because it keeps the engine in its torque band on the grades out of the Mekong basin and on the upgraded national roads. All three engines share service parts with the wider Cummins ISD family already supported in Phnom Penh.

RHD build and cab configuration

The E6 must be built right-hand-drive for Cambodia, and this must be stated in the order because the chassis is otherwise built left-hand-drive for most export markets. Spec the day cab for shuttle work or the sleeper cab for inter-provincial runs where the driver returns the same day but values rest space. Wheelbase should be 4,500 to 5,600 mm for a cargo body of 6.2 to 7.6 metres; longer bodies need the longer wheelbase and a reinforced frame. Tyres are 10.00R20 or 275/80R22.5 depending on the load and the road quality the truck will see.

Key point: State right-hand-drive explicitly in the purchase order. The E6 chassis is built LHD for most markets by default, and a steering-side error is not a field correction — it is a re-build.

Step 2: quotation on the right Incoterms

As with any import, the quotation arrives as FOB or CIF. For Cambodia the discharge port is almost always Sihanoukville, with overland cartage to Phnom Penh adding a further leg. CIF Sihanoukville is the simpler route for a first import; FOB saves cash per unit once you import a consolidated batch and can negotiate sea freight directly out of Shanghai or Shenzhen.

The E6 ships almost always in containers rather than RoRo, because it is a cab-chassis that is often consolidated with bodies or spares, and container rates to Sihanoukville are competitive on the China-Southeast Asia lane. Two E6 cab-chassis units normally fit a 40-foot container when bodies are omitted, which halves the per-unit freight.

Key point: Two E6 cab-chassis units share one 40-foot container to Sihanoukville. Consolidating freight this way cuts per-unit ocean cost roughly in half versus single-unit RoRo and is the standard Cambodian route.

Step 3: payment and contract terms

Cambodian importers settle on TT or LC much as Ecuadorian buyers do, but with a smaller ticket the LC charges bite harder as a percentage. For a single E6 at USD 22,000, a 1.5 percent confirmation charge is USD 330, which is meaningful against the margin. We recommend a 30 percent TT deposit with 70 percent against bill of lading for established repeat buyers, and a 30/70 LC at sight for first-time importers who want document-based protection. Either way, the contract must name the engine rating, transmission, RHD, wheelbase, body length, tyre size and colour, plus a stated lead time and a late-delivery penalty of at least USD 50 per day per unit beyond the agreed date.

Step 4: lead time and pre-shipment inspection

Standard E6 configurations build in 20 to 35 days; RHD builds and custom wheelbases extend this to 30 to 50 days. Pre-shipment inspection matters more for Cambodia than for a mature market because the truck will be assembled or bodied locally on arrival, and a configuration error discovered in Phnom Penh is expensive to fix. Commission inspection at the factory: confirm VIN, engine and gearbox plates, RHD steering, wheelbase, tyre size, and a short engine run to temperature. Budget USD 300 to USD 550 per unit; against a USD 22,000 truck this is negligible insurance and it gives your Cambodian buyer confidence in the supplier.

Step 5: shipping and local cartage to Phnom Penh

Container freight from China to Sihanoukville runs 6 to 12 days on the short sea lane, plus 2 to 4 days of port handling. From Sihanoukville the truck is carted to Phnom Penh by low-loader or driven if it has been assembled and plated, a further 230 km taking 4 to 6 hours. Container shipping protects the truck from weather and is cheaper to consolidate, so it is the default for the E6. If you body the truck in Cambodia, coordinate with a Phnom Penh bodybuilder before the container ships so the chassis arrives to a booked slot rather than sitting in a yard accruing storage.

Step 6: customs, duty and documents in Cambodia

Cambodia applies import duty and a value-added tax on imported commercial vehicles, with rates that vary by engine capacity and GVW. Medium-duty trucks in the E6 class typically face a combined import-duty-plus-tax burden in the region of 15 to 30 percent of CIF value depending on configuration and current schedule, and a further special tax on certain vehicle categories. The used-import habit distorts perception here: a new E6 carries the same headline duty as a new competitor, but it avoids the age-based surcharge that punishes older used units, and it arrives with warranty and known condition.

Your Phnom Penh customs agent needs the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, and the manufacturer's technical data sheet naming the Cummins ISD rating and emissions level. Inconsistent documents are the main cause of hold-ups at Sihanoukville, and storage there runs USD 25 to USD 60 per day per container.

Step 7: parts kit and warranty for the first year

The E6 carries a 12 to 24 month or 80,000 to 120,000 km powertrain warranty by agreement. The Cummins ISD is among the most widely supported engines in Southeast Asia, and filters, belts, thermostat and coolant are available in Phnom Penh; Fast Gear 6 and 9-speed boxes are mechanically simple and locally serviceable. Order a first-year kit with the truck: two filter sets, a belt set, a clutch kit, an alternator, a starter motor, a water pump and a set of wheel bearings and seals. For a 5-unit fleet this runs roughly USD 3,500 to USD 5,500 and prevents a cheap-part wait grounding a working truck.

Build a service relationship in Phnom Penh before delivery. The E6 is diagnosable with standard Cummins tools, and the chassis, brakes and axles are conventional air or hydraulic systems with no proprietary lockout. A fleet that holds its first-year service schedule and its parts kit will keep the E6 on the road through year three with residual value intact.

Conclusion

Buying a SAGMOTO E6 for Cambodia follows the same disciplined sequence as any new-truck import, adapted to an RHD medium-duty market. Specify the Cummins ISD rating to the load, state right-hand-drive in the order, take CIF Sihanoukville for a first small import or FOB with consolidated container freight at volume, settle on a 30/70 LC or inspected TT, allow 20 to 50 days of build, ship by container with local cartage to Phnom Penh, and clear it on the current duty-and-tax schedule with the body built either at the factory or by a booked Phnom Penh bodybuilder. A completed E6 4x2 lands in the region of USD 27,000 to USD 41,000 all-in, and an E6 6x4 in the region of USD 36,000 to USD 52,000.

Against the used-Japanese and used-Korean parc that dominates Cambodia, the E6's case is total cost of ownership across three years, not sticker price: warranty cover, known configuration, no age surcharge, and a local parts kit that keeps a working truck working.
