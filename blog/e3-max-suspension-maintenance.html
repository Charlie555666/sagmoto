E3 MAX Suspension Maintenance: Leaf Springs, Bushings and Ride Height

2026-10-30Maintenance8 min read

The E3 MAX heavy mixed-duty chassis, rated 460 to 520 hp from the Weichai WP12 engine and up to 2250 Nm, is exported by Shaanxi Fenghan Trading Co., Ltd., the Xi'an distributor holding SQIDACN 28 and SAG-2025-201 for SAGMOTO. Operators run it as a heavy tipper hauling 22 to 28 t on mine roads, or as a freight unit pulling 40 to 60 t on paved corridors. These roles load the leaf-spring suspension oppositely, so one schedule cannot fit both. This article covers inspection intervals, U-bolt torque, bushing limits, ride height, cracked-leaf diagnosis, shock testing, lubrication and a parts kit for the E3 MAX. Buyers can compare the platform with the SAGMOTO dump truck models 6x4 8x4 before finalising ratings.

Inspection intervals split by duty cycle

A tipper on a 12 hour shift loads and unloads up to forty times per day, slamming the rear springs against the bump stops with forces a freight tractor never sees. For this duty the spring pack, U-bolts and shackle bushings should be inspected every 5,000 km or weekly, with a torque re-check at the same interval. Any unit that has suffered an overload above the 28 t rear axle limit, a road accident, or a ditch-recovery event must be inspected before service, since fatigue is cumulative.

U-bolt torque procedure and re-torque schedule

The U-bolts clamping the spring pack to the axle seat are the most neglected item on a heavy tipper, and a loose one lets the spring walk sideways under the axle, scarring the seat and eventually snapping a leaf at the centre bolt. The E3 MAX uses class 10.9 U-bolts with a nominal seating torque in the 450 to 600 Nm range depending on thread size and whether a hardened washer is fitted, and the value must be confirmed against the axle builder's plate. Torque in a cross pattern at half, then two thirds, then full value, and never shock the nut with an impact gun above target because that stretches the bolt past yield and hides a false reading. New springs should be re-torqued after the first 500 km because the leaves bed in and clamp load drops as the pack settles.

Re-torque the U-bolts after the first 500 km on a new or rebuilt spring pack. A settled pack can lose 15 to 20 percent of clamp load, enough to start the spring walking under the axle.

A calibrated torque wrench sized for the 600 Nm ceiling is mandatory; a 350 Nm wrench used past its scale will read low and pass a joint that is actually loose. Investigate any bolt that will not hold torque, since repeated loosening signals a stretched bolt, a deformed seat, or a spring that has lost camber.

Spring eye bushing wear limits and replacement

The spring eyes pivot on silent-bush rubber or polyurethane bushings that absorb the small fore-aft movement of the leaf as it flexes, and once the bore elongates the axle shifts under braking, producing a clunk and uneven tyre wear. The acceptance limit for the E3 MAX eye bushing is 3 mm of radial play measured with a dial gauge at the shackle bolt, and any bushing showing cuts, extrusion past the housing lip, or separation from the shell must be changed regardless of the gauge figure. Replacement is a bench job that presses the old bushing out with a mandrel bearing only on the shell and presses the new unit in with the bore aligned and a light film of lubricant so the shell seats square without tearing.

After fitting, the shackle nut is torqued to the suspension fastener schedule, not the U-bolt schedule, and the vehicle is lowered onto its wheels before the final bushing preload is set, because tightening a bushing while the axle hangs distorts it and shortens its life. When both eyes of a spring share a bushing failure the pack has usually run dry or overloaded, and the adjacent leaf should be checked for scuffing at the same time.

Never tighten a spring eye bushing with the axle suspended. Load the chassis onto its wheels first, then set the shackle preload, or the bushing will bind and tear within a few thousand kilometres.

Ride-height measurement procedure

Ride height is the loaded-frame-to-axle dimension that shows whether a spring has lost camber, whether one side has settled more than the other, and whether the chassis sits level for body alignment. Measure from a fixed point on the frame rail to the centre of the axle tube on both sides, with the tyres at the specified cold pressure and the fuel tank half full, because a laden measurement or a low tyre reads falsely low. The E3 MAX nominal rear ride height sits in the 280 to 310 mm band for the standard spring, and the two sides must agree within 10 mm; a difference beyond 15 mm indicates a fatigued or broken leaf on the low side that needs isolation.

Write the cold and loaded figures side by side so the fleet can track spring sag over time; a pack that drops more than 25 mm of ride height between services is losing camber and should be planned for replacement before it breaks. Correcting ride height means renewing the spring, not adding spacer blocks, because blocks shift the load path and crack the seat.

Cracked-leaf diagnosis and spring replacement

The bottom leaf of a tipper spring pack carries the highest bending stress at the centre bolt, and that is where transverse cracks appear first, often on the underside where they are missed during a hasty walk-around. Inspect with the wheel off the ground and the spring relaxed, brushing grit away and running a fingernail or a dye penetrant across the suspect line; a crack that catches the nail or shows under the dye is a replace-now fault. When replacing, fit a complete matched pack rather than a single leaf, because mixing a new leaf with fatigued ones produces an uneven rate and premature failure of the fresh part.

Always replace the centre bolt with the spring, since the old one is stretched and its head may be cracked, and never reuse U-bolts run past yield during the failure event. The new pack should be shimmed so the ride height returns to the nominal band, and the technician should confirm the axle centreline has not shifted before torqueing, because a pack installed a few millimetres off centre wears the tyres and bushings unevenly. Keep a spare rear pack on the shelf for tipper fleets on remote sites, because a broken spring strands a loaded unit and the downtime cost far exceeds the holding cost of one assembly.

Shock absorber test procedure

The E3 MAX runs dual telescopic dampers on each driven axle to control spring oscillation, and a failed damper lets the leaf bounce, which accelerates eye bushing wear. Test by lifting the wheel, gripping the damper body and working the axle through its travel by hand: a healthy unit resists movement in both directions with even force, while a failed one slides freely, weeps oil from the rod seal, or feels notchy from internal scoring. A bounce test with the wheel on the ground also works: push the chassis corner down and release, and it should settle in one motion, not oscillate more than one and a half cycles.

When fitting new dampers, match the extended and compressed lengths to the original so the ride height is not altered, and torque the eye mounts to the suspension fastener value rather than the U-bolt value. A damper mounted with loose eyes will rattle and elongate the bushings within weeks, while one over-tightened with the axle hanging will bind on the first bump.

Grease points and lubrication schedule

The shackle pins, the greasable spring eye bushings, the torque rod ends and the anti-roll bar links all need periodic lubrication, and on a tipper the grease interval should track the U-bolt re-torque rather than the longer engine schedule. Use a high-load lithium or molybdenum grease rated for the operating temperature, and pump each nipple until fresh grease appears at the seal, not just until the gun stops resisting, because a blocked channel leaves the far side dry. Wash the nipples before greasing so grit is not pumped past the seal into the bearing, a common cause of premature pin wear on dusty mine sites.

Grease the shackle and torque rod nipples at every U-bolt re-torque. On tipper duty that means weekly, and a blocked nipple should be cleared, not skipped, or the pin wears and mimics spring failure.

Parts kit list and ordering

A suspension service kit for the E3 MAX should be built around the failure rates above: two complete rear spring packs for every ten tippers, a box of class 10.9 U-bolts and hardened washers, a set of front and rear eye bushings per chassis per year, a pair of dampers per axle in rotation, and a supply of shackle pins and centre bolts. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading supplies these against the VIN-specific parts list so the thread size and length match the axle builder's specification, and ordering by VIN avoids the costly error of a near-identical bolt that is two millimetres short. Keep the torque wrench calibration certificate current, because an out-of-calibration wrench invalidates every U-bolt reading on the inspection card.
