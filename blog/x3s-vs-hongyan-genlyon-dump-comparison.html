SAGMOTO X3s vs Hongyan Genlyon: 8x4 Dump Trucks Compared

2026-11-26Comparison Guide9 min read

An 8x4 dump truck is judged on how it performs a short, punishing cycle: load, haul, tip, repeat. Two machines that meet in that duty are the SAGMOTO X3s and the Hongyan Genlyon, the latter being the real Chinese 8x4 tipper from SAIC Hongyan also known as the Jieshi. The X3s is offered with Cummins ISME power in the 280 to 460 HP range, including the 345 and 420 HP ratings, or with Weichai WP10 and WP12 units, producing 1,800 to 2,100 N m through a Fast 12JSD200T gearbox, with an 8x4 tipper rated at 31 t GVW. This comparison uses indicative ranges for the Genlyon rather than invented precise figures, and focuses on the decisions a fleet actually faces: engine, gearbox, payload, parts support and residual value.

The 8x4 tipper duty cycle

A construction tipper does not spend its life on the highway. It works a short loop between an excavation or quarry and a dump point, often on unsurfaced ground, carrying a full load a large share of the time and tipping many times a day. That cycle is hard on the clutch, the gearbox, the frame and the body mountings. Both the X3s and the Genlyon are designed for this kind of work, and both are built around an 8x4 layout that puts two driven axles under the rear of the body. The comparison should therefore focus on durability and payload rather than on top speed or highway comfort.

Duty also shapes the specification. A truck that tips on a level site needs less torque margin than one that climbs a loaded ramp out of a pit. A fleet should describe the gradient, the surface and the cycle time to the supplier, because the same model can be geared very differently for a quarry and a municipal job. The engine rating alone does not describe how well a tipper will perform; the final drive, the gearbox and the low-speed torque delivery matter just as much.

Power bands compared

The SAGMOTO X3s covers a broad power range from roughly 280 to 460 HP, with the Cummins ISME offered at 345 and 420 HP ratings and Weichai WP10 or WP12 alternatives also available. Torque sits in the 1,800 to 2,100 N m band. The Hongyan Genlyon typically uses engines in the 350 to 420 HP band for its 8x4 tipper variants, with Cursor-derived power units common in that family. Exact ratings differ by market and specification, so a buyer should confirm the certified figure for the destination rather than rely on a general brochure. Both trucks therefore occupy a similar working band rather than one being clearly more powerful.

Because the numbers overlap, the decision rarely comes down to peak horsepower. A Genlyon at the upper end of its band and an X3s at 420 HP are close in output, and the practical difference comes from how each engine delivers torque at low speed and how the gearbox is matched. On a loaded tipper pulling away on a ramp, low-end torque and final drive matter more than the headline maximum. Ask both suppliers for the torque curve and the recommended final drive for your site gradient before comparing figures.

Engine technology and service familiarity

The Cummins ISME in the X3s is a well established heavy-duty engine with a large installed base, which usually means good parts availability and a pool of technicians who know it. The Weichai option gives a fleet an alternative where local support for Weichai is stronger. The Genlyon's Cursor-based engines are also widely used in Chinese trucks, and in markets where Hongyan has a long presence the service knowledge is established. Neither engine is exotic, so the practical question is which one the local workshop actually supports.

Familiarity has a direct cost effect. A tipper that waits two weeks for a turbocharger or an injector is losing revenue every day, and the purchase price difference disappears quickly. Ask the local dealer which engine they stock parts for and how quickly they can supply common wear items such as filters, belts and water pumps. For a fleet adding trucks to an existing operation, matching the engine already in the yard reduces the spare-parts inventory and the training burden. That argument often outweighs a small difference in rated power.

Key point: The X3s and a Hongyan Genlyon 8x4 sit in overlapping 350 to 420 HP territory, so base the choice on local engine support and final drive matching rather than on a headline horsepower difference.

Gearbox and driveline

The X3s uses the Fast 12JSD200T 12-speed gearbox, as a manual or an automated version, matched to the 1,800 to 2,100 N m torque band. That unit is common in heavy Chinese trucks and is generally regarded as robust in construction service. The Genlyon is also offered with heavy manual or automated gearboxes in similar speed counts, and the exact model depends on the engine and the market. A buyer should request the gearbox model on each quote, because the same truck name can cover more than one transmission.

Driveline durability is the real concern on a tipper. Repeated loaded launches send torque spikes through the clutch, propshafts and differentials, and a gearbox that is rated adequately on paper can still suffer if the driver launches aggressively. Specify a clutch and driveline sized for the loaded gradient, and include driver training in the delivery plan. Ask whether an automated option is available if the operation suffers from inconsistent gear selection, since an automated unit can reduce shock loads and clutch wear across a shift.

Gross weight, payload and body

The X3s 8x4 tipper is rated at 31 t GVW, and its frame is quoted with a section in the region of 300 by 80 mm with a composite web arrangement, giving the stiffness needed for a heavy body. The Genlyon 8x4 is a real competitor in a similar gross weight class, though the exact rating varies by specification and market, so a buyer should confirm it directly rather than assume a figure. Once the chassis rating is known, the payload follows from the weighed body tare, which should be measured on a weighbridge rather than taken from a design estimate.

Body construction deserves as much attention as the chassis. A tipper body that is too heavy eats into payload, while one that is too light may not survive the material being carried. For abrasive rock, a harder floor and reinforced headboard are worth the extra tare; for soil or sand, a lighter body preserves capacity. The subframe and the tipping gear must be matched to the chassis, with shear plates near the bogie to carry braking loads. Ask for the body drawing and the weighed tare before comparing the two trucks on payload.

Frame and tipping stresses

Tipping imposes a load case that highway trucks never see. When the body lifts, its weight pivots about the rear hinge and the front of the body rises, transferring load to the rear of the frame and the bogie. A heavy load that shifts during tipping adds a dynamic component, and the subframe must carry both the vertical weight and the moment. On the X3s the ladder frame and reinforcing are specified for this duty, but any body that concentrates mass away from the crossmembers can still cause a fatigue crack over time.

Parts support and service network

Service support often decides the winner in a tipper fleet. A truck that is off the road for a minor part is losing capacity, and remote quarry sites make the problem worse. The X3s benefits from the commonality of Cummins or Weichai engines and the Fast gearbox, which are supported in many markets, but the exact engine and transmission variant must be confirmed with the nearest dealer before an order. Ask for a recommended spare-parts kit covering filters, brake parts, clutch components and pump seals.

The Genlyon has a genuine advantage in regions where SAIC Hongyan has operated for years and where a local dealer network holds stock. In other markets that support may be thin, which changes the calculation entirely. A buyer should compare the two on the actual local presence rather than on reputation elsewhere. Resale and total cost of ownership

Resale value follows the buyer pool and the service history. An 8x4 tipper with a documented record and a well known engine is easier to sell on, and both the X3s and the Genlyon should find buyers where construction activity is strong. The condition of the body and the frame matters more than the engine rating at resale, because a buyer can see the wear. Keep the service records, the weighbridge tickets and any frame repair documentation with the vehicle.

Total cost combines purchase price, fuel, tyres, service and downtime over the holding period. On a short construction cycle, tyres and brake wear often rival fuel in the budget, and downtime can exceed both. Build a five-year model with your own figures and test how sensitive it is to a two-week parts delay. That sensitivity frequently decides the case between two trucks whose headline specifications are close, and it is the reason a buyer should look beyond the engine band before committing.

Key point: In an 8x4 tipper the condition of the body and frame drives resale more than engine power, so keep weighbridge tickets and repair records with the X3s vehicle file.

Verification before placing an order

Before committing, ask both suppliers to state the certified engine power and torque, the gearbox model, the frame section, the chassis GVW and the weighed body tare for the exact build being offered. Confirm what spare-parts kit is recommended and how quickly common items can be supplied to your site. Require the duty cycle to be stated in writing, because a warranty built around highway use may not cover a quarry operation. These checks take a few days and prevent most of the disputes that appear at delivery.

Neither truck is the universal answer. The X3s is compelling where engine choice, parts commonality and a documented 31 t tipper rating fit the operation, while the Genlyon is strong where a local Hongyan network already supports a fleet. Match the truck to the cycle and the local service reality, and the total cost calculation will follow from the work rather than from a specification sheet.
