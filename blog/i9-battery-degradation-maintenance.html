SAGMOTO i9 Battery Degradation and Maintenance: Extending Electric Truck Life

October 12, 2026Maintenance & After-sales14 min read

The SAGMOTO i9 is the brand's workhorse electric truck, built on a lithium-iron-phosphate (LFP) battery pack sized between 282 kWh and 420 kWh depending on the variant. LFP chemistry is chosen deliberately for commercial duty because it tolerates high charge rates and a deep cycle life far beyond the NMC cells used in passenger cars. This maintenance guide explains how i9 battery degradation actually progresses and how fleet operators can slow it.

How i9 Battery Degradation Works

All lithium batteries lose capacity over cycles, but LFP degrades gently and predictably. The i9 pack is engineered to retain 80 percent state-of-health (SoH) at around 3,000 full-equivalent cycles — roughly 600,000–900,000 km in typical distribution duty. Degradation is driven by three factors: depth of discharge, operating temperature, and sustained high charge rate. None of these is fatal if managed.

Thermal Management and Cooling

The i9 pack uses liquid thermal management with a plate-cooled architecture and a cabin-linked heat pump. In hot climates (Gulf, Southeast Asia, parts of Latin America), keep the cooling system serviced on a 12-month interval: check coolant concentration, clean the front battery radiator, and verify the compressor setpoint. A blocked battery radiator is the leading cause of accelerated summer degradation.

Charging Discipline

The single biggest lever is charging behaviour. Overnight AC charging at 0.3–0.5C is gentlest on the cells; reserve DC fast charging (up to 1.5C) for mid-shift top-ups. Avoid parking a fully charged pack in direct 50°C sun for extended periods, and avoid discharging below 10 percent routinely. Fenghan Trade recommends a fleet charging policy that caps daily DC fast sessions at two and favours depot AC overnight.

Maintenance insight: Battery replacement is rarely needed inside the 5-year warranty on a well-managed i9. The cost of a pack swap only becomes a fleet concern past 600,000 km — and by then the vehicle has typically paid for itself two or three times over versus diesel on fuel and service alone.

Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Fenghan Trade's i9 electric maintenance plan is lighter than a diesel equivalent: no oil, no injectors, no DPF. The key electric items are:

  • Monthly: visual inspection of high-voltage connectors and cooling lines; tyre rotation.
  • 6-month: battery SoH scan via SAGMOTO Connect telematics; brake inspection (regen does most of the work).
  • 12-month: coolant service; HVAC and heat-pump check; insulation-resistance test on the HV system.
  • 24-month: HV cable retorque; inverter coolant flush.

Telematics and SoH Monitoring

Every i9 streams cell-level voltage, temperature and SoH to the SAGMOTO Connect platform. Fleet managers see per-truck degradation curves and receive alerts when a module drifts. This turns battery health from a mystery into a managed metric and lets Fenghan Trade pre-position replacement modules before a failure occurs. Conclusion

The SAGMOTO i9's LFP battery is built for commercial cycle life, and degradation is manageable with thermal care, charging discipline and a light preventive schedule. Operators who follow the depot-AC charging policy and keep the cooling system serviced will see 80 percent SoH well past 600,000 km.
